Good afternoon from Willowfield Ground in Eastmoor, where Granite XI won the toss, chose to field, and almost immediately dragged this match into the sort of tense, slightly uneven rhythm that makes a modest target feel much more complex than the raw score later suggests, because Eastmere XI did not begin without intent, Rafe Milton flashing hard through the off side and giving the first couple of overs a little sting, but the innings kept being interrupted before any early positivity could mature into control. Jace Ward removed Rafe with Dylan Reed taking the catch, Nolan West then trapped Owen Blake in front, and although Calvin Drake and Damon Frost tried to restore some shape through the first proper stand of the innings, Granite never allowed that repair to become comfortable. Calvin’s twenty-one had enough clean strokes to suggest he might take the innings somewhere sturdier, but Evan Cross changed the game’s tone by removing him and then immediately taking out Eli Dean as well, turning Eastmere from a side recovering at sixty-seven for three into one suddenly stuck at the same score with four down and the middle order already exposed. That was the first decisive fracture. Damon Frost stayed longer than most and played the kind of innings that has more structural value than surface sparkle, twenty-eight from thirty-four, not fluent, not fast, but important because somebody had to keep one end from simply disappearing. Miles Hart helped briefly, only for Milo Brooks to break that passage, and then Adrian Cole became the one Eastmere batter who truly altered the back half. His unbeaten thirty-eight from twenty-four was the rescue hand the innings badly needed, struck with enough timing and enough late force to move the score beyond the awkwardly low and into the merely defendable. The stand with Damon added twenty-seven, mostly through Adrian’s cleaner scoring, but Evan Cross again provided the key interruption by having Damon stumped, and from there Eastmere’s finish became a series of small pushes rather than one genuine surge. Isaac Wells and Grant Bishop could not stay long. Jordan Pike was run out late. Kevin Doyle only helped add the final scraps. Eastmere closed on one hundred and thirty-six for nine, a total that had Adrian’s late punch, Damon’s occupation, Calvin’s earlier order, and Rafe’s initial spark, but never the one unbroken stretch that would have made it feel properly heavy. Granite’s bowling had the right spread of damage: Jace made the first cut, Nolan clipped the other opener, Felix Stone and Hugo Clarke kept the middle from breathing, Milo Brooks bought a useful wicket despite leaking at the other end, and Evan Cross was the hinge of the whole innings, three wickets for fifteen, all of them arriving at moments when Eastmere were trying to re-form. Chasing one hundred and thirty-seven, Granite should have been comfortable, but knockout cricket rarely lets a chase stay clean for long, and Kevin Doyle made sure of that by removing Blake Turner first ball, Adrian Cole holding the catch and suddenly making the target feel sharper than it had looked at the interval. Carter Miles responded with a burst that briefly removed the nerves, twelve from six with a boundary and a six, but Jordan Pike bowled him before that counterpunch could become a proper powerplay. That left Evan Cross with the harder job: not only scoring the runs, but absorbing the instability around him. Dylan Reed gave him the first useful platform, adding fourteen and enough time to let the chase slow down without stalling, though Elliot Dean’s caught-and-bowled dismissal kept Eastmere alive. Gavin Price then played a useful supporting hand, seventeen from eighteen, and again the chase seemed to be settling, only for Elliot Dean to strike once more, catching Gavin through Calvin Drake and later bowling Hudson Shore after another small stand had taken Granite just past the hundred mark. That was the point where the match still had a trace of danger. Granite were five down, the target was close but not gone, and Eastmere had one bowler in Elliot Dean who had kept the middle phase genuinely uncomfortable. Evan Cross simply refused to let that discomfort become panic. His unbeaten sixty-three from fifty-four was the most complete innings in the match because it moved through every stage: first-ball damage, early counterattack, middle-overs caution, another wobble, and finally the closing push. He did not bat like someone trying to win it in one over. He kept trimming the target, took the boundary when it appeared, trusted the gaps when the field spread, and once Milo Brooks joined him, the finish finally became clear. Milo’s unbeaten sixteen from seven was the perfect last hand, direct, clean, and aggressive enough to stop Eastmere from crowding every ball around the set batter. Their unbroken stand of thirty-seven turned one hundred and one for five into one hundred and thirty-eight for five, Granite crossing the line in the twentieth over and qualifying by five wickets. Eastmere had fought properly. Doyle’s first-ball wicket gave them an ideal start, Jordan Pike removed the dangerous early counter, and Elliot Dean’s three wickets made the chase far less routine than the target suggested. But they were always defending a total that needed clustered panic, and Evan Cross gave Granite the opposite: calm, continuity, and the one innings long enough to settle the match. That is why the player-of-the-match decision is straightforward. Evan had already broken Eastmere’s innings with the ball, then he carried the chase without being dismissed. In a game where both sides had fragments of control, he was the only player who owned both halves of the contest.
