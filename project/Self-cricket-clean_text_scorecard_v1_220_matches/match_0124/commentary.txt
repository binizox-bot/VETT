Stonecrest won the toss at Red Mesa Ground in Eastmere and chose to bat, and for the first few overs Meadowgate could still argue that they were not out of the game, because the opening phase had movement without yet becoming a full-scale powerplay collapse for the fielding side. Kian Wells found early timing, driving and cutting cleanly enough to give Stonecrest a brisk first push, while Bram Shaw played the more measured hand from the other end, settling into the pitch, watching the bounce, and making sure the innings did not become too dependent on one batter’s first burst. Tyler Brooks gave Meadowgate the first useful correction by removing Kian, and at twenty-four for one the match was still balanced enough for the bowling side to believe they could drag the innings into a more controlled pattern. Bram and captain Damon Ward prevented that from happening immediately. Damon’s twenty-one was not a dominant innings, but it was practical and important: he kept the strike turning over, punished the ball that missed straight, and allowed Bram to continue the longer work of building the innings. Their stand moved Stonecrest to seventy, and even though Mason Hart eventually removed Damon, the batting side had already avoided the early stutter that Meadowgate wanted. Finn Cross then changed the speed of the innings. He did not stay long enough to own the scorecard, but his thirty-four from nineteen balls hit the middle overs with exactly the right force, two fours, three sixes, and enough intent to stop Meadowgate from squeezing after the second wicket. Bram remained the thread through that phase, reaching forty-seven and keeping the innings coherent, but once Finn fell and Bram followed soon after, Stonecrest were still in a position that could have gone either way. They were strong, certainly, but not yet out of reach. That changed when Rohan Vale took over the death overs. At first his role was only to keep the innings alive through a small wobble, because Liam Parker and Hayden Marsh both came and went after short stays. Then the final stretch became something completely different. Rohan began picking the length early, especially anything in the slot or fractionally short, and once Nolan Pierce joined him for the last burst, the innings exploded. The final partnership was worth fifty-four, most of it from Rohan’s bat, and it turned a good total into a genuinely severe one. Nolan’s single-ball six mattered because it kept the field scattered, but Rohan was the real damage, finishing unbeaten on sixty-seven from twenty-three with seven sixes and four fours. It was not a long innings in terms of time. It was a finishing assault, the kind that makes the fielding side feel that all its earlier control has been partly erased. Meadowgate’s card had good bowling hidden inside the damage: Mason Hart took three wickets and bowled the most valuable spell, Tyler had the early wicket, and Gavin Reed removed two dangerous middle-order players. Yet Caleb Turner’s final over was wrecked, and several supporting spells could not hold the rate down once Stonecrest’s hitters began lining up the close. Stonecrest finished on two hundred and sixteen for six, and the shape of the innings was layered: Kian’s early push, Bram’s platform, Damon’s support, Finn’s middle burst, then Rohan’s violent finish. Chasing two hundred and seventeen, Meadowgate needed one huge partnership and probably another useful one beside it. They got energy, but not enough continuity. Owen Nash fell early to Kyle Mercer, yet Caleb Turner and Ethan Doyle kept the response alive with the best stand of the innings, Ethan striking crisply through the off side and Caleb adding enough pace to stop the target from feeling completely distant. Rohan Vale broke that partnership by removing Ethan, and Hayden Marsh then bowled Caleb, which left Meadowgate having to restart with the rate already pressing. Ryan Mercer gave them their most serious answer. His sixty-one from thirty-three balls was a proper captain’s counterattack, full of boundaries, clean hitting, and the sort of intent that kept Stonecrest from relaxing even with a large total behind them. The issue was that every time Ryan found a partner, Stonecrest found another interruption. Jacob Flynn was bowled by Hayden. Logan Price was removed by Kyle. Tyler Brooks stayed long enough to help Ryan add another useful little piece, but when Oliver Kent had Ryan caught at one hundred and thirty, the chase lost the only batter who looked capable of bending the equation for more than a couple of overs. Tyler followed soon after, stumped off Finn Cross, and Meadowgate were seven down with too much still to do. Connor Shaw briefly made the game noisy again, hitting four boundaries in a fierce eighteen from eight balls, and Mason Hart’s unbeaten twenty-five gave the lower order some spine, but those were late sparks rather than a rebuilt chase. Rohan removed Connor at a crucial moment, adding a second wicket to the innings he had already shaped with the bat, and Kian Wells’ run-out of Dylan West near the end kept the final overs from becoming messy. Meadowgate reached one hundred and eighty-five for nine, a strong enough reply to show they had fought, but thirty-one short of the target. The difference between the sides was not simply that Stonecrest hit harder. It was that Stonecrest connected their phases better. Bram and Damon built after the first wicket, Finn lifted the middle, Rohan detonated the end, and then the bowlers kept cutting Meadowgate’s chase into fragments. Meadowgate had Ryan Mercer’s serious counterattack, Ethan Doyle’s early support, Connor Shaw’s late flash, and Mason Hart’s resistance, yet no partnership after the second-wicket stand lasted long enough to make the required rate feel controlled. Rohan Vale as Player of the Match fits the structure cleanly. His unbeaten sixty-seven transformed Stonecrest’s first innings, and his two wickets in the chase removed batters at moments when Meadowgate were trying to gather momentum. Stonecrest won because the biggest passage of the match belonged to them, and because every time Meadowgate threatened to make the chase coherent, another wicket arrived.
