Noah Pierce gave him the first serious support, not spectacularly, more through patience, strike movement and enough boundary pressure to stop the bowlers from crowding Bennett alone, and their stand moved Ridgefield out of immediate trouble into a platform. When Daniel Frost eventually removed Pierce, Brookmere had a route back, yet Caleb Stone arrived and played the next necessary hand, busier than Pierce, strong enough to keep the field honest, and willing to let Bennett take ownership of the innings without becoming invisible himself. That fourth-wicket partnership was where Ridgefield crossed from recovery into control, because Bennett had now moved beyond survival and into full command, hitting through the off side when width arrived, pulling anything short enough to sit up, and using the straight boundary just often enough to make Brookmere stop bowling the fuller attacking length they had trusted early. Caleb was trapped by Victor Hayes with the score in the low one-seventies, and even there the innings was still not safe from flattening, but Ryan Cole then gave Bennett the late-innings partner he needed. Lucas Bennett’s player-of-the-match award is therefore clear. The result, Ridgefield by 232 runs, matches the actual flow of the day: early danger absorbed, one dominant innings built, late acceleration added, and then a chase compressed into fragments until the margin became as heavy as the cricket had felt.
