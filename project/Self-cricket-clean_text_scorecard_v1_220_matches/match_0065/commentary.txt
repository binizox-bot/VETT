Good afternoon from Meadow Park Oval in Westhaven, where Northbridge chose to bat first and ended up defending a total that looked useful rather than commanding at halfway, mainly because they kept finding small repairs whenever the innings threatened to break, and then because Riley Stone later turned every Eastmoor recovery into another interruption before the chase could become a proper pursuit. The first innings began badly enough for Northbridge to know immediately that this would not be a clean batting day. Leon Cross never found any rhythm, Nolan Turner followed almost as quickly, and with both early wickets gone the innings had the feel of a side being asked to rebuild before it had even settled into the surface. Mason Vale gave the top order a little time, nothing flashy, just enough occupation to stop the first few overs from becoming a complete collapse, but the first real stabilizing hand came from Owen Clarke. Owen batted with the sort of patient control that low-scoring games often need, leaving well when Harvey Stone and Ethan Price kept things tight, then punishing the rare width with a cleaner stroke than most of his partners managed. Mason’s dismissal left the innings three down before it had truly opened up, and Parker Dean briefly gave the score a pulse with two sharp boundaries, though Isaac Rowe cut that off quickly enough to keep Eastmoor in front of the rhythm. The important phase came when Owen Clarke and Quentin Brooks began to put shape back into the innings. Their partnership was not explosive, and it did not need to be. It gave Northbridge a middle, gave the dressing room a little calm, and forced Eastmoor to bowl through a passage where one more wicket would have exposed the lower order much too soon. Owen’s thirty-eight was the steadier piece, Quentin’s thirty-one carried more of the later repair, and together they dragged the innings out of the early mess into something defendable. Julian Frost eventually caught and bowled Owen, which felt like a major opening for Eastmoor because the score was still not high enough for Northbridge to relax, and when Quentin fell not long after, the innings again seemed ready to stall. That is where Riley Stone’s first contribution began to matter. His thirty-four was not a decorative lower-order score. It was the hand that made the difference between a thin total and one that could ask real questions. He first stayed with Quentin, then with Sawyer Holt, then kept finding ways to add while partners came and went. The lower order around him was untidy: Sawyer made only a handful, Theo Walsh barely got started, Victor Lane scratched out a few, and Wyatt Cole was left at the end. But Riley kept the innings breathing. He took the singles that were available, found the boundary when Eastmoor relaxed length, and made sure Northbridge did not waste the work Owen and Quentin had done earlier. Aiden Brooks eventually removed him at 166, and then closed the innings by bowling Victor Lane, leaving Northbridge all out for 172 in the forty-third over. It was a total made from fragments rather than dominance: Owen Clarke holding the central repair, Quentin Brooks bridging the middle, Riley Stone lifting the back end, and twenty extras quietly helping the innings through several awkward pauses. Eastmoor’s bowlers had enough good work to feel they had done the hard part. Ethan Price had the early double strike, Isaac Rowe removed Mason and Parker before either could fully settle, Julian Frost bowled a long controlling spell and took two useful wickets, and Aiden Brooks finished strongly with three. Still, Northbridge had extracted just enough from each phase to make the chase uncomfortable rather than simple. Chasing 173, Eastmoor were hit almost immediately when Brandon Shaw was trapped in front by Quentin Brooks before the score had moved, and that first wicket changed the mood because the target was modest enough to chase calmly but not small enough to waste early stability. Adrian Wells and Aiden Brooks then gave Eastmoor their best top-order passage. Aiden was the more positive player, striking cleanly and using one big hit to stop Northbridge from settling too deeply into defence, while Adrian took the slower route, absorbing balls and trying to build the kind of base that would make the middle overs easier. Their stand carried Eastmoor into a position where the match was very much alive, and for a few overs Northbridge had to work rather than simply wait. Riley Stone changed that. He removed Aiden first, then bowled Dominic Ward almost immediately, and suddenly the chase had gone from steady to nervous in the space of a few deliveries. Connor Blake gave Adrian a little support, but Owen Clarke removed him, and when Riley returned to take Adrian as well, Eastmoor had lost both the tempo player and the anchor. From that point the chase was forced into a series of repairs, each one smaller and more pressured than the last. Julian Frost tried to stabilize with Isaac Rowe, Kyle Mercer was run out before he could help, and Parker Dean’s wicket of Julian left Eastmoor seven down with too much still to do. The one passage that made Northbridge worry again came through Isaac Rowe and Finley Grant. Isaac played the captain’s hand properly, measured and firm, not reckless, using the gaps and keeping the innings alive. Finley gave him enough support to turn a fading chase into a live one again, and their stand of forty-two briefly changed the sound of the match. Eastmoor were still behind, but they had a route: Isaac to bat deep, Finley to stay with him, and the tail to find only a little more than usual. Riley Stone shut that route down. His return was the decisive moment of the afternoon. He removed Isaac with Theo Walsh taking the catch, then picked up Finley, and finally bowled Ethan Price to close the innings at 141. The figures were excellent, but the timing was even better. Riley did not take six wickets in one meaningless late rush. He broke the chase at the top when Aiden and Dominic fell, broke the anchor when Adrian went, then broke the last real recovery when Isaac and Finley were threatening to make the final overs matter. That is why the player-of-the-match award fits cleanly. His batting had already added the lower-order weight that gave Northbridge something to defend, and his bowling then cut through every Eastmoor structure that looked capable of reaching it. Eastmoor can point to their own useful spells and say they had Northbridge under pressure for long periods, and that is true. They can also point to Aiden Brooks’ top-order burst, Isaac Rowe’s late resistance, and Finley Grant’s support as evidence that the chase never completely died until the last few wickets. But the larger pattern was clear: Northbridge kept repairing their innings just before it became too light, while Eastmoor kept losing the batter who was supposed to turn repair into control. That difference decided the match. Northbridge’s 172 was never huge, yet it was layered enough to defend, and once Riley Stone began hitting the chase in separate waves, Eastmoor ran out of partnerships before they ran out of belief.
