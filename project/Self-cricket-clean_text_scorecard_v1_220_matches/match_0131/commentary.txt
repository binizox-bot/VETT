At Eastbank Cricket Ground in Larkmoor, Ridgehaven XI chose to field first and, for a while, the decision looked like it might keep Meridian XI in a state of constant negotiation rather than clean control, because the first innings never moved in one smooth line: it began brightly, stalled, rebuilt through one serious middle partnership, then almost cracked apart at the end before enough late scraps carried the total into awkward territory. Owen Kirk gave Meridian the first burst of force, and his short stay was exactly the kind of innings that can make a fielding side feel as though its early plans have been hurried before they are even fully formed, because he struck cleanly, got into the boundary options early, and made Arun Vale’s steadier presence at the other end more useful than it might have looked in isolation. The opening stand reached thirty-nine before Mark Dale removed Owen, and that was Ridgehaven’s first important correction because Owen was the batter giving the innings its sharpest early edge. Jaden Hall then tightened the game by bowling Arun and later removing Pavel Stone, so Meridian’s promising start had been pulled back to sixty-seven for three before the halfway mark, and at that stage Ridgehaven had a real opportunity to turn a brisk opening into something only moderate. That is where Quinn Mercer and Rian Brooks changed the shape of the innings. Quinn did not play a flashy captain’s hand, and that was precisely why it mattered. He stayed, absorbed the harder overs, worked the ball into gaps, and gave the innings a spine after the early wickets had removed the sense of freedom. Rian Brooks supplied more visible punch beside him, hitting with better tempo and forcing the field to keep shifting even while Quinn held the longer rhythm. Their stand of seventy-two was the structural center of the match, not simply because it was the largest partnership on the card, but because it took Meridian from a vulnerable position into one where a defendable total became realistic. Ridgehaven were not poor in that phase; Jaden Hall remained controlled, Jason Vale kept things from running completely loose, and the field still had chances to apply pressure. The problem was that Quinn and Rian kept finding just enough: a gap after a quiet spell, a boundary when the field crept in, a free run when the line drifted, and gradually the innings changed from repair into platform. Then came the late mess that kept the match alive. Quinn was run out at one hundred and thirty-nine, Rian followed almost immediately, Soren Gray’s short hit was stopped, Toby Lane was bowled, Milo Price was run out, and suddenly Meridian’s finish looked far less clean than the middle had promised. Elias Ward and Nate Cross nudged the last few deliveries just far enough to carry the total to one hundred and sixty-one for eight, which felt neither safe nor soft. It was a target built from one good opening punch, one strong central stand, and one slightly chaotic close. Ridgehaven’s bowling reflected that same split. Mark Dale took two wickets but paid heavily. Jaden Hall was the most economical and removed two of the top three. Jason Vale and Mason Reed both struck late, while the extra balls and wides prevented the innings from being fully strangled. Chasing one hundred and sixty-two, Ridgehaven needed a calm first six overs, and instead the chase was damaged almost immediately. Ronan Scott was gone first ball, caught behind off Soren Gray, and the tone changed at once from pursuit to repair. Brian Hale and Henry Ellis briefly suggested a counter, but Elias Ward then took over the match in the exact passage that later justified the award. Brian was bowled at fifteen, Henry followed at eighteen, and Gary Mason was pinned at twenty-one; three early wickets for Elias and four down inside the powerplay stretch left Ridgehaven trying to chase from a hole rather than from a plan. That early damage did not end the match, because Edwin Ross played the innings that nearly dragged Ridgehaven back into it. He first rebuilt with Caleb Orion, who gave him the necessary calm support in a stand of fifty-five, and that partnership was the one moment where Ridgehaven began to look like a side with a route rather than merely a side delaying defeat. Caleb’s twenty-two was slow but useful, the kind of innings that lets the more fluent batter settle into the chase, while Edwin gradually became the player Meridian had to remove. When Nate Cross finally bowled Caleb, Ridgehaven were still behind the rate but no longer broken. Leo North then gave them a burst of urgency with a sharp fifteen, and for a moment the match began to tighten again because Edwin was still there and the required rate had not yet reached fantasy. Nate’s wicket of Leo at one hundred and one was therefore a major intervention, because it stopped that brief acceleration before it became a full surge. Edwin then found another partner in Jaden Hall, and this was the passage that gave Ridgehaven their last real hope. Edwin was hitting cleanly now, lifting the ball with authority, and Jaden’s unbeaten hand kept the scoreboard from freezing around him. Their partnership pushed the chase to one hundred and thirty-two, close enough that Meridian had to hold its nerve rather than simply defend the scoreboard. Toby Lane produced the decisive answer. Edwin was caught by Arun Vale, and that wicket removed the only batter who had managed to combine survival and threat. Two balls later Jason Vale was stumped, and the chase, which had just looked alive, lost its center. Mark Dale and Jaden Hall scraped a few more, but Mark’s run-out in the final over left too much for too few balls, and Mason Reed’s last-ball strike was only enough to narrow the margin, not change the result. Ridgehaven finished on one hundred and forty-five for nine, sixteen short. The result made sense because Meridian won the decisive moments rather than every minute of the game. They built one large partnership after early pressure, survived a late batting collapse with just enough on the board, then used Elias Ward’s new-ball and early-middle damage to force Ridgehaven into a chase of constant repair. Edwin Ross gave Ridgehaven a serious fighting hand, and Jaden Hall’s late resistance kept the score respectable, but once Edwin fell, the innings no longer had a batter capable of carrying the target home. Elias Ward’s player-of-the-match award fits the internal logic of the scorecard: his batting was minor, but his bowling ripped away Brian Hale, Henry Ellis and Gary Mason in the phase where Ridgehaven most needed stability, and those wickets shaped the chase long before the final margin appeared.
