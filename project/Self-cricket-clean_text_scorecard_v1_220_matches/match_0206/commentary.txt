Good morning from Brookfield Ground Two in Westhaven, where this third-place qualifier began with Mesa making the obvious aggressive call, choosing to field first and immediately making Coastline look as if they had walked into the match half a step late, because the first act of the innings was all pressure, all loose edges, all batters trying to find time that the bowlers would not give them. Ari Wells gave Coastline a few early runs but not a base, and when Blake Stone had him caught by Cam Wells, the first crack was already open. Cole Hudson then deepened it. Owen Vale went after a short, busy start, Ethan Rowe followed not long after, and Caleb Stone’s duck left Coastline in the kind of playoff trouble that changes the whole shape of a batting innings. At 38 for 4, the question was no longer whether Coastline could dominate the first innings. The question was whether they could keep the match alive at all. Nolan Vale answered that question with the innings that decided the day. He did not counterattack blindly, and that is exactly why the hand became so valuable. He absorbed the early damage, slowed the emotional speed of the game, and then found the right partner in Lucas Finn, who gave the innings the long, disciplined support it desperately needed. Their fifth-wicket stand of 118 was not one of those pretty middle-overs partnerships that begins from comfort. It was a rescue act built under pressure, a long piece of repair work that slowly changed Mesa’s field from attacking to containing. Nolan carried the larger strategic burden, judging which overs could be milked and which ones had to be answered more firmly, while Lucas played a patient fifty that mattered far more than its surface tempo. He did not try to compete with the captain for control. He stayed, rotated, protected the innings from another collapse, and made Mesa bowl through a period where the early wickets stopped feeling like the whole story. Aidan Gray eventually broke that partnership by removing Lucas at 156, and that did bring Mesa back into the innings, but the damage had already been repaired. Coastline were no longer a side trying to survive 38 for 4. They were a side with a defendable score forming underneath them. Dylan Ross gave Nolan another useful passage, making 15 and taking the total into safer territory before Leo Pierce pinned him, and when Nolan himself finally fell for 81, caught by Aidan Gray off Leo, the innings had been transformed. He walked off with Coastline at 196 for 7, and that number matters because without his long stay the innings probably never gets anywhere near that region. The closing overs were messy, with Connor Miles and Mason Clarke both run out, but Adrian Hale made sure the tail still produced value. His unbeaten 28 was sharp, especially at the very end, where he took most of the final stand and pushed Coastline to 222 all out in 48.4 overs. Mesa’s bowlers had not failed. Blake Stone took three wickets, Cole Hudson’s early spell created the collapse, Leo Pierce broke the middle and late sections, and Aidan Gray took the key wicket of Lucas Finn. The issue was that Mesa could not finish the innings when it had Coastline down early. They won the first eight overs, then lost the next thirty because Nolan and Lucas refused to let the card keep bleeding. Chasing 223, Mesa needed one of two things: either a strong opening platform or a long middle-overs anchor to match what Nolan had done earlier. They got neither cleanly enough. Ruben Hale began positively and Noel Bennett helped the score to 15, but Connor Miles struck early, and Dylan Ross followed by removing Evan Holt before Mesa had even settled. Ruben’s own dismissal soon after made it 22 for 3, and from there the chase was already bent out of shape. Aidan Gray and Leo Pierce tried to slow things down, then Leo and Isaac Vale added a little more resistance, but every stand looked too small against the size of the task. Adrian Hale’s run-out of Isaac was a particularly damaging moment because Mesa were trying to turn occupation into something more solid, and instead another repair was cut short. Nathan Reed then began to take over the middle of the chase. He removed Leo, then Toby Grant, and later Cam Wells, and those wickets mattered because each of those batters had been involved in one of Mesa’s attempted rebuilding phases. Toby and Julian Cross added 23, but it did not last. Julian and Cam Wells then gave Mesa their best and most fluent passage, adding 36 for the eighth wicket, with Cam striking the ball more cleanly than anyone else in the chase. For a few overs, Coastline had to stay serious, because Cam’s 30 and Julian’s 23 gave Mesa a faint route toward making the finish awkward. Nathan Reed ended that possibility by removing Cam, and Ethan Rowe then cut off Julian. The final pair of Blake Stone and Cole Hudson delayed the end rather than changing it, Blake making 18 and Cole blocking at the other end, but Dylan Ross returned to close the innings with Mesa on 150. Coastline’s defence was not built on one spectacular burst. It was built on pressure arriving in layers. Connor Miles took the top away, Dylan Ross struck at the start and the finish, Mason Clarke bowled ten overs of restriction without the scoreboard rewarding him with a wicket, Adrian Hale added both a wicket and a run-out influence, Nathan Reed broke the middle resistance, and Ethan Rowe removed the last batter who could still give the chase a little shape. That is why the result feels earned. Coastline were under real pressure early, but they had the one partnership that defined the match. Mesa had moments, and they had bowlers who made the first innings uncomfortable, but they never produced a batting stand with the same weight as Nolan Vale and Lucas Finn’s recovery. In a playoff where match sat behind the result, that difference was decisive. Coastline won by 72 runs, and Nolan Vale was the correct Player of the Match because his 81 did more than top-score. It turned a collapsing innings into a total that could be defended, changed the emotional balance of the match, and gave his bowlers something substantial enough to protect.
