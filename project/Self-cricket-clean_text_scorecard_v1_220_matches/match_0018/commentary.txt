At North Ridge Stadium in Hillview, Harborline XI won the toss and chose to field, and for the first stretch that decision had real bite, because the new ball did enough to make Dunecrest XI start their innings in fragments rather than rhythm; Caleb Turner got the first reward when Aaron Hale pushed at one that never quite let him free his hands, Noah Grant then went through Isaac Rowe before the innings had settled, and although Kieran Shaw was playing with the cleanest early intent, driving whenever Harborline missed full and picking up anything drifting into his pads, Dunecrest still had the uneasy shape of a side trying to recover while one man kept the board alive. Daniel Mercer gave Shaw the first serious support, not fluent, not flashy, just careful enough to stop the innings from becoming a collapse, and together they rebuilt from the low twenties into the eighties before Ryan Cross found the ball that got through Shaw and reopened the whole question of the innings. Evan Hart went quickly after that, and at 85 for four Harborline had every right to believe they had read the morning correctly. Mason Vale changed the game from there. He did not begin as a hitter looking to erase the pressure in one burst; he began as a batter willing to take the dull overs, let Daniel Mercer breathe at the other end, and wait for the ball to soften before expanding. When Mercer finally fell, Dunecrest were still only halfway safe, but Mason had already moved into the role that would define the first innings. Leo Bennett then gave the innings the acceleration it had been missing, and the partnership between Leo and Mason became the passage Harborline could never fully repair, Leo scoring with sharper timing, Mason holding the spine, both batters helped by the kind of extras that arrive quietly and then matter loudly when a total begins to grow. Owen Foster eventually removed Leo after a brisk fifty-four, and he would remain Harborline’s best wicket-taker, but by then Dunecrest had gone from survival to control. Zane Price added a short, hard burst, Aiden Clarke clipped and lifted enough at the finish, and even though Mason Vale fell before the final over after carrying the innings to seventy-eight, Dunecrest had reached 288 for eight. The number had not come from one smooth domination. It had come from a bruised start, Shaw’s early answer, Mason’s long repair, Leo’s tempo shift, Zane’s late hit, and Harborline losing control of several small margins through wides and loose closing overs. Chasing 289, Harborline needed a clean opening and one serious middle partnership. They got part of both, which is why the chase stayed alive for so long, yet never enough continuity to make Dunecrest panic completely. Ethan Blake and Adrian Cole began with care, collecting runs more through patience and extras than authority, before Dylan Fox removed Ethan and Ben Turner followed by bowling Mason Reed. Connor Hayes briefly gave Adrian a workable partner, but Mason Vale’s run-out cut that stand before it could become a platform, and suddenly Harborline were rebuilding for the third time inside the first third of the chase. Luke Parker then played the innings that made the result uncertain. He struck cleanly, especially when the length sat up, and his stand with Adrian Cole was the one passage where Harborline looked capable of turning the target into a managed chase rather than a chase of hope. Adrian was slower, more methodical, but that was exactly why the stand worked; Luke gave it force and Adrian gave it memory. When they reached the mid-innings stretch, Dunecrest had to hunt for an event rather than merely defend the rope. Ben Turner supplied the first event by trapping Luke, and Daniel Mercer supplied the second by removing Adrian soon after, and that little cluster changed the chase more than any single over of scoring had done. Ryan Cross and Dylan Price gave Harborline a second surge, one dangerous enough to take the reply beyond two hundred and make Dunecrest sharpen again, but Dylan Fox returned to take Dylan Price, Noah Grant’s brief attack was cut off by Tyler Shaw, Owen Foster was run out under pressure, and when Ryan Cross finally lost his stumps with the score still short of the line, the chase turned from a route into a sequence of late fragments. Caleb Turner tried to stretch the final overs, but there was too much left and too few partners. Liam Brooks fell with the total at 252, and Dunecrest won by 36 runs. The result fits the deeper shape of the match: Harborline won the opening phase, threatened again through Luke Parker and Adrian Cole, then threatened once more through Ryan Cross and Dylan Price, but Dunecrest owned the heavier passages, first through Mason Vale and Leo Bennett, then through timely wickets from Dylan Fox, Ben Turner, Daniel Mercer and Tyler Shaw. Mason Vale’s player-of-the-match award fits cleanly because his seventy-eight gave Dunecrest an innings after early damage, and his run-out of Connor Hayes stopped Harborline’s chase from finding its first real base. The scoreline was competitive enough to respect Harborline’s resistance, but the full match belonged to the side that recovered better, finished harder, and broke the chase every time it began to look organized.
