At Pinewater Oval in Eastmere, Dunecrest won the toss and chose to bat, and the decision almost immediately lost any practical force because Seabrook did not give the innings a single comfortable entrance point. Lucas Grant began with the new ball in a way that made even ordinary survival feel narrow, holding the line close enough to the stumps that every defensive prod looked like it might become a wicket, while Dylan Carter kept the other end heavy with dots and awkward lengths. Owen Marsh and Ari Khan tried to occupy the first few overs, but the score barely moved, and when Lucas had Owen caught with only three on the board, the whole innings began to take on that grim, airless shape that low youth totals often have. Noah Vale then went without adding anything, bowled by Lucas, and Dunecrest were already two down before they had produced a real scoring shot. Ari Khan was the only batter who briefly looked as though he might resist for a while, making five from twenty-three balls and finding one boundary, but even his stay was more survival than control. Ethan Noor made two alongside him, and the pair nudged the total to nine before Dylan removed the captain. Luca Pierce then faced nine balls without scoring before Dylan bowled him too, and by the time Marcus Dale entered the attack, Dunecrest were already boxed into a scorecard where every run felt accidental. Marcus turned that pressure into the decisive collapse. Ethan Noor was bowled, Rafi Bell was caught, Samir Cole fell soon after to Ben Foster, and the innings slipped to fifteen for seven without ever forming a partnership that looked capable of changing the field. Caleb Ward and Mason Hale produced the biggest stand of the innings, but that says more about the innings than about the partnership because it was worth only seven and four of those were extras. Caleb made four, Mason did not score, and even that minor delay never forced Seabrook to defend. Marcus came back to remove Caleb, then Mason, and finally Hugo Stone, leaving Jude Lane stranded not out on two as Dunecrest were bowled out for twenty-four in the eighteenth over. The score was brutally small, and the internal details made it even harsher: only fifteen runs came from the bat, nine came in extras, no batter reached double figures, and the fall of wickets repeatedly froze on the same score. Seabrook’s attack worked as a sequence rather than a random collapse. Lucas Grant made the first incision, Dylan Carter cut through the top and early middle, Ryan Shaw gave nothing away during the bridge overs, Ben Foster added one wicket, and Marcus Dale’s five for three turned a weak innings into a total with almost no defensive value. Chasing twenty-five, Seabrook did not allow even a token low-target wobble to appear. Leo Hart took the chase by the throat immediately, striking eighteen not out from eight balls with two fours and a six, while Ethan Brook played the simple supporting hand and stayed unbeaten on four. Dunecrest had only two bowlers involved, Ari Khan and Samir Cole, and neither could create a chance. Three wides helped the chase along, but the main point was that Seabrook were scoring with the bat, not merely waiting for mistakes. The target vanished in eleven legal deliveries, Seabrook finishing at twenty-five without loss. Marcus Dale was the correct Player of the Match because his spell broke the only section of the innings where Dunecrest could still have hoped to extend the score, but the win was also a full bowling-unit performance. Lucas Grant and Dylan Carter made the top order fragile, Ryan Shaw preserved the chokehold, Marcus completed the dismantling, and Leo Hart made sure the reply ended before Dunecrest could manufacture even symbolic pressure. The final result, Seabrook by ten wickets, matches the cricket exactly: one side lasted long enough to show resistance but not enough to build runs, while the other controlled the ball, the field, and the chase from the opening exchanges to the final stroke.
