At South Meridian Institute Ground in Westhaven, Shoreline Rangers chose to bat first and built one of those heavy age-group fifty-over totals that never depended on one clean uninterrupted march, because Meridian Strikers did find wickets in clusters and did have bowlers who kept dragging the innings back toward them, yet Shoreline kept answering every interruption with another useful partnership, another set batter, or another loose over that pushed the total further away from ordinary. Finn Archer and Leo Marsh gave Shoreline the first shape of the day, and although Leo’s 18 was more support than command, Finn looked in control from the start, scoring through proper cricket shots rather than panic swings and making sure the opening stand reached 62 before Zane Cooper finally removed Leo. When Rory Blake was bowled without scoring almost immediately after that, Meridian briefly had the kind of double-hit moment that can change a first innings. Shoreline were two down at the same score, and the fielding side had a real chance to turn a good start into a crisis. Finn did not allow that. He rebuilt with Miles Carter in the most important early-middle partnership of the innings, a stand of 76 that carried Shoreline to 138 and restored the innings completely. Carter’s 36 from 53 was not explosive, but it gave the card balance, and Finn’s 74 from 68 was the innings that made the first half of the total possible, because he had already crossed from bright opening hand into genuine platform-maker by the time he was finally dismissed. Meridian still fought. Aiden Scott’s spell was outstandingly controlled for a game that later became so one-sided, and Miles Benton kept taking wickets at important points, removing Finn, Dylan Vale, Noah Grant, and Aaron Miles. Zane Cooper’s three wickets also mattered because he prevented Shoreline from enjoying a completely clean top order. The problem for Meridian was that wickets never became full control. After Finn fell at 144, Dylan Vale took over the innings in a different way. He first moved through smaller stands with Connor Wells and Evan Price, neither huge on its own but both important because they prevented the innings from freezing after the set opener had gone. Then Dylan and Noah Grant produced the stand that really broke the match open, adding 68 and pushing the score from 184 to 252. Dylan’s 63 from 60 gave Shoreline the second major batting spine they needed, and Noah’s 24 from 28 made sure Meridian could not simply attack one end. Even after Dylan departed, Toby Reed and Logan Shaw found enough late runs to pull the innings to 282 all out in 47.2 overs. That final total was built from layers: the 62 at the start, the 76 after the double blow, the Dylan-led middle, and then the lower-order nudges. Meridian could point to Miles Benton’s four wickets, Zane Cooper’s three, and Aiden Scott’s extreme economy, but the 17 wides, the expensive spells around the control bowlers, and the inability to make any one breakthrough spell last for long meant Shoreline had reached a score that was already close to crushing. Arin then pushed on with Tyler Quinn and briefly looked like the one batter who might give Meridian a platform of their own. That is where Aaron Miles became the central figure of the match. He did not have a major batting role earlier, but with the ball he took the air out of the chase, first removing Tyler, then Blake, later Leo Foster and Cameron Ellis, finishing with four wickets for only four runs from five overs. Meridian’s batting never produced the kind of resistance required for a chase of 283. Arin’s 24 was the best hand, Tyler’s 13 was the only other double-figure score with any attacking shape, and the rest of the card either stalled or fell before a partnership could grow. Leo Foster battled for 25 balls for 2, Cameron Ellis survived briefly, Zane Cooper was left not out after one boundary, but none of those fragments changed the direction of the game. Shoreline’s defense was ruthless because it understood the scoreboard perfectly: they did not need miracle balls, only straight overs, held chances, and the discipline to keep making Meridian restart. By the time Miles Benton was bowled at 74 all out in the twenty-fifth over, the scale of the win felt fully earned. Shoreline had batted in waves, recovered from multiple wickets, and built a total too large for Meridian’s fragile reply. Meridian had individual bowling success, especially through Miles Benton, Zane Cooper, and Aiden Scott, but their chase had no equivalent to Finn Archer’s platform or Dylan Vale’s middle-overs authority. Aaron Miles was the correct player of the match because his spell transformed a difficult chase into a dismantled one, and the final result, Shoreline Rangers by 208 runs, reflected the full arc of the day: one side kept rebuilding until the score became enormous, and the other briefly resisted before the middle order collapsed beyond repair.
