At Westhaven Youth Cricket Ground in Meridian, Ridgefield against Coastline played out like one of those fifty-over youth matches where the early scoreboard promises more than the innings eventually delivers, because Ridgefield won the toss, chose to bat, and for a long opening stretch seemed to have built enough calm to shape the day, only for Coastline’s bowlers to keep peeling away the structure until a workable platform became a small target and a chase that briefly looked awkward became settled by one proper middle stand. Owen Hart and Leo Mercer began with the right idea for the format, not rushing the new ball, not trying to force a surface that was clearly rewarding straight bowling and patience, and their opening stand of 52 gave Ridgefield the first act they wanted. Owen was the batter with more authority, finding the rope when anything sat up and playing with enough shape to suggest he could carry the innings deep, while Leo’s 14 from 50 was slow but still useful because it held one end long enough for the partnership to form. Then the match changed. Ethan Brooks removed Leo, Noah Reed quickly cut off Carter Shaw and Ben Porter, and from 52 without loss Ridgefield were suddenly restarting every few overs instead of building. Owen kept trying to hold the card together and reached 46 from 74, but Aaron Vale got through him at 69, and that was the hinge of the first innings because the one batter who had properly read the pace of the day was gone before the score had reached safety. Gavin Lane followed without the total moving, Alec Stone’s little boundary hand ended almost as soon as it began, Adrian Vale and Nolan Reed were both taken by Aaron Vale, and Isaac Ford could not give Jason Brook the partnership he needed. Jason’s 27 from 42 was valuable because it dragged Ridgefield from the edge of embarrassment toward something at least defensible, and the last stand with Aiden Frost gave the innings a small final lift, but 112 all out in 40.4 overs was still well below what the opening stand had suggested. Coastline’s bowling was not one single over of chaos; it was a sustained squeezing job. Mason Cross and Noah Reed broke the first attempts at rebuilding, Ethan Brooks struck at the start and end, and Aaron Vale’s 10 overs for 11 with four wickets gave the innings its decisive shape. Chasing 113, Coastline began quickly enough to suggest a simple finish, with Miles Kane playing the most positive early shots in a 32-run opening stand, but Ridgefield did create one real moment of pressure. Miles fell first, Logan Pierce was bowled soon after by Carter Shaw, and Ethan Brooks was caught with the score only 43, so the chase was three down before it had fully settled. That was Ridgefield’s best chance, and for a few overs the small target did begin to feel larger. Cole Grant and Tyler Brook removed that tension by batting the situation rather than chasing the scoreboard. Cole made 43 from 40 with five fours and a six, and he understood that Coastline did not need reckless counterattack; they needed one batter to stay calm while the target kept shrinking. Tyler was quieter but essential, finishing unbeaten on 20 from 36 and refusing to let Ben Porter’s spell become a real collapse. Their fourth-wicket stand of 60 took Coastline from 43 for 3 to 103 for 4, and by the time Cole finally fell, the game had already been reduced to a handful of controlled runs rather than a dangerous finish. Aaron Vale then joined Tyler and helped close the match he had defined with the ball, finishing unbeaten as Coastline reached 114 for 4 in 22.5 overs. Ridgefield had one genuine innings from Owen, a lower-order resistance hand from Jason, and a strong defensive burst from Ben Porter, but they never recovered from the collapse after their opening stand. Coastline were sharper where the match actually turned: Aaron Vale broke the first innings, Cole Grant rebuilt the chase, and Tyler Brook stayed long enough to make sure the early wobble did not become a final twist. The result lands cleanly: Ridgefield 112 all out after wasting a stable start, Coastline 114 for 4 after one brief scare and one mature partnership, a six-wicket win built through bowling control first and chase management second.
