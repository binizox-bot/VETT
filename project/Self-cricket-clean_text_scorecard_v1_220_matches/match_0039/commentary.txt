At Southfield Cricket Ground in Eastport, Seacliff won the toss, chose to field, and for a few minutes that decision looked more than reasonable, because Northbridge lost Brandon Hale before the innings had really settled and the new ball was still doing enough to make every forward press feel slightly uncertain. Julian Moore found that first opening, Dylan Reed completed the catch, and Seacliff had the kind of early success that can make a fielding side believe the day is going to follow its plan. It did not quite go that way. Adrian Stone and Caleb Rowe absorbed the first wobble with a stand that did not feel spectacular in the moment but became the shape-setting passage of the innings. Adrian was the more authoritative player, compact through the line, quick to take anything overpitched through the off side, and disciplined enough not to chase every loose-looking ball. Caleb gave him the right second voice, busier against the change bowlers, willing to attack when the line drifted, and strong enough to stop Seacliff from simply building their whole field around Adrian. Their partnership did not rush, yet it kept removing Seacliff’s early advantage ball by ball. By the time Ian Foster removed Caleb, Northbridge had moved from a nervous beginning into a platform with real value. Adrian then carried the innings into its next phase alongside Dominic Vale, and that was where the total began to feel more than merely repaired. Dominic was not fluent in the same way, but he held enough shape to let Adrian keep controlling the scoring. Hudson Clarke eventually bowled Adrian, and that wicket mattered because Adrian had been the one batter giving Northbridge the cleanest sense of order. His seventy-six was the innings’ central piece: not a hundred, not a violent burst, but the hand that turned a first-over problem into a fifty-over total with structure. Seacliff did drag the innings back for a while after that. Dominic fell, Elliot Reed came and went, and the middle overs briefly had the kind of texture where a batting side can lose its platform without noticing until the total has flattened. Finley Lane prevented that. He was not spectacular, and that was the point. He stayed. He kept the innings intact while partners changed. Gavin Holt gave him a useful stand, not huge, but enough to push Northbridge out of the soft middle and into a closing phase where they could still reach a defensible score. Isaac Dean and Jordan Pike did not last long, but Kyle Mercer’s last little push beside Finley helped Northbridge close at two hundred and forty-seven for eight. That total had been built by sequence rather than explosion: Adrian and Caleb restoring the innings, Adrian and Dominic extending it, Finley giving the back end its spine, and smaller late contributions keeping Seacliff from finishing the innings on their terms. Seacliff’s bowlers were not poor. Julian Moore struck early and returned later. Hudson Clarke took two important wickets even though he was expensive. Ian Foster removed Caleb and Elliot. Gavin Price and the fielding unit added a late wicket through pressure. But Seacliff never quite turned a wicket into a three-over squeeze. Northbridge kept finding one more patch of batting just when the fielding side needed silence.

The chase began in the worst possible way for Seacliff. Aaron Cole was trapped first ball, and that was not just a wicket; it was a complete change in the emotional temperature of the reply. Blake Turner tried to answer with some intent, but Jordan Pike bowled him before the innings could take shape. Dylan Reed was pinned without scoring, Evan Brooks followed, and suddenly Carter Vale was walking through the ruins of a top order that had barely begun. At thirty-three for four, the target had not yet become enormous in pure arithmetic, but the innings had already lost its structure. Carter’s response was the reason the match became memorable rather than one-sided. He did not simply counterattack blindly. He rebuilt in sections. First Felix Grant stayed with him long enough to stop the freefall. Felix’s seventeen was not a long innings, but it gave Carter enough time to move from survival into scoring. Gavin Price then added a sharper hand, taking the ball on just enough to keep Northbridge from closing the ring around Carter. Even when Gavin fell and Hudson Clarke soon followed, Carter did not let the chase vanish. He recalibrated again. The partnership with Ian Foster was the match’s great swing back toward Seacliff. Ian played the correct supporting innings, patient, careful, and willing to take the quieter end of the job while Carter did the heavier scoring. Carter’s hundred came from inside a broken chase, which makes it a different kind of innings from a top-order century built on a platform. He had to repair the start, manage the scoring rate, farm momentum without looking desperate, and keep trusting that one proper stand could still pull the target back into reach. He drove cleanly, cut hard, and kept finding enough boundaries that Northbridge could never simply wait for the equation to die. When the eighth-wicket stand pushed Seacliff beyond two hundred, the match was suddenly alive in a way it had no right to be after the first few overs. Gavin Holt’s wicket of Ian Foster changed that. It broke the only partnership that had truly made Seacliff look capable of completing the chase. Julian Moore still came in swinging, and his short burst with Carter dragged the game deeper, helped by some wides and the nervousness that starts to creep into fielding sides when a supposedly broken chase keeps refusing to end. But the entire innings still depended on Carter. Kyle Mercer finally removed him with Seacliff still short, and once Carter was gone for a magnificent hundred and sixteen, the chase lost its center. Julian tried to keep it alive for a few more balls, but Isaac Dean held his nerve and finished it himself, taking the return catch that ended Seacliff’s innings at two hundred and thirty-seven. Northbridge won by ten runs, which sounds narrow because it was narrow, but the result also reflected how much of Seacliff’s chase rested on one extraordinary batter. Carter Vale was the best individual performer in the match, and his Player of the Match award fits even in defeat because that innings almost overturned a game that had been broken at the start. Northbridge won because their work was spread across more phases. Adrian Stone gave them the main first-innings platform, Finley Lane held the closing overs together, Jordan Pike ripped apart the start of the chase, Kyle Mercer claimed the two wickets that mattered late, and Isaac Dean closed the door. Seacliff had the greatest innings of the day, but Northbridge had the more complete match.
