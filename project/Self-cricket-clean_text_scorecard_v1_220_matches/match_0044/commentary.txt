At Greenfield Cricket Ground in Eastport, Coastmere chose to bat first and ended up playing one of those innings that kept threatening to become useful without ever really escaping Ridgefield’s grip, because the first stand gave them a reasonable base, the middle order kept finding little patches of resistance, and yet every time the card looked ready to settle into a proper shape, Adrian Hale or one of the supporting bowlers cut it down before it could grow into something defendable. Dorian Ward and Adrian Stone gave Coastmere a start that was sensible rather than forceful, Adrian Stone taking the straighter scoring options when they were offered and Dorian already looking like the player most prepared to carry time, absorb good balls, and turn loose width into controlled runs. Their opening partnership moved the innings into the forties, which was enough to deny Ridgefield the perfect new-ball beginning, but Finley Cross eventually found the first break, and that was the beginning of the stop-start pattern that followed. Cameron Vale stayed long enough to keep Dorian company but never long enough to change the tempo, and Isaac Vale’s lbw gave Ridgefield their second foothold. Elliot Moss then gave Coastmere the one middle-order partnership that briefly suggested a stronger total might still be built. His 22 had more intent than most of the later hands, and with Dorian still holding the innings together, the pair added the best stand of the card. That passage was important because it took Coastmere beyond the early danger and gave them a route toward a total around two hundred if somebody could stay. Adrian Hale stopped that route from opening. His first major intervention was the wicket of Dorian Ward for 49, a dismissal that mattered far beyond the raw number because Dorian had been the only batter joining the first phase, the repair phase, and the middle overs into one coherent thread. Once he fell, Coastmere were still alive, but they no longer had a true anchor. Elliot Moss followed soon after, pinned in front by the same bowler, and from that point the innings became a sequence of smaller resistance attempts rather than a structure. Mason Reed tried to hold the lower middle together, but he was constantly forced to start again with new partners. Caleb Frost was stumped, Finley Marsh was taken by Adrian Hale in his own follow-through, Gavin Holt was cut off by Logan Price, and even when Connor Hayes and Isaac Dunn dragged the innings into the final overs, it felt more like survival than late acceleration. Connor’s 11 from 29 and Isaac’s 14 from 26 did have value because they carried Coastmere from a vulnerable lower-order position into the mid-one-sixties, but they were not enough to turn the innings into something truly imposing. Kyle Mercer was left not out after Isaac Dunn became the final wicket, and Coastmere closed on 166 all out in the forty-sixth over. The total had effort in it, especially from Dorian Ward and from the late Isaac-Connor occupation, but it also carried the clear mark of Adrian Hale’s spell. His five wickets did not arrive as cheap tail-end decoration. He removed the central batter, the one promising middle-order partner, and then several of the pieces Coastmere needed to stretch the innings. Isaac Vale’s ten-over spell was just as important in a quieter way, because three maidens and only 21 conceded kept the middle overs tight enough that Coastmere never had the freedom to rebuild aggressively. Logan Price took two late wickets despite being the more expensive option, Finley Cross gave the first breakthrough, and Dorian Vale closed the innings. Chasing 167, Ridgefield then began in the worst possible way, Ethan Cole lbw first ball to Kyle Mercer, and for a brief moment that small target looked as though it might acquire the kind of pressure that only low chases can create. Adrian Hale refused to let that happen. He came in after the first-ball shock and batted as if the match needed to be simplified immediately, not through wild hitting, but through controlled aggression. Cameron Blake gave him the right partner: slow enough to stabilize, solid enough to prevent Coastmere from attacking only one end, and calm enough to let Adrian keep the tempo high. Their partnership of 93 essentially broke the chase open. Adrian’s 66 from 47 was the innings that erased Coastmere’s best chance of turning 166 into a nervous defence. He hit strongly square, drove cleanly when the ball was overpitched, and used the boundary options early enough that the field had to spread before Coastmere had created real scoreboard pressure. Cameron’s 26 looked modest next to that, but it was structurally vital because it kept the chase from becoming dependent on one batter. Gavin Holt eventually removed Adrian, and that wicket gave Coastmere a way back into the emotional side of the game even if the equation had already become much easier. He then took Cameron as well, and when Liam Parker’s brisk 22 ended after a short burst of hitting, the chase began to wobble more than Ridgefield would have wanted. Dorian Vale and Hayden Marsh added enough to keep the score moving, but Gavin Holt kept dragging the game back, having Hayden stumped and then watching Dorian run out immediately after, leaving the chase suddenly less clean. Isaac Vale was then bowled by Gavin Holt, and from a position that had looked almost routine, Ridgefield had slipped to seven down with a small but real amount still to finish. That is why the final partnership mattered. Finley Cross did not need to play a grand hand. He needed to stay. Noah Bennett, by contrast, needed only a few balls to close the argument, and he did it with exactly the right clarity, striking three boundaries in a short unbeaten burst and removing any lingering doubt before Coastmere could turn wickets into true panic. Ridgefield reached 169 for 7 in the twenty-seventh over and won by three wickets, a result that looked slightly tense at the end but was still rooted in the Adrian Hale passage much earlier. Coastmere had one major spell of resistance through Dorian Ward and a late, admirable fight from Gavin Holt with the ball, whose five wickets made the chase far messier than it should have been. But Ridgefield had the decisive all-rounder. Adrian Hale first broke Coastmere’s innings with the ball, then answered the first-ball loss in the chase with the innings that made the target manageable. The late wickets made the finish entertaining; they did not change the underlying balance of the match.
