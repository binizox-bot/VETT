At South Meridian Institute Ground in Westhaven, Bayline Titans won the toss, chose to bat, and ended up producing the kind of fifty-over total that never looked dominant in a clean top-order sense but still became awkward because enough small repairs kept arriving before Meridian Strikers could fully close the innings down. Kai Mercer started with a little snap, finding one boundary and one six in his 17, while Owen Hayes took the longer, more cautious route at the other end, but the opening stand was cut off at 24 when Zane Cooper removed the captain and forced Bayline into their first reset. Noel Reed briefly gave Owen company, and then Luca Parris supplied the most fluent early-middle hand, striking 23 from 22 and helping Owen carry the innings to 86 before Miles Benton held the return catch and stopped the first proper surge. From there the innings became a sequence of partial rebuilds. Carter Blake made 15 in a slow holding stand with Owen, but Aiden Scott, who bowled an excellent controlling spell, removed him at 110. Owen himself fell soon after for 46 from 66, and at 113 for 5 the innings was in danger of shrinking into something very chaseable. Miles Benton kept striking at the right moments, later removing Dylan Shore and Mason Ford, and with Oscar Price also struggling to move freely, Meridian had the game where they wanted it for a long stretch. The problem for them was that Caleb Turner refused to let Bayline fade away. He first worked through a slow stand with Oscar, then after Oscar was run out at 165 for 8, he found the decisive late partnership with Nate Shaw. It was not a huge stand in the ordinary sense, but the unbeaten 24 at the end changed the match because Caleb’s 45 not out, built with one boundary and three sixes, pushed Bayline to 189 for 8 instead of something in the low 170s. Nate Shaw’s 13 not out also mattered because he stayed with him, turned over just enough strike, and kept the tail from exposing the last batter. Meridian’s bowling was good in important pockets: Aiden Scott gave away only 13 in ten overs and took the key wickets of Carter Blake and Owen Hayes, Miles Benton’s four wickets repeatedly stopped Bayline from building through the middle, and Zane Cooper gave the first breakthrough. Still, the last phase slipped just enough to matter. Chasing 190, Meridian Strikers began with reasonable shape rather than panic. Arin Wells and Isaac Vale added 34, Isaac looking the sharper of the two with four boundaries, but Logan Brooks bowled Arin and Nate Shaw quickly removed Isaac, so the chase had to be rebuilt from 38 for 2. Evan Carter became the batter around whom almost everything turned. He was not explosive, but he read the chase clearly, first rebuilding with Tyler Quinn, then Aiden Scott, then Blake Morgan, and each time Meridian looked close to taking control, Bayline found a way to remove the partner. Tyler’s run-out at 69 hurt because he had given the innings momentum. Aiden’s dismissal at 94 slowed the chase again. Blake Morgan then helped produce the most important middle stand, a 40-run passage that took Meridian to 134 for 5 and made the target look very reachable, but Oscar Price removed him before the stand could become decisive. Evan still kept the chase alive and reached 52 from 93, a patient innings full of responsibility, yet Nate Shaw’s second wicket at 154 was the biggest moment of the reply because it removed the one player who had held all the broken pieces together. Julian Vale and Leo Foster then tried to finish the chase in smaller steps, and when Leo and Cameron Ellis pushed the score to 185 with one ball still to be accounted for, the match had become almost absurdly tight. Bayline’s composure in the field decided it. Cameron was run out by Kai Mercer off the penultimate ball, and Miles Benton was run out by the same fielder on the final ball, leaving Leo Foster stranded on 17 not out and Meridian three runs short at 186 for 9. That finish fitted the whole game. Bayline did not win through one huge innings or one overwhelming bowling spell. They won because Caleb Turner’s late 45 not out gave them a cushion that looked modest at the time and enormous by the end, because Nate Shaw removed Isaac Vale and Evan Carter at the two most important stages of the chase, because Oscar Price broke two rebuilding stands, and because their fielding held at the exact moment Meridian needed calm. Meridian had enough contributors to stay alive all day, especially Evan Carter, Isaac Vale, Tyler Quinn, Blake Morgan, Julian Vale, and Leo Foster, but every partnership ended just before it could become control. Bayline Titans 189 for 8, Meridian Strikers 186 for 9, Bayline winning by three runs after a match built almost entirely out of small margins, late nerves, and one final-ball fielding act that turned a narrow defense into a completed one.
