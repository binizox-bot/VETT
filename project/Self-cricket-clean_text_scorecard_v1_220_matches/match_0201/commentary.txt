At Birchwood Academy Ground in Norchester, Ridgefield won the toss, chose to field, and then built their match through the kind of pressure that rarely looks spectacular for one over at a time but slowly leaves the batting side with no clean way to breathe. Coastmere never truly settled. Evan Brooks was run out early with Oliver Reed involved, Aaron Pike was bowled soon after, and by the time the scoreboard showed only seven for two, the innings already had the cramped feel of a side trying to survive rather than construct. Logan West and Isaac Vale briefly slowed the damage, but their partnership was more a holding pattern than a rebuild, helped heavily by wides and leg-side leakage, and Oliver Reed returned to bowl Logan before the innings could take on shape. Isaac Vale did enough to reach twelve, but Caleb Turner removed him at thirty-three, then later took Tyler Marsh and Jordan Hayes as part of a middle-overs spell that gave Coastmere almost no release. Tyler Marsh used up time without pushing the field back. Liam Stone tried to stay around Gavin Moore, but Luke Parker removed him at fifty, and when Jordan fell at fifty-one, Coastmere were seven down with the innings threatening to disappear completely. Gavin Moore was the only batter who gave the card a proper line of resistance. His thirty-five from seventy-two balls was slow, but in this match it mattered because he was the one player who could hold a phase together. With Cole Bennett he added thirty-four for the eighth wicket, and that stand dragged Coastmere from total wreckage into something Ridgefield still had to chase rather than merely sign off. Oliver Reed broke that resistance by trapping Cole, then also removed Aiden Frost, and Nathan Shaw finally ended Gavin’s stay with Owen Barrett taking the catch. Coastmere closed on ninety-four all out in the forty-third over, and the structure of the innings tells the truth: one late partnership gave the score a little dignity, but the batting unit never owned a full passage. Oliver Reed’s four for sixteen was the defining spell because it struck at both ends of the innings, while Caleb Turner’s three wickets and four maidens made the middle feel almost impossible to repair. Ryan Cross, Nathan Shaw, and Luke Parker kept enough control around them that Coastmere’s only real scoring phase arrived too late and from too deep in the order. Chasing ninety-five, Ridgefield had one mild interruption when Owen Barrett was caught behind off Tyler Marsh after a cautious four, leaving the score at eighteen for one. That was the only moment Coastmere could point to. Ethan Cole was already looking far too clean at the crease, and Lucas Grant joined him with the exact calm needed to prevent a small target from becoming awkward. Ethan’s unbeaten sixty-one from thirty-eight was the innings that stripped the chase of tension, full of sharp boundary hitting and enough six-hitting power to make the field spread almost immediately. Lucas Grant gave him steady, useful support with twenty-two not out, and their unbroken seventy-eight-run stand carried Ridgefield home in the thirteenth over. Coastmere’s bowlers had no sustained pressure after Tyler’s early wicket. Gavin Moore and Carter Fox leaked too much, Aiden Frost and Evan Brooks could not turn their short spells into threat, and the extras only made the target disappear faster. Ridgefield reached ninety-six for one, won by nine wickets, and qualified with a performance that felt controlled from the first innings through the chase. Oliver Reed was the correct Player of the Match because his spell shaped the day before Ethan Cole finished it, and the wider story is simple: Coastmere survived for overs without building authority, Ridgefield kept taking wickets before any recovery could harden, then chased with enough speed and certainty to make the result look exactly as one-sided as the cricket had been.
