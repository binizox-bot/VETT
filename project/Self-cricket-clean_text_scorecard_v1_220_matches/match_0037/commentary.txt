At Southfield Cricket Ground in Eastport, Ridgefield A chose to bat first in a shortened contest and built their innings in the sort of layered, professional way that does not always look explosive from the first over but steadily leaves the chasing side with less and less room to breathe. Adrian Stone and Liam Brooks gave them the foundation, and the important thing about that opening stand was how little of it felt accidental. Liam did the quieter work, taking time, getting behind the ball, letting the first spells pass without feeding the cordon, and giving Adrian enough of the strike without turning the innings into one-man batting. Adrian was the clearer force from early on. He was not swinging for show; he was finding the ball earlier than anyone else, especially through the off side, and whenever Coastline A strayed a fraction too full or too wide, he converted those errors into clean, controlled boundaries. The opening stand carried Ridgefield deep into the first third of the innings and, because the extras were not doing all the work for them, it felt like a genuine batting platform rather than a scoreboard inflated by loose bowling. Mason Hart eventually broke it by removing Liam, but the wicket did not change the balance immediately because Adrian was already set and still reading the pace of the surface well. Owen Parker came in and gave the innings exactly the correct second voice. He did not freeze after the first wicket, did not try to overmatch Adrian either, and that mattered because Coastline needed a quiet passage to drag the game back. Instead Owen’s boundaries kept the field spread, while Adrian continued to work the gaps and punish the bad ball with very little fuss. The second stand carried Ridgefield into command, and when Isaac Shaw finally removed Adrian, the opener had already made the innings’ defining contribution. His seventy-six was the sort of top-order hand that shapes everything after it: not a hundred, not a violent burst, but long enough, clean enough, and authoritative enough to make the rest of the batting order play from a position of control. Coastline briefly found an opening after that. Owen went not long later, and the innings had to be rebuilt through the middle rather than simply allowed to roll. Caleb Hayes handled that phase with proper captain’s patience. He began carefully, let Connor Shaw play the linking hand, and then carried the innings again once Connor was bowled by Dylan Fox. Caleb’s stand with Ryan Foster was important because it stopped Coastline from turning a couple of wickets into a real squeeze. Ryan was not rushing at first; he was reading what the innings still needed, taking singles, keeping the field moving, and waiting for the later overs to arrive. When Caleb fell, Ridgefield had already passed into a strong position, but the total still needed a finish to become properly difficult. Dylan Price supplied the sharper late hand, hitting with enough intent to change the final shape of the innings, while Ryan stayed there and made sure the closing overs did not become a collapse after the hitters came and went. Dylan’s cameo, Lucas Reed’s brief appearance, the slightly untidy run-out of Evan Brooks, and the final single from Ryan all helped Ridgefield close on two hundred and fifty-three for eight from their forty-eight overs. It was not a score built only on Adrian, although his innings clearly sat at the center of it. It was Adrian first, Owen and Caleb keeping the middle alive, Ryan Foster controlling the finish, and Dylan Price giving the endgame its bite. Coastline’s bowlers had respectable pieces: Liam Foster’s two wickets and control, Isaac Shaw’s removal of both Adrian and Owen, Mason Hart’s early breakthrough, Connor Wells’ late strike, and Dylan Fox’s middle-overs wicket. The problem was that their pressure came in sections rather than in long blocks. Ridgefield kept finding a next partnership before the previous one had fully lost value. Chasing two hundred and fifty-four, Coastline needed one of their top four to own a long portion of the reply, and instead the innings kept producing danger in flashes, then losing the player who could have stretched that danger into a serious chase. Ben Carter went early to Evan Brooks, and Tyler Grant never settled long enough to take charge before Zane Perry bowled him. Isaac Shaw briefly changed the mood with a sharp, attacking hand, striking boundaries quickly enough to make Ridgefield adjust the field and think about more than simple containment. Daniel Cross gave that burst a little structure, and for a few overs the chase had its first real rhythm. Lucas Reed’s lbw against Isaac was therefore a major wicket, because it removed the one batter who had actually made the target feel smaller. Daniel then became the center of the reply, first with Aaron Blake, then with Luke Barrett. Aaron’s contribution was mostly occupation, but it gave Daniel enough time to work through the first rebuild. Dylan Price broke that stand, and again Coastline had to restart. Luke Barrett’s arrival gave them their best sustained passage of the chase. He was not wildly fast, but he was composed, strong enough to punish the loose ball, and durable enough to stop Ridgefield from closing the game immediately after Daniel’s wicket pressure began building. Daniel’s forty-four kept the innings alive through the middle, and the stand with Luke was the moment when Coastline looked most capable of making Ridgefield work hard for the result. Aiden Cole ended that threat by removing Daniel, and then Leo Turner followed shortly after, which put Coastline into a familiar pattern: a useful phase, then a wicket just before it could become a platform. Luke and Connor Wells still refused to fold. Their partnership dragged the chase deep enough to keep the score respectable and force Ridgefield to bowl their closing spells properly, yet the asking rate and wickets were both working against them. Zane Perry returned to bowl Luke, and that was the true end of the chase as a live contest. Dylan Fox fell soon after, Mason Hart tried to occupy time but could not turn it into a finish, and Lucas Reed removed the final two wickets to close the innings at two hundred and two. Coastline had not been humiliated. Isaac Shaw, Daniel Cross, Luke Barrett, and Connor Wells all left marks on the reply. But they never found the one long partnership that a chase of this size demanded. Ridgefield’s bowling group understood that. They did not need one spectacular spell to dominate the whole innings. They needed repeated interruptions, and they got them: Evan Brooks with the first wicket, Zane Perry with the early and late control, Lucas Reed with the key wicket of Isaac and the closing blows, Dylan Price with Aaron Blake, and Aiden Cole with the middle-overs strikes that removed Daniel and Leo. Adrian Stone’s Player of the Match award fits because the whole result was built from the first innings platform he created. His seventy-six gave Ridgefield enough command to absorb the later wickets, and once the bowlers kept breaking Coastline’s reply into fragments, that platform became the difference between a competitive chase and a fifty-one-run win. Ridgefield A were not perfect in every phase, but they played the match in sequence better than Coastline did: first building, then managing, then finishing, then defending through timely wickets rather than panic.
