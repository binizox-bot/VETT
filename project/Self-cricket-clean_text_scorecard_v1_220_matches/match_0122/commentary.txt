Mariner Bay won the toss at Redstone Cricket Ground in Mirrendale and chose to field, and for the first stretch of Ridgefield’s innings that decision looked as though it might be punished heavily, because Miles Tanner and Ronan Holt gave the batting side a start with very different kinds of pressure attached to it. Ronan was not the spectacular part of the stand, but he was important because he stayed long enough to let the opening phase breathe, took the single when Miles was hot, and kept enough presence at the other end to prevent Mariner Bay from simply bowling at one batter. Miles was the player who made the powerplay dangerous. His fifty-eight from twenty-four balls came with five fours and five sixes, and the tone of that innings was immediate: anything full enough disappeared, anything short enough was pulled or cut, and anything in between still seemed to be traveling hard enough to make the fielders feel late. The first stand reached seventy, and at that stage Ridgefield looked as if they might be heading far beyond a normal strong total. Mariner Bay needed the first interruption badly, and Hudson Clarke finally provided it by removing Ronan. That wicket did not stop the damage, but it at least changed the rhythm. Ian Frost then removed Miles, and that was the first major recovery point for the fielding side because Ridgefield’s most violent hitter had gone before the innings could run completely out of reach. Carter Flynn and Dylan Ross then gave Ridgefield the second layer of the innings, less wild than the first stand but still valuable, because they prevented Mariner Bay from turning two wickets into a full middle-over squeeze. Dylan’s twenty-eight was compact and useful, Carter’s twenty-five carried the innings into the later overs, and for a while Ridgefield seemed to be rebuilding toward a finish that could still touch the really severe range. Felix Moore cut that passage by removing Dylan, and Evan Brooks followed by taking Carter, which meant the innings had finally started to narrow after all that early momentum. The late overs then became a contest between Ridgefield’s short bursts and Mariner Bay’s strike bowling. Hudson Vale played the best of those bursts, twenty-four from twelve with clean hitting that kept the total moving upward even after the middle pair had been broken. Leo Grant came and went, Felix Ward fell without scoring, and the innings briefly stumbled at the exact point where Ridgefield would have wanted a clean acceleration. Felix Moore and Evan Brooks were the main reason for that. Both finished with three wickets, and both struck in the phase where Ridgefield were trying to convert a strong total into something closer to unreachable. Julian Moss added only a small hand, Gavin Price and Oscar Lane nudged the last few balls, and Ridgefield closed on one hundred and eighty-four for eight. It was a strong total, certainly, but it carried two emotional truths at once. Ridgefield had opened with enough violence to dominate the first half, yet Mariner Bay had taken enough late wickets to feel they had saved themselves from something worse. That mattered when the chase began, because one hundred and eighty-five was difficult but not impossible. Then Logan Field made it feel almost impossible in two balls. Kai Mercer was bowled first ball. Noah Hayes was trapped in front second ball. Mariner Bay were nought for two before the chase had even become a chase, and Ridgefield had the kind of opening that can make a large target feel twice as large. Adrian Cole, however, walked into that chaos and never once looked as though the situation had made him frantic. That was the central reason Mariner Bay survived the first over and then eventually took the whole match away. Carter Miles helped him through the immediate repair, adding a useful twelve and making sure Adrian did not have to carry the first few overs alone. The third-wicket stand was only twenty-eight, but in context it mattered because it moved Mariner Bay from crisis into something that could at least be played. Felix Ward broke that stand by removing Carter, and at twenty-eight for three Ridgefield still looked strongly placed. One more wicket there and the chase probably collapses into a scramble. Instead, Adrian Cole and Dylan Reed produced the partnership that defined the game. At first it was rescue work. Adrian took the lead, choosing his scoring options carefully, punishing the bad ball but refusing to chase everything just because the target was large. Dylan Reed began with the kind of calm that is often more valuable than immediate fireworks, staying with Adrian, reading the bounce, and making sure the innings had no fourth shock. Then the stand changed gear. Adrian began to hit through and over the field with complete authority, and the boundaries came without the innings ever feeling reckless. His unbeaten ninety-four from fifty balls was not just aggressive; it was controlled aggression from a batter who understood the chase’s mathematics better than the bowlers did. Dylan’s unbeaten seventy-three from forty-four gave the chase its second engine. He hit six sixes, many of them once the field had already been forced wider by Adrian, and his ability to clear the rope meant Ridgefield could not simply build one plan around one set batter. That was what broke the defence. Logan Field’s opening over had given Ridgefield a dream start, but by the end even his figures had been dragged into the damage of the stand. Oscar Lane could not hold the rate down. Ian Frost’s no-balls were costly at a time when the chasing pair were already set. Gavin Price’s single over leaked momentum. Julian Moss was the one bowler who looked capable of restoring a little order, but control without a wicket had limited value once Adrian and Dylan were this deep. The unbroken fourth-wicket stand reached one hundred and fifty-seven, and by the time Mariner Bay entered the final stretch of the chase, the target no longer felt protected. It felt dismantled. Adrian kept finding the rope with a mixture of timing and force, Dylan kept clearing the field whenever Ridgefield tried to change the pace, and the pair refused to let the required rate create panic. Mariner Bay reached one hundred and eighty-five for three in the eighteenth over, winning by seven wickets with the kind of chase that completely redefines an innings after a nightmare start. Ridgefield will look back at this as a match they had in their hands twice: first through Miles Tanner’s powerplay assault and then through Logan Field’s two wickets in two balls. But they could not find the fourth wicket, and that failure became fatal. Mariner Bay, by contrast, absorbed the worst possible beginning and then answered it with one of the cleanest chase partnerships imaginable. Adrian Cole as Player of the Match is the only sensible call. He came in with the score already damaged, watched another partner fall before the fourth over was complete, and then batted through the entire chase with authority, judgment, and power. Dylan Reed’s role was nearly as large, because his unbeaten seventy-three turned the recovery into domination. But Adrian was the thread from the crisis to the finish, and that is why Mariner Bay won a match that had looked, after two balls of the chase, almost lost.
