At Westhaven Youth Cricket Ground in Meridian, this Western match match had the feel of a fifty-over contest that was decided long before the chase became formal, because Ridgefield chose to bat first, found a few pockets of resistance, and yet never managed to turn any of them into control, while Coastline kept dragging the innings back into the same narrow corridor until the target was small enough to be handled with patience rather than risk. Ridgefield’s beginning was not disastrous at first. Kian Hart and Theo Wells gave them a careful opening stand, Kian playing the more active hand with a couple of early boundaries, Theo taking the slower, holding role, and for a few overs it looked as though the batting side might at least build one of those long youth platforms where scoring is secondary to staying intact. That hope changed quickly once Damian Ross broke through. Kian went at 20, Blake Fen was bowled almost immediately, and suddenly the innings had lost its clean opening shape. Theo and Owen Pike then tried to rebuild, and their stand was the first real repair job of the day, not fast, not pretty, but necessary. Owen slowly became the central figure of the innings, leaving well, defending straight, and taking the few scoring chances that arrived, while Theo’s stay gave Ridgefield enough time to reach the mid-forties before Mason Clarke got through him. From there the innings became increasingly tight. Isaac Flynn occupied deliveries but could not release the pressure, and Ronan Cross began to take over the match with a spell that was less about raw pace than relentless accuracy. He bowled Isaac, then trapped Owen lbw after the only Ridgefield batter who had properly settled had reached 30 from 73 balls, and that wicket was the decisive blow because Owen had been the one player standing between a low score and a complete collapse. Cameron Hale fell to Gavin Reed, Dylan Moor was removed by Ronan, Ethan Shore was run out after a long struggle to move the score, and the tail never found a way to disturb the rhythm of the fielding side. Luke Mercer stayed at the end, Connor Tate and Ryan Shaw added only scraps, and Ridgefield were all out for 84 in 34.2 overs. Coastline’s control was built in layers. Damian Ross gave them the early double strike, Gavin Reed held one end down across a long spell, Mason Clarke removed the important third wicket, Logan Brooks cleaned up the last two, and Ronan Cross supplied the heart of the innings with 8 overs, 3 maidens, 11 runs, and 3 wickets, a spell that fully justified the player-of-the-match award because it cut through the one phase where Ridgefield still had a batter capable of dragging the total toward something more awkward. Chasing 85, Coastline still had to avoid the kind of careless start that can make a tiny target feel strange, and Ridgefield did get a small opening when Evan Mills was caught early and Damian Ross later fell after making 17. At 39 for 2, there was just enough scoreboard space for the fielding side to imagine one more wicket creating tension. Tristan Hayes and Logan Brooks removed that thought. Tristan’s unbeaten 38 was exactly the innings the chase needed, calm without being passive, clean enough to punish the bad ball, and patient enough not to make the chase about ego. Logan Brooks gave him the right support with 21 not out, including the one six that finally knocked the remaining pressure out of the contest, and their unbeaten stand of 47 carried Coastline to 86 for 2 in 17.4 overs. Ridgefield had a few useful bowling moments from Ryan Shaw, Connor Tate, and Kian Hart, but the target simply did not give them room to build a sustained squeeze. Once Tristan and Logan settled, the finish became orderly rather than dramatic. The match therefore resolves cleanly: Ridgefield had one slow top-order resistance through Owen Pike but never enough batting depth around him, while Coastline bowled with discipline, broke the innings in phases, and then chased with enough composure to make an eight-wicket win feel entirely natural.
