At Greenfield Cricket Ground in Eastport, Coastbay A chose to field first and had enough early control in patches to believe the decision could work, but Ridgeford A turned the innings into a long, layered accumulation that kept refusing to give the fielding side the one clean collapse they needed. Adrian Stone and Brandon Hale did not start as if they were chasing a spectacle. They started like two batters who understood the value of making the first spell fade without damage. Brandon was the earlier aggressor, quicker onto width and much more willing to drive on the rise, while Adrian took the more measured route, working himself into the surface before beginning to find the square boundary and the straight push with increasing certainty. Their opening stand became the first major shape of the match. Coastbay did not gift all of it away; Liam Foster and Mason Hart had decent spells, Connor Wells beat the bat often enough, and Isaac Shaw was already asking questions. The problem was that one good over rarely became two. A good line would be followed by a loose ball, a quiet passage would be interrupted by a boundary, and Brandon in particular kept damaging the sense that Coastbay were in control. When Gavin Holt trapped him, Ridgeford had already moved into a position where the first wicket felt more like a pause than a breakthrough. Adrian then carried the innings through the next phase with Cameron Blake. Cameron’s innings was slow by comparison, but it had a role. He stayed long enough for Adrian to finish the platform, and when Adrian finally fell to Isaac Shaw, Ridgeford were still well placed rather than exposed. Cameron followed later, and that was the moment Coastbay needed to turn the innings into a squeeze. Instead, Lucas Barrett and Finley Lane played the stand that gave Ridgeford their real weight. Lucas brought the sharper threat, using his feet well and clearing the rope often enough to change the field, while Finley worked the ball into the gaps and prevented the innings from becoming a one-man rush. Their partnership moved the total out of the merely useful range and toward something far more demanding. Finley’s dismissal gave Coastbay a chance to slow the end, but Lucas stayed a little longer with Dominic Ward and kept the pressure on the bowlers. Isaac Shaw did good work in that phase, removing Lucas and Dominic after already taking Adrian, and his spell was the one Coastbay passage that most clearly cut into Ridgeford’s control. Yet Ridgeford still found late value through Elliot Vale, Isaac Dunn, Jordan Pike, and the small final movement around the last two wickets. It was not a perfectly clean finish. There were dismissals, a little clutter, and enough late pressure to stop the total running past three hundred. But Ridgeford closed on two hundred and ninety-three for nine, and the total reflected the innings correctly: a strong opening, a steady second phase, a major middle surge, and enough lower-order fragments to keep the score climbing. Coastbay had useful bowling figures in places. Isaac Shaw’s three wickets gave them their best route back. Liam Foster removed two late names. Connor Wells and Owen Grant struck at meaningful moments. Dylan Fox picked up Elliot Vale before the damage could become larger. Still, they were always reacting to a batting side that kept rebuilding before the last phase had fully collapsed.

The chase began well enough to make the game briefly interesting. Adrian Wells and Brandon Shaw gave Coastbay the one partnership they badly needed at the top, and for a while it looked as if they had decided the only way to approach a target this large was to keep risk controlled but not passive. Adrian was fluent, finding the rope repeatedly and giving the reply its first real voice. Brandon was slower, much more watchful, but he helped keep the opening stand alive long enough to make Ridgeford think. That stand reached into the sixties and delayed the pressure that usually arrives early in a chase of this size. Then Elliot Vale entered the match in the way that defined it. He removed Adrian, and that wicket did more than break the opening pair. It removed the batter who had made the target look at least faintly divisible. Caleb Foster tried to settle with Brandon, but Elliot struck again, and from there the chase began to narrow. Brandon was bowled by Jordan Pike soon after, which meant Coastbay had lost both openers and the first repair hand before the innings had reached a truly stable point. Elliot Marsh and Mason Reid tried to pause the slide, but Elliot Vale kept finding the right moment to intervene. Mason went. Elliot Marsh went. Finley Cross stayed briefly and tried to give the middle order some resistance, but the spell had changed the atmosphere of the innings. Coastbay were no longer chasing with intent; they were trying to make each new partnership last long enough to matter. Gavin Holt produced one small counterpunch, but Jordan Pike removed him. Connor Hayes then became the last batter capable of giving the score some dignity, moving into the teens while the lower order struggled to stay with him. The problem was that Ridgeford’s bowling did not loosen after the main wickets. Jordan Pike kept attacking the stumps and the pads. Isaac Dunn took Jordan Vale. Elliot Vale returned to complete his five-wicket haul by removing Finley Cross, the last middle-order player who might have turned survival into a stand. After that, the rest was a controlled closing operation. Isaac Shaw briefly struck a boundary and fell. Owen Grant stayed at the end. Connor Hayes was the last wicket, bowled by Jordan Pike, and Coastbay were all out for one hundred and forty-two in the thirty-third over. The margin became huge because the chase had only one genuinely healthy partnership and no second platform. Adrian Wells gave them a start, Brandon Shaw helped build it, and then the innings broke in stages. Elliot Vale was the reason it broke. His five wickets did not come as tail-end decoration after the match was already done; they arrived at the exact points Coastbay needed to rebuild: the fluent opener, the first support batter, the middle-order reset, the secondary stabilizer, and finally the captain. That is why his Player of the Match award fits more strongly than any batting contribution, even in a game where Ridgeford had three major top-order hands. Adrian Stone, Brandon Hale, and Lucas Barrett gave Ridgeford the total. Elliot Vale made that total feel untouchable. Coastbay’s opening stand hinted at a chase, but Ridgeford’s bowling turned the hint into a memory very quickly, and once the middle order failed to form around it, the result moved steadily toward a one-sided win.
