At Harborview College Ground in Eastgate, Brookport won the toss and chose to field, and the decision looked justified almost immediately because Islehaven never found the stable opening shape that a fifty-over innings needs, even in a low-scoring match. Leo Marsh gave Brookport the first cut when Kai Mercer was caught by Samir Vale with only five on the board, and that early wicket mattered less for the number itself than for the tone it created: Islehaven were not going to be allowed a quiet bedding-in period. Finn Carter then became the central figure of the innings. Tomas Reed tried to hang around, but he was bowled before he could turn survival into scoring; Nolan Frost arrived and was trapped almost immediately; and when Evan Rowe, briefly the only batter beside Arun Hale to show any sign of attacking timing, was bowled as well, Islehaven were already four down with only twenty-two on the board. Arun Hale was the one player who refused to let the innings vanish entirely. His unbeaten twenty-seven was slow, careful, and often more about occupation than pressure, but in the context of the batting card it was the only real resistance Islehaven had. He stood through every collapse point, watched partners arrive under pressure, and kept trying to steal enough singles and late deflections to drag the total toward something his bowlers could at least look at. Evan’s six gave him the first small partnership, and later Rafi Stone and Ben Ellis helped him stretch the innings in little fragments, but none of those phases became a true rebuild. The biggest stand of the innings was only fourteen, and that tells the story more clearly than any single dismissal. Brookport did not need to keep breaking large partnerships; they only had to prevent small ones from becoming real. Arjun Cole removed Milo Grant and then Dylan Shore, Mason Pike took out Ben Ellis, Carter Wells was run out after a brief late push with Arun, and Owen Hale closed the innings by taking Hugo Lane caught and bowled. Islehaven finished on sixty-four all out in the twenty-seventh over, and even that figure leaned on extras as much as batting authority. The batters made only fifty-two, the extras added twelve, and no one apart from Arun reached double figures. Finn Carter’s spell was the decisive act: eight overs, three maidens, four wickets, and the key middle damage that kept the batting side from ever forming a working base. Leo Marsh and Arjun Cole supported him with wickets, Noah Bright’s three-over spell gave almost nothing away, and the rest of the attack kept enough control to ensure Islehaven could never turn survival into momentum. Chasing sixty-five, Brookport had no need for recklessness, but Samir Vale and Ethan Brook still made sure the chase would not drift into the nervous, low-target pattern that can sometimes create unnecessary drama. Ethan was the sharper early scorer, driving cleanly and lifting the tempo with three boundaries and a six, while Samir played with the calm of a wicketkeeper-opener who understood that one solid partnership would finish the day. Their opening stand of forty-eight took the target almost completely out of Islehaven’s reach, and although Milo Grant did give his side a small moment by having Ethan caught by Arun Hale, that wicket arrived too late to alter the contest. Caleb Ross joined Samir with only a handful still required, and the two of them ended the chase without letting Islehaven imagine a second opening. Samir finished unbeaten on thirty-one, Caleb stayed there with seven, and Brookport reached sixty-five for one in the eighth over. Islehaven’s bowling had one wicket and a few loose moments, but defending sixty-four leaves no margin for an expensive over or a missed line, and they gave Brookport enough width and extras to make the chase feel even lighter. Milo Grant took the only wicket but conceded twenty-five in three overs. Evan Rowe and Arun Hale could not keep the scoring quiet enough, and Hugo Lane’s short spell came with the match already slipping away. The result therefore reads cleanly: Islehaven were squeezed into a tiny total by Finn Carter’s new-ball and early-middle pressure, Arun Hale stood alone in a broken innings, and Brookport then chased with enough control and speed to make the nine-wicket margin feel entirely fair. Finn Carter was the correct Player of the Match because the match was effectively decided by the collapse he created. His wickets removed the structure from Islehaven’s batting, reduced the target to something Brookport could approach freely, and gave the chase the character of a formality rather than a contest.
