At Willowbank Lakeside Ground in Eastport, Harborlight Women chose to field first and from the opening stretch the match took on that familiar low-scoring shape where the batting side is not destroyed immediately but is never allowed to stand up straight either, because Dunecrest Women kept finding just enough bat on ball to stay alive while Harborlight kept finding the next interruption before any partnership could become a full platform. Mira Stone and Clara Wells began with patience rather than force, and for a while that looked sensible enough, Mira clipping and steering her way to fifteen while Clara took longer to judge the surface, yet the opening stand of thirty-one already had a slightly restrained feel to it, not a launch, more a holding pattern, with two extras helping and the fielding side still very much able to keep the ring tight. Ruby Hale then made the first incision by removing Mira Stone, and that wicket mattered because it broke the only phase of the innings in which Dunecrest had both openers present and the scoreboard moving without visible panic. Clara Wells stayed, and she became the one player in the Dunecrest card who looked capable of giving the innings a spine. Leah Quinn briefly gave her some company and found one boundary, but Ruby Hale cut that short too, and when Sophie Lane trapped Nora Price in front, Dunecrest were no longer building from a platform, they were trying to rebuild around Clara again and again. That became the core rhythm of the innings. Clara would find a scoring option, Harborlight would squeeze the next batter, and just as the partnership began to look useful, another wicket would arrive. Paige Dawson’s support was probably the best of the middle phase, not loud but necessary, and her stand with Clara took Dunecrest to seventy-nine. For a few overs that was the one passage where the batting side could imagine a total in the one-thirties if the last five overs opened cleanly. Mia Rowan ended that thought by bowling Clara Wells for thirty-nine, and that was the most important first-innings wicket because it removed the only batter who had connected the innings from the powerplay into the death. From that point, Dunecrest were left with fragments. Paige Dawson fell to Ella Brook. Brooke Lane came and went to Mia Rowan. Tessa Cole made eight but could not turn that stay into a late push. Eden Scott was run out just when the tail needed any sort of calm, and although Claire Vaughn and Sadie Grant scratched five more from the final few balls, one hundred and eleven for eight still felt light for a side that had chosen to bat first. Harborlight’s bowling was strong precisely because it was layered. Ruby Hale damaged the top order, Ella Brook gave four very tight overs and removed two middle-order batters, Mia Rowan took the key wicket of Clara and added another, Sophie Lane found the lbw that kept the score from settling, and Lucy Hayes’ equivalent here in control was Ella and the surrounding squeeze rather than one miracle spell. The economy pressure mattered as much as the wickets. Harborlight did not need Dunecrest to collapse in one dramatic over because they kept making them restart every two or three overs. The chase of one hundred and twelve could still have become awkward if an early wicket had arrived, because small targets often create their own kind of tension, but Lina Vale and Nia Parker never allowed Dunecrest that opening. Nia started with the cleaner tempo, pushing into gaps, taking the loose ball through the off side, and making sure the first few overs did not become a waiting game. Lina Vale was the more complete batter and the one who steadily took ownership of the chase. She did not force the pace unnecessarily, which was the clever part. She saw that Dunecrest had only a modest total to defend and refused to give them the one thing they needed most, a careless wicket that might make the target feel larger. The stand grew through accumulation first and then through increasingly confident boundary placement, Lina moving into her cuts and drives with more certainty as the ball softened, Nia holding the other end with exactly the right amount of activity. Dunecrest’s bowlers had brief moments of containment. Tessa Cole’s full spell kept the chase from becoming a complete rush, Faith Monroe found a few tidy overs, and Brooke Lane did not start badly, but without a wicket there was no way to convert containment into pressure. Nora Price and Paige Dawson leaked just enough to make the target shrink faster than Dunecrest wanted, and the extras did not help either, because nine free runs in a chase of this size quietly remove a lot of scoreboard stress. By the time Lina Vale moved past fifty, the contest had already lost its real edge. Nia Parker was still there, still unbeaten, still rotating well enough that Dunecrest could not bowl six balls at one batter and build a trap. Lina’s unbeaten sixty-seven from fifty-seven gave Harborlight the innings’ authority, while Nia’s thirty-eight not out from thirty-five gave it continuity, and together they turned the chase into one uninterrupted piece of work. Harborlight reached one hundred and fourteen without loss in the sixteenth over, and the ten-wicket margin accurately reflected the difference between the sides. Dunecrest had one serious batting hand in Clara Wells, a few supporting scraps from Paige Dawson, Tessa Cole, Claire Vaughn, and Sadie Grant, but no sustained second pillar. Harborlight had a complete bowling plan, then a complete opening partnership. That is why Lina Vale was the right player of the match: she did not merely top-score in the chase, she removed the only route Dunecrest had back into the game by refusing to let a low target become emotional. Harborlight won through discipline first and control second, and by the end the result felt less like a chase completed than a pressure match calmly flattened.
