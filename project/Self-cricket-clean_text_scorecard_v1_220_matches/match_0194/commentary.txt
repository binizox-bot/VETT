At Alderbrook Oval in Westgate, Bayport won the toss, chose to bat, and built the match around one enormous innings that made every later phase easier for them and every mistake by Cresthill more expensive. The start was already useful rather than cautious. Samir Vale, the wicketkeeper, played the cleaner support hand in the opening stand, taking boundaries when the ball was full and helping Bayport move to fifty-five before he was caught from Jasper Hill’s bowling. The stand also carried eight extras, which mattered because Cresthill were not simply being hit; they were giving Bayport free movement before the innings had properly settled. Finn Mercer was already looking composed at the other end, and once Caleb Rowe joined him, the innings found its most controlled early-middle passage. Caleb’s twenty-five from forty-seven was not loud, but it was exactly the stabilizing hand Finn needed after the first wicket. Their seventy-run partnership moved Bayport from a strong start into a platform, and because Finn kept scoring through that phase rather than merely occupying, Cresthill were forced to defend much earlier than they wanted. Caleb eventually fell at one hundred and twenty-five, but the innings did not reset. Arlo Bennett came in and struck a short, sharp seventeen, Noah Pike stayed long enough for another stand, Mason Hart and Dylan Shore added small pieces, and through all those changes Finn Mercer remained the player who kept the innings in one line. His hundred was not a separate event from the match; it was the match’s structure. He scored with Samir, then Caleb, then through the captain’s cameo, then through the middle order, and by the time he was finally removed for one hundred and forty-five, Bayport had already reached two hundred and fifty-nine. That dismissal gave Cresthill their best late sequence. Noah Lane, expensive earlier, suddenly became the main wicket-taker and ran through the lower order, taking Finn, Dylan Shore, Leo Grant, Ben Carter, and finally Owen Hale. His five-wicket return kept Bayport from stretching beyond three hundred, and Milo Grant’s three wickets had also slowed parts of the middle. But those wickets arrived too late to change the broader shape. Cresthill had already conceded too many extras, too many boundary phases, and too much of Finn Mercer. Owen Hale’s sixteen, Leo Grant’s eight, and Evan Wells’ not-out single were enough to drag Bayport to two hundred and eighty-one all out in the forty-sixth over. The innings was not perfectly clean, especially after Finn fell, but the main damage had long been done: one opener had carried the side through seven wickets and made a total that looked far beyond what Cresthill’s batting later proved capable of reaching. Chasing two hundred and eighty-two, Cresthill needed a long top-order innings immediately. They got the opposite. Jasper Hill was trapped in front first ball by Caleb Rowe, and the chase was thrown into survival mode before it had even begun. Carter Wells and Milo Grant scratched out a small repair, but Ben Carter removed Carter with only five on the board, then had Milo caught by Finn Mercer at fifteen. Aaron Pike fell without adding anything, Theo Finch bowled him and then Liam Frost, and suddenly Cresthill were nineteen for five with no batter having taken control of even a small passage. Noah Lane and Rafi Stone produced one of the few bits of resistance, but again the scale was tiny. Their stand was worth nine, and when Noah was caught from Caleb Rowe’s bowling, Cresthill were twenty-eight for six and the chase had fully disappeared. Arun West and Rafi Stone then made the largest partnership of the innings, only eleven, which says everything about the batting card. Arun’s six was useful only in the sense that it delayed the end. Rafi Stone’s eleven became the top score because he simply stayed longer than most of the others. Theo Finch removed Owen Reed and then Rafi, Caleb Moss remained unbeaten on eight, and Evan Wells finished the innings by bowling Rohan Vale at fifty-five. Bayport’s bowling performance was not about one bowler alone. Caleb Rowe cut into the top and middle, Ben Carter broke the early rebuild, Theo Finch produced the most complete spell with four wickets for eighteen, and Evan Wells closed the card. Owen Miles went wicketless but kept the pressure tight enough that Cresthill never found a release phase. The final margin of two hundred and twenty-six runs therefore feels fully consistent with the cricket. Bayport had a match-owning innings from Finn Mercer, useful early support, enough lower-order movement to pass two hundred and eighty, and a bowling unit that never allowed Cresthill a stand above eleven. Cresthill had moments with the ball through Noah Lane and Milo Grant, but no batting reply to match the scale of the target. Finn Mercer was the clear Player of the Match because his one hundred and forty-five did not just top-score; it gave Bayport the entire strategic shape of the match and turned the second innings into a defense from overwhelming strength.
