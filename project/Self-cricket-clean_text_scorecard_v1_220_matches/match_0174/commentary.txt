At Westhaven Youth Cricket Ground in Meridian, Ridgefield against Coastline played out like one of those fifty-over youth matches where the early score almost lies to you, because Ridgefield won the toss, chose to bat, and for a long opening stretch seemed to have built enough calm to make the innings work, only for Coastline’s bowlers to keep peeling away the structure until a useful platform turned into a small target and a chase that briefly looked nervous became controlled by one decisive middle partnership. Owen Hart and Leo Mercer began with the right idea for the format, not rushing the new ball, not trying to force a high tempo on a surface that was clearly rewarding straight bowling and patience, and their stand of 52 gave Ridgefield the kind of first act a batting side wants after electing to set a score. Owen was the proper hand in that partnership, moving with more confidence, finding the rope when anything sat up, and playing with enough shape to suggest he might carry the innings deep. Leo was far more restrained, making 14 from 50, but his occupation still mattered because Ridgefield were at least keeping wickets intact. Then the match changed. Ethan Brooks removed Leo, Noah Reed quickly cut off Carter Shaw and Ben Porter, and from 52 without loss the innings slid into a sequence of restarts that never became a second platform. Owen tried to keep the card alive, reaching 46 from 74, but Aaron Vale got through him at 69 and that was the hinge of the first innings, because the one batter who had understood the day was gone before the score had truly become safe. Gavin Lane followed without moving the total, Alec Stone’s little boundary hand ended almost as soon as it began, Adrian Vale was bowled, Nolan Reed was taken by the same bowler, and Isaac Ford could not give Jason Brook the support he needed. Jason’s 27 from 42 was valuable in the lower order because it dragged Ridgefield from the edge of embarrassment toward something at least defendable, and the last stand with Aiden Frost gave them a few more runs before Ethan Brooks finished it, but 112 all out in 40.4 overs was still well short of what the opening stand had promised. Coastline’s bowling was not built on one flashy over alone. Mason Cross and Noah Reed broke the first wave of rebuilding, Ethan Brooks struck at the start and end of the innings, and Aaron Vale’s 10 overs for 11 with four wickets gave the whole card its decisive shape. His spell mattered because it removed Owen Hart and then closed down the tail before Ridgefield could crawl into the 130s, which is why the player-of-the-match call fits the match rather than just the scorebook. Chasing 113, Coastline began quickly enough to suggest a routine finish, with Miles Kane playing the most positive early shots in a 32-run opening stand, but Sandgate-style pressure appeared for a moment when Ben Porter changed the tone. Miles fell first, Logan Pierce was bowled soon after by Carter Shaw, and Ethan Brooks was caught with the score only 43, so Coastline were three down before the chase had fully settled. That was Ridgefield’s best chance, and for a few overs the small target did begin to feel a little larger. Cole Grant and Tyler Brook removed that tension by batting the situation rather than the scoreboard. Cole was the more assured player, making 43 from 40 with five fours and a six, and he understood that Coastline did not need a reckless counterattack; they needed one batter to hold the chase together while the target kept shrinking. Tyler Brook was quieter but essential, staying unbeaten on 20 from 36 and refusing to let Ben Porter’s spell become a real collapse. Their fourth-wicket stand of 60 took Coastline from 43 for 3 to 103 for 4, and by the time Cole finally fell, the game had already been reduced to a handful of calm runs rather than a dangerous finish. Aaron Vale then joined Tyler and, fittingly, helped close the match he had defined with the ball, finishing unbeaten as Coastline reached 114 for 4 in 22.5 overs. Ridgefield had one genuine innings from Owen, a small lower-order fight from Jason, and a strong defensive burst from Ben Porter, but they never recovered from the collapse that followed their opening stand. Coastline were sharper where the match actually turned: Aaron Vale broke the first innings, Cole Grant rebuilt the chase, and Tyler Brook stayed long enough to make sure the early wobble did not become a final twist. The result therefore lands cleanly: Ridgefield 112 all out after wasting a stable start, Coastline 114 for 4 after one brief scare and one mature partnership, a six-wicket win built through bowling control first and chase management second.
