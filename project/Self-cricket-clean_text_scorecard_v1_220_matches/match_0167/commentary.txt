At Cedarbank Cricket Oval in Westmoor, Bayport Women ended up defending a total that looked neither huge nor fragile at the halfway stage, and that is why this match worked as a proper pressure game rather than a simple one-sided card, because Eastvale Women chose to field, found real wickets through Reese Bennett, and still could not stop one anchor innings from giving Bayport enough structure to control the chase later. Bayport’s start was uneasy rather than disastrous. Mina Brooks and Mara Vale put on a useful opening stand of 25, with Mina taking the small early scoring role and Mara already looking like the batter prepared to bat through the awkward overs, but Reese Bennett broke that start by removing Mina, then almost immediately took Chloe Ray as well, and at 27 for 2 the innings had lost its early cover. That was the first important pressure point. Mara Vale did not answer it with reckless hitting. She answered it by staying in the match. Sofia Grant gave her the first proper repair stand, adding 43 with three extras, and although Sofia’s 14 from 19 was not explosive, it gave the innings a shape again while Mara moved into the role that would decide the first half. Freya Wood bowled Sofia at 70, Skye Dawson later had Lila Mercer stumped, and when Mara herself finally fell for 50 from 46 at 88 for 5, Eastvale had every reason to believe the total could still be dragged down toward something very chaseable. Mara’s innings, though, had already done the core work: five boundaries, one six, and more importantly enough time at the crease to stop the innings from becoming a collapse after Reese Bennett’s early strikes. Bayport then found the smaller late contributions that often matter more than they look in youth T20s. Eva Stone’s 21 from 14 brought the tempo Bayport needed after the anchor departed, Nina Cross added 9 in the rotating role, Ruby Cole stayed there at the finish, and Zoe Pike’s last-ball boundary pushed the total to 124 for 7. That final nudge mattered because Bayport had been 88 for 5 and could easily have ended twenty runs lighter. Eastvale’s bowling was strong in pieces. Reese Bennett’s 4 for 33 was the standout threat, Skye Dawson’s two wickets came from a spell that kept asking the batters to reach, Erin Blake gave four very tight overs for only 14, and Freya Wood struck at a useful moment. The problem was that Eastvale never quite removed both forms of resistance at once: when Mara was anchoring, someone stayed with her; when Mara finally went, Eva supplied the late push. Chasing 125, Eastvale needed one calm top-order hand and one proper middle partnership, but the innings kept breaking just before either could become decisive. Grace Nolan fell early to Ruby Cole, Ella Cross and Amelia Shaw added 19 to calm the first few overs, and Lucy Harper then joined Amelia for the best passage of the chase, a stand of 22 that briefly made the target feel playable. Amelia Shaw’s 19 from 25 was not fluent, but it gave Eastvale time. Lucy Harper looked the most likely to make the chase real, reaching 25 from 31 and holding the middle together after Amelia was caught off Holly Reed at 48. Yet Bayport kept finding the key interruption. Holly Reed had Isla Morgan stumped by Mina Brooks, Nina Cross bowled Lucy Harper at 69 for 5, and that wicket was the real breaking point because it removed the one batter who still had enough time and rhythm to drag Eastvale into the final overs with pressure attached. After that, Eastvale’s chase became a series of fragments. Mia Walsh was bowled by Ivy Turner, Freya Wood went hit wicket to Nina Cross, Poppy Lane was run out by Nina, Skye Dawson was run out by Mara Vale, and Erin Blake was stumped at the end as Eastvale were dismissed for 87 in 19.2 overs. The closing pattern says a lot about Bayport’s discipline. They were not relying on one bowler only. Holly Reed took the two wickets that broke the middle. Ivy Turner’s 2 for 13 kept the chase from settling. Ruby Cole gave the first breakthrough. Nina Cross finished with 3 for 18 and controlled the endgame. Mara Vale, after making the half-century, still contributed with three tight overs and the run-out of Skye Dawson, which gave her player-of-the-match case even more weight. Eastvale had moments through Reese Bennett with the ball and through Lucy Harper plus Amelia Shaw in the chase, but they never owned a long enough phase. Bayport did. Mara Vale’s 50 gave them a score, Eva Stone’s late hand made that score defensible, and the bowlers then turned 124 into a much heavier target than it looked at the interval. That is why the 37-run margin felt earned: Bayport had the one complete batting innings, the sharper middle-overs defense, and the cleaner response every time Eastvale looked ready to build.
