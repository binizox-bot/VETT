Eastmoor won the toss at Red Mesa Ground in Eastmere and chose to bat, and for most of the first half of the innings that decision looked much more fragile than confident, because Northbridge attacked the new ball with enough discipline and enough variation to keep the batting side from ever settling into a clean powerplay rhythm. Ethan Vale was gone almost immediately, caught off Timothy Young, and that early wicket placed Eastmoor into a defensive shape before they had found any real pace. Kieran Moore tried to hold one end with a cautious thirteen, and Mason Clarke briefly gave the innings its first pulse with twenty-three from fourteen balls, three boundaries, and the sort of intent that suggested Eastmoor might still turn the early damage into a usable platform. Mark Owen cut that off by bowling Mason, and from there the innings began to fray quickly. Kieran followed soon after, Nolan Hayes could not turn his stay into momentum, and Owen Parker was trapped in front. At fifty-five for five, Eastmoor were no longer thinking about a strong total. They were trying to find a way to bat out the overs without being left with something plainly inadequate. That is where Caleb Grant took control of the match. His innings did not begin as a finishing assault. It began as rescue work. He needed to stay, read the pace, and allow the innings to stop falling apart. Dylan Hart gave him the first proper support, and their stand of forty-five was the section that changed Eastmoor’s day from collapse management into recovery. Dylan’s nineteen from eighteen was practical and valuable, giving Caleb enough company to bat with shape rather than desperation. Caleb, meanwhile, moved gradually from survival into command, first collecting singles, then picking off the loose ball, and finally trusting his hands once the field began to spread. Northbridge still kept taking wickets. Ian Mercer removed Dylan, Mark Owen added Connor Webb, and at one hundred and twelve for seven Eastmoor were again in danger of wasting the recovery. Isaac Rowe’s late cameo was therefore important. His unbeaten eight from two balls gave Caleb just enough late support, and Caleb himself finished the innings with the authority of a batter who had carried the hardest overs and still had enough left for the close. His unbeaten sixty-seven from forty-seven, with six fours and three sixes, was the clear center of the total. Eastmoor closed on one hundred and fifty for seven, a score that looked modest in isolation but much stronger once you remembered they had been five down before reaching sixty. Northbridge’s bowlers had done plenty right. Mark Owen’s three wickets nearly broke the innings open. Timothy Young and Anthony Reed struck around the top. Ian Mercer and Edward Roberts both made useful interventions. Yet they could not remove Caleb, and that single failure changed the match. Northbridge’s chase began with enough intent to make the target feel reachable. Brian Perez fell early to Aiden Brooks, but Ronald Scott batted with far more fluency than most players on either side had shown, finding six boundaries in thirty-one and giving the innings a real first push. Gary Vincent supported briefly, and even though Dylan Hart removed Ronald and then Gary in quick succession, Northbridge had still moved far enough through the powerplay to remain well inside the chase. Ian Mercer and Edward Roberts then formed the stand that most threatened Eastmoor. Their forty-one-run partnership was the cleanest rebuilding phase of the reply, Ian giving it structure and Edward bringing the more assertive scoring. Edward’s thirty-eight from twenty-nine was the innings that made Northbridge believe the chase could be controlled rather than merely survived. When Ethan Vale had Ian stumped at eighty-eight, Eastmoor had found a necessary break, and Calvin Moore made that break more meaningful by removing Jason Underwood almost immediately. Still, Edward was there, and Henry Scott’s arrival kept Northbridge dangerous. Henry made sixteen from twelve, struck cleanly, and their stand of thirty gave the chase another serious life. At one hundred and twenty-one for six, the game was tight enough that one more over of clean batting could have pushed Eastmoor into real trouble. Ethan Vale removed Henry, and then Calvin Moore struck the decisive blow by having Edward caught. That wicket at one hundred and twenty-four for seven changed everything. Northbridge no longer had the set batter who could manage the last stretch. Kevin Price and Mark Owen could not hold the tail together, Isaac Rowe removed Mark, and although Timothy Young hit one late six to keep a faint noise in the chase, the structure had gone. Kevin fell, Anthony Reed was left with too much to do, and Ethan Vale bowled Timothy to end the innings at one hundred and thirty-seven in the twentieth over. Eastmoor won by thirteen runs, and the result fits the match’s inner shape. They were not the cleaner side for every phase. They collapsed early, recovered through one batter, then had to fight through a chase that twice looked alive. Northbridge had Ronald Scott’s early fluency, the Ian-Edward stand, and Henry Scott’s late support, but they never produced the completed innings that Caleb Grant had given Eastmoor. That is why Caleb was the correct Player of the Match. His sixty-seven not out was not just the highest score; it was the reason Eastmoor had a defendable target at all. Dylan Hart’s early chase wickets, Ethan Vale’s three late strikes, Calvin Moore’s removal of Edward, and Isaac Rowe’s lower-order wickets all mattered, but every part of the defence was made possible by Caleb’s rescue. Eastmoor won because they survived the worst phase of the match, then made Northbridge face pressure in every over of the chase until the last set batter was gone.
