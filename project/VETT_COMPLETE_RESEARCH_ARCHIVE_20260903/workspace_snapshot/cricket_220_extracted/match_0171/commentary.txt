From Meridian Youth Cricket Ground in Westport, this match match between Bayridge and Coastal Rangers turned into exactly the sort of low-scoring fifty-over contest where the number on the board never looked huge, yet every small mistake made the chase heavier, and that is why Coastal’s 146 all out eventually became enough despite Bayridge spending the first part of the reply looking as though they had the match under control. Coastal Rangers chose to bat first, and their innings never really became fluent, but Leo Hart gave it the one innings of genuine shape it badly needed. Oliver Reed helped him through the opening spell, making 14 from 35, and the first stand of 46 was patient rather than forceful, helped by a few extras but still useful because it kept Coastal from being opened up immediately. Nathan Vale broke that stand by removing Oliver, and Bayridge then did good work through Isaac Marsh and Carter Blake to make sure Coastal never turned the early base into something large. Evan Cole batted a long while for 17, but Isaac Marsh bowled him at 84 for 2, and when Leo Hart himself fell for 51 at 96 for 3, caught by Kai Brooks off Nathan Vale, the innings was suddenly in a delicate place. That wicket mattered because Leo had been the only batter who seemed fully able to read the pace of the surface and build without panic. After him, Coastal had to live on fragments. Mason Lane made 13, Theo Cross barely settled, Caleb Stone went without scoring, Jonah Price offered 9, Rylan Moss worked 10, and only a couple of late nicks from Owen Shaw, Dylan Frost and Logan Vale pushed the total from the low 120s into the mid-140s. Bayridge’s bowling was strong enough to deserve credit. Isaac Marsh’s 4 wickets from 10 overs were the cleanest spell of the innings, Carter Blake also took 3, Nathan Vale struck twice, and Eli Ford added a useful lbw. Coastal were bowled out in the forty-fourth over, and at the break 146 looked like a score that could be chased with basic discipline. The catch, of course, was that basic discipline became the one thing Bayridge could not sustain long enough. Kai Brooks and Isaac Marsh began cautiously but sensibly, adding 51 for the first wicket, and that stand should have been enough to settle the chase. Kai made 34, Isaac’s 12 from 38 was slow but functional, and with 21 extras eventually folded into the innings, Bayridge were being given enough help to make the target feel smaller. Then the first major mistake arrived, Isaac run out by Jonah Price, and the chase lost a little of its calm. Miles Carter came and went for 4, caught behind off Logan Frost, and when Dylan Frost bowled Kai at 68, the innings changed from controlled pursuit into repeated repair work. Ryan Vale and Ari Blake briefly steadied things, taking the score to 91, but Owen Shaw removed Ryan, and then the middle began to fracture. Ben Carter was run out at 99, Eli Ford was run out two deliveries later in scoring terms, and suddenly a chase that had once been 51 without loss had become 101 for 6. Ari Blake tried to hold it together with the lower order, and in many ways his 26 from 61 became the last thread of Bayridge’s chase. Carter Blake helped him for a while, Miles Shaw stayed long enough to push the score to 127, and Nathan Vale survived a few balls, but Coastal kept finding the right interruption just before Bayridge could truly settle. Caleb Stone removed Carter Blake, Jonah Price dismissed Miles Shaw, Logan Frost bowled Nathan Vale, and the match reached its final stage with Bayridge still close enough to believe but too damaged to feel safe. Zane Porter’s unbeaten 8 kept the last pair moving, but Leo Hart returned with the ball and bowled Ari Blake at 140, sealing a 6-run win and completing the kind of captain’s match that deserved the award. Coastal did not win because they dominated every hour. They won because they had the best batting hand of the day in Leo’s 51, enough disciplined support from Dylan Frost, Owen Shaw, Caleb Stone, Jonah Price and Logan Frost with the ball, and sharper fielding at the moments Bayridge most needed composure. Bayridge had the stronger opening position in the chase, and Isaac Marsh’s first-innings spell should have set them up to finish the job, but the run-outs and middle-order stalls changed the entire emotional direction of the match. Coastal Rangers 146 all out, Bayridge 140 all out, and a narrow defense that felt messy, tense, and fully earned because the side defending the smaller-looking score stayed clearer in the moments where the chase became nervous.
