Live from Windward Academy Oval in Mariston, this Meridian A against Valleyforge A match had the shape of a chase that kept pretending it had already been solved, then kept reopening just enough to make the finish feel properly alive, because Meridian’s decision to bat first produced a strong score without producing the kind of complete innings that would have truly buried the side chasing. Nikhil was strong square of the wicket, quick onto width, and his thirty-eight from twenty-four had five boundaries and a six without ever looking frantic. Theo was sharper, more vertical in his hitting, and his thirty-nine from twenty-one carried the heavier early threat, especially once he started lifting anything too full into the leg-side spaces. Their stand of seventy-six had only two extras inside it, which matters because it was not built on messy bowling help. It was built on batters taking control. Reece Foster finally broke it by removing Nikhil, and Luca Bennett followed with Theo soon after, and that two-wicket sequence changed Meridian’s innings from launch mode into construction mode. Mason Vale had to play a captain’s hand from there. He never made the loudest score of the innings, and he never quite accelerated into a fully dominant finish, but his thirty-seven from thirty-three was the thread that held the middle together after the openers had gone. Corin Hayes gave him a useful twenty-three, and that partnership took Meridian to one-twenty-one before Reece Foster came back through the middle and bowled Corin. That wicket was important because Meridian had been threatening to rebuild one more time around two set batters, and Valleyforge needed the next interruption before the total reached the death overs with too much batting still intact. Luca Park came and went cheaply, and for a moment the innings looked as though it might settle around the low one-sixties. Caleb Grant removed Mason at one-sixty-five, and after that the final ten balls became messy, fast, and just about productive enough. Dylan Shore was run out after a short punchy seven, Rian fell at one-seventy-six, Owen Vale was also run out at the same score, and Meridian had briefly lost three wickets while barely moving. That could have left them stranded short of where the innings had promised to go. Noah Grant and Caleb Shaw prevented that. Valleyforge’s bowling card told the truth of a recovery effort. Reece Foster’s three for twenty-four was the decisive spell because he broke the opening stand, removed Corin when the innings was rebuilding, and later cut out Rian before the finish could explode. Caleb Grant was expensive but still took Mason Vale. Luca Bennett and Isaac Turner both found important single wickets. The wider point was simple: Valleyforge lost the first phase badly, then won just enough of the middle and late phases to keep the target in reach. Chasing one-eighty-eight, Valleyforge started with Aiden Moss hitting as though the number did not intimidate him at all. His nineteen from ten included two early sixes, and although Blake Sutton removed him at twenty-two, the target had already been made to feel playable. At that point the chase looked healthy. Then it nearly broke. Mason Cole was pinned lbw almost immediately, Connor followed at seventy-six, and suddenly Valleyforge had gone from one down and controlled to four down and exposed. That was the match’s pressure pocket. Meridian had the field up, the noise was back in the game, and the chase needed someone to take ownership before the asking rate and the wicket column started working together. Milo Hart did exactly that. His innings was not a straight-line assault. It was a staged rescue. First he steadied with Noah Pierce, whose twenty-two from sixteen was much more valuable than the raw number because he gave Milo a functioning partner after the collapse. Milo became more assertive once Luca settled, using the pace better, picking gaps when the field spread, and then lifting the ball when Meridian tried to buy a quiet over with fuller length. Luca’s dismissal at one-seventy-five reopened a small door, especially with the target not yet fully erased, but Milo never let that moment turn into panic. Owen Price only needed a small finishing role, and he played it cleanly. Milo closed unbeaten on fifty-five from thirty-three, with four fours and two sixes, and Valleyforge crossed to one-eighty-eight for six with two balls left. Meridian had strong defensive moments. Blake Sutton’s three for twenty-six was excellent and repeatedly brought the contest back, especially when he removed Aiden early, Noah in the middle, and Luca at the end. Cole Martin was tidy, Jordan Knox and Tyler Rowan both struck during the collapse that made it seventy-six for four, and for a few overs Meridian looked capable of defending a total that had felt slightly short of domination but still very strong. The problem was that they never removed Milo Hart. That is the whole result in its simplest form. Meridian had the bigger first-innings platform through Nikhil and Theo, then enough middle-order work from Mason and Rian to reach a demanding one-eighty-seven. Valleyforge had the wobble that could have lost the chase, then the one batter who converted the wobble into a manageable finish. Milo Hart was the right player of the match because his innings connected every difficult part of the reply: early control after Aiden’s dismissal, crisis management after seventy-six for four, partnership rebuilding with Noah, acceleration with Luca, and the final calm with Owen Price. Meridian played a strong game in fragments. Valleyforge won the decisive stretch more completely, and that is why a match that swung hard through the middle still ended with Valleyforge getting home by four wickets.
