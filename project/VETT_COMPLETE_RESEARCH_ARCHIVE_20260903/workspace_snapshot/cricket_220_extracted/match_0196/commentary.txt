At Pinehurst School Oval in Eastmere, Stonebay won the toss and chose to bat, and what followed was one of those long, airless low-score innings where the batting side did not disappear in a reckless rush but instead spent nearly thirty overs being squeezed into smaller and smaller scoring options. Owen Vale and Liam Archer survived the opening spell for a while, yet survival never became control. Owen made only three from twenty-one balls, Liam could barely move the score at the other end, and even the opening stand of six needed three extras to reach that point. Noah Pike made the first cut by bowling Owen, and then he kept the same straight, patient pressure on the next pair. Kai Stone briefly stayed with Liam, but the score only crept to ten before Kai fell, and Noah Frost followed without scoring. At ten for three, Stonebay were already in a position where every new batter was entering not to build an innings but to stop the card from breaking completely. Liam Archer tried to hold that line for a little longer, but Arjun Blake’s arrival changed the match from a slow squeeze into a proper collapse. He removed Liam for four, then bowled Ethan Dale, and suddenly the middle order had no rhythm, no release, and no batter capable of changing the pace of the innings. Lucas Reed was taken out by Caleb Ross, Dylan Shore was caught and bowled by Arjun, and Mason Brook’s small stay ended the same way the rest of the innings seemed destined to end, with the ball attacking the stumps and Stonebay unable to get away. Carter Wells and Ben Hale produced the largest stand of the innings, but even that was only eleven, and eight of those runs were extras. That is the detail that explains the whole first innings. Stonebay were not slowly building hidden value; they were being kept alive by wides and the occasional mistake from the fielding side. Carter faced thirty-one balls for four, Ben stayed unbeaten on three from thirty-five, and that pair’s time in the middle merely delayed the finish rather than changing the match. Arjun eventually bowled Carter, then trapped Hugo Price to close the innings at thirty-six all out in the thirtieth over. The numbers beneath the score are brutal. Stonebay made only twenty runs from the bat. Extras made sixteen. No batter passed four. The fall of wickets went six, ten, ten, fifteen, sixteen, eighteen, eighteen, twenty-three, thirty-four, and thirty-six, which means there was never a recovery phase, only increasingly stubborn fragments of resistance. Ashford’s bowling worked as a complete pressure system, but Arjun Blake was the obvious center of it. His six for five from seven overs and four balls was not just a striking figure; it was the spell that took the innings apart from the middle through the end. Noah Pike’s three early wickets created the conditions for that destruction, Ryan Vale gave almost nothing away, Caleb Ross bowled five overs for two runs and a wicket, and Mason Cole’s brief expensive spell was the only real looseness in an otherwise suffocating innings. Chasing thirty-seven, Ashford did not allow even the usual small-target awkwardness to appear. Evan Hart and Mason Voss came out with a clear plan: finish it quickly, but without giving Stonebay a symbolic wicket to celebrate. Evan played the louder hand, twenty not out from fifteen balls, three fours and a six, and he made the target shrink almost immediately whenever Stonebay missed their length. Mason Voss was calmer but still positive, making twelve not out from twelve and keeping the opening stand clean. Stonebay’s bowlers could not create a chance. Kai Stone’s two overs cost fourteen, Carter Wells could not turn his over into pressure, Ben Hale leaked nine, and Hugo Price’s short spell ended the match with Ashford already in full control. The chase closed at thirty-seven without loss in the fifth over, and the ten-wicket margin fit the day exactly. Stonebay had lasted a long time with the bat without ever making Ashford defend a meaningful score; Ashford had bowled with enough discipline to make scoring feel almost impossible, then chased with enough intent to stop the second innings from becoming even a minor contest. Arjun Blake was the correct Player of the Match because his six-wicket spell was the point at which Stonebay’s slow struggle became a complete defeat. Noah Pike started the damage, Caleb Ross and Ryan Vale reinforced the squeeze, and Evan Hart plus Mason Voss finished the result without interruption. By the end, the match had a very simple shape: Stonebay occupied time but not territory, Ashford owned both innings, and the scoreline reflected that control with total clarity.
