At Cedar Ridge Arena in Westmere, this reduced eighteen-over match began with Ironvale choosing to field, and for a decent stretch that call looked like it had cricketing sense behind it, because Lakehaven were never allowed the sort of frictionless start that lets a shortened innings become wild from the first over. Arden Cole and Blake Hayes did give Lakehaven a solid opening base, and Arden was clearly the more assertive of the two, using the early pace well enough to reach thirty-two from twenty-four with three fours and a six, while Blake worked more quietly from the other end, keeping the partnership stable and making sure the first wicket did not fall with the innings still half-asleep. Their stand of fifty-three gave Lakehaven the platform, but Theo Marsh changed the tone by bowling Arden at exactly the stage where the opening pair might have begun to stretch the innings into something far more dangerous. Carter Miles then arrived and played a useful little hand, twenty-one from seventeen, but Ironvale did not let that second phase run away either. Victor Shaw removed Blake Hayes, and then Theo Marsh struck again by getting Carter caught, which meant Lakehaven had moved from fifty-three without loss to eighty-seven for three without ever quite cashing in fully on the platform. Dylan Reed could not settle, and when Xavier Holt bowled him at ninety-six for four, the innings suddenly looked vulnerable to finishing short of what the format demanded. That is where Evan Brooks and Felix Grant produced the partnership that gave Lakehaven something genuinely defendable. Evan’s forty-one from twenty-four was the innings’ sharpest acceleration, three fours, two sixes, and a clear sense that he understood the score needed more than tidy accumulation. Felix played the supporting role with real efficiency, staying unbeaten on twenty-four from eleven and keeping the back end moving whenever Ironvale tried to crowd Evan. Their stand of fifty-two took the innings from a slightly awkward position into a proper target, and although Evan was run out by Parker Sloan in the final over, the damage had already been done. Lakehaven closed on one hundred and fifty-four for five, a total shaped by the opening base and then rescued by the Evan-Felix finish. Ironvale’s bowling had useful structure: Theo Marsh was the key operator with two wickets for twenty-one, Warren Chase kept things reasonably tight, Victor Shaw and Xavier Holt each removed a set phase, and Zach Dean’s expensive opening spell was partly balanced by the wickets that came later from others. Chasing one hundred and fifty-five, Ironvale suffered the sort of start that can make a medium chase feel instantly awkward. Noah’s thirty-five from twenty-one gave the chase its first real push, five fours and a six, while Parker supplied the steadier support and made sure Ironvale did not waste the powerplay repairing damage too slowly. Blake Hayes removed Noah, and Arden Cole later dismissed Parker, but by then Ironvale had built a functioning pursuit. Quentin Hale then became the middle-overs organizer. His thirty-one from twenty-three was not the largest innings of the chase, but it was structurally important because he kept replacing lost partners with another short stand. First he carried the innings with Parker, then with Riley Brooks, and Riley’s twenty-three from sixteen pushed the chase into a strong position before Hudson Clarke broke that stand. The game still had tension because Quentin then fell to Ian Foster at one hundred and twenty-eight, and Lakehaven could sense one more wicket might expose the tail under pressure. Sawyer Grant made sure the chase did not freeze. His twenty-two from twelve gave Ironvale the late hitting they required, and with Theo Marsh staying calm at the other end, the sixth-wicket stand moved the score to one hundred and forty-two before Sawyer was stumped. That dismissal briefly reopened the door. Ironvale still needed a clean finish, and Theo remained there with the match not fully done. Victor Shaw walked in and settled it with immediate force, taking nine from three balls and clearing the rope once to push Ironvale to one hundred and fifty-six for six in the eighteenth over. The winning margin by wickets looks comfortable enough, but the chase had been built through several narrow phases rather than one dominant innings: Noah’s top-order burst, Quentin’s middle control, Riley’s support, Sawyer’s late hitting, and Theo Marsh’s calm presence through the final overs. That is why Theo as Player of the Match makes sense even without the biggest batting number. His two wickets had checked Lakehaven at the exact moment their innings might have run beyond reach, and his not-out finish helped keep Ironvale composed when the chase had lost Quentin and Sawyer. Lakehaven had real contributions from Arden, Evan, Felix, and their bowlers kept finding interruptions, but Ironvale had more functioning phases across the match. They limited the first innings just enough, then chased in pieces until Victor’s final blow ended it.
