Good morning from Northport Academy Oval in Shorehaven, where Seacoast won the toss, chose to field, and then built their seven-wicket win through two very different kinds of control, first by cutting Highland Vale down almost every time a repair began to look usable, and later by turning a briefly awkward chase into a calm, almost clinical finish once Noah Bennett and Owen Parker found the stand that the first innings had never quite managed to protect for long enough. Highland Vale’s start had a little movement on the board without ever truly feeling safe. Logan Turner struck one early boundary and gave the opening overs a bit of life, but Dylan Price kept the line narrow enough that nothing looked free, and when Logan was taken behind for eight the innings immediately moved into that familiar youth-cricket space where one batter is trying to settle while the next is already being asked to repair. Arden Shaw became the early holder, not fluent in a dominating way, but good enough to keep the first phase from collapsing at once. Evan Scott tried to stay with him, though his seven came slowly and Connor Blake’s lbw broke that second stand before Highland Vale could claim any real platform. Isaac Ward, the captain, then lasted long enough to make the scorecard look heavier in time than in runs, but Ryan Cross removed him for one, and that was the first sign that Ryan was not merely going to collect wickets at the end of an innings already gone; he was going to shape the whole middle of it. Arden still remained, and for a short while with Julian Hart he looked capable of dragging Highland Vale back toward something structured, but Ryan struck again to remove Arden for twenty-four, and that left the batting side at fifty-two for four with its only settled top-order hand gone. Carter Wells was bowled by Mason Reed soon after, and at fifty-six for five Highland Vale were in a position where anything near respectability required a proper sixth-wicket rescue rather than a few loose boundaries. Julian Hart and Miles Quinn provided exactly that rescue, and their partnership was the only time in the innings when Highland Vale looked like they had found a coherent route through the bowling. Julian took the senior role, reaching fifty-four from eighty-two balls, and his innings mattered because it contained both patience and just enough scoring force to change the mood for a while, especially once he began lifting and driving with more certainty. Miles Quinn supported with thirty-three from sixty, not spectacular, not rushed, but valuable because he stopped Ryan Cross and Dylan Price from bowling at a single resistance point. Their stand of seventy carried the innings from a broken fifty-six for five to one hundred and twenty-six for six, and it briefly made the total feel as though it might stretch into genuinely awkward territory. That hope ended when Ryan Cross returned to remove Miles, and the timing of that wicket was the real hinge of the first innings. Tyler Dean then made only two, Jordan Ross was bowled without scoring, and Highland Vale again lost shape just when the recovery needed a second layer. Julian Hart was still there, but Dylan Price ended his innings with a caught-and-bowled dismissal at one hundred and forty-eight for nine, and after that only Cole Morgan and Zachary Price were left to drag the total slightly further. Their last stand added enough to take Highland Vale to one hundred and sixty-four, but Ryan Cross closed the innings by bowling Zachary and finishing with six wickets, the kind of spell that explains a match without needing exaggeration. He removed the captain, the early holder, the major support batter in the big rescue, two lower-order blockers, and the final wicket. Around him, Dylan Price was outstanding in control, ten overs for very little and two crucial wickets at the start and near the end; Connor Blake made the early repair harder; Mason Reed took the fifth wicket that pushed Highland Vale into crisis; Lucas Grant went wicketless but helped keep the rate from escaping. The innings therefore finished as a patchwork rather than a structure: Julian Hart and Miles Quinn gave it its only serious substance, Arden Shaw gave the top order some resistance, and the rest came in small pieces plus extras. Chasing one hundred and sixty-five, Seacoast had to handle one early scare before taking full command. Ethan Cole was caught and bowled by Carter Wells with only a few on the board, and Liam Hayes, after doing some patient work for eighteen, also fell to Carter, so at thirty for two Highland Vale could at least imagine that their total might begin to feel larger if one more wicket came quickly. That wicket never came in time. Noah Bennett had already begun to settle, and when Owen Parker joined him the chase changed from a contest of nerves into a contest of discipline, which Seacoast won comfortably. Their third-wicket partnership of one hundred and eleven was the match-winning passage, not just because it was large, but because it was perfectly shaped to the target. Noah was the more continuous hand, unbeaten on sixty-one, finding boundaries often enough to keep the field honest and rotating with enough calm that the scoreboard never froze. Owen Parker, as captain and wicketkeeper, gave the chase its visible authority, making fifty-two from fifty-four balls and matching Noah almost stride for stride through the middle phase. The stand did not feel like one batter dragging the other. It felt balanced, and that balance removed Highland Vale’s best defensive option. They could not squeeze one end. They could not wait for panic. They could not turn the chase into a single-batter risk. By the time Evan Scott finally had Owen caught at one hundred and forty-one, the result had already become formal rather than uncertain. Ryan Cross then walked in and finished the day in the style of a player already central to the match, hitting eighteen not out from seven balls with two fours and two sixes, turning the final stretch into a sharp closing statement rather than a slow drift. Noah remained unbeaten, Seacoast reached one hundred and sixty-five for three in the twenty-eighth over, and the seven-wicket result reflected the difference between the sides. Highland Vale had one major stand but not enough around it; Seacoast had one major stand and enough calm before and after it to make the chase feel clean. Carter Wells gave Highland Vale early hope with two wickets, and Evan Scott at least broke the big partnership late, but the bowling never found the cluster it needed after thirty for two. So the match belonged to Seacoast because they won both decisive hinges: Ryan Cross broke Highland Vale’s recovery before it could become a full innings, and Noah Bennett with Owen Parker broke Highland Vale’s defence before it could become a real chase. Ryan Cross was the right player of the match, not only for the six wickets, but because his late unbeaten cameo made sure the finish carried the same authority as his bowling spell.
