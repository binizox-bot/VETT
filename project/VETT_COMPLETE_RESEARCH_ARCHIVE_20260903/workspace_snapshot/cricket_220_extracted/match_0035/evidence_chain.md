| L1 | Direct extraction | Basic match facts can be read directly from the table and commentary. | Capeview won by 82 runs; Ethan Vale was Player of the Match; Capeview made 167 all out and Harborfield were all out for 85. |
| L1 | Key player identification | Main contributors remain identifiable without complex calculation. | Ethan Vale scored 46 and took four wickets; Lucas Grant made 31; Connor Wells added 23 from the lower order; Leo Turner top-scored for Harborfield with 29. |
| L2 | Cricket-rule arithmetic | Requires checking batting runs, extras, final totals, and victory margin. | Capeview batting runs add to 150 and extras add 17, giving 167; Harborfield batting runs add to 72 and extras add 13, giving 85; 167 minus 85 gives the 82-run margin. |
| L2 | Partnership and FOW consistency | Requires cumulative verification across partnerships and fall-of-wickets. | Capeview partnerships move through 3, 3, 5, 55, 79, 105, 118, 128, 145, and 167; Harborfield partnerships move through 27, 30, 36, 39, 43, 49, 66, 75, 76, and 85. |
| L2 | Bowling reconciliation | Requires understanding that byes and leg-byes are not charged to bowlers. | Harborfield bowlers concede 164 charged runs while Capeview total 167 because three runs are byes/leg-byes; Capeview bowlers concede 82 charged runs while Harborfield total 85 because three runs are leg-byes. |
| L2 | Ball-count consistency | Requires checking legal balls against innings length. | Capeview batters face 197 legal balls across 32.5 overs; Harborfield batters face 121 legal balls across 20.1 overs. |
| L3 | Commentary-hidden inference | Key facts are embedded through pressure, rhythm, and turning points instead of dense scoreboard repetition. | The text presents Capeview’s early collapse, Ethan Vale’s repair innings, the lower-order extension, and Harborfield’s chase breaking after Ben Carter’s run-out. |
| L3 | Narrative-to-table validation | Readers must connect descriptive phrases to table evidence. | “Three down before the innings had a shape,” “Lucas and Ethan rebuilt the first real platform,” and “Ethan’s spell kept striking whenever Harborfield tried to reset” map to FOW, partnerships, and bowling figures. |
