At Meridian Youth Cricket Ground in Westhaven, Crestview against Valley Hawks became the kind of fifty-over match where the innings length almost made the collapse feel slower and harsher, because Valley Hawks chose to bat first, did enough in the first ten overs to suggest they might build something patient, then lost control so completely after the first wicket that the rest of their afternoon turned into survival rather than construction. Mason Vale and Liam Ford gave Valley Hawks a workable start, putting on 41 with a few extras mixed in, and for a short while Mason looked capable of giving the innings a proper spine, driving cleanly enough for his 26 and making the early score feel more secure than the batting actually was. Liam was much more restricted, crawling to 9, but while the partnership lasted Valley Hawks still had wickets, time, and the outline of a fifty-over innings. Nolan Pierce changed that outline almost immediately. He removed Mason at 41, Arjun Wells trapped Owen Blake soon after, and then Nolan came back through the same early channel to bowl Caleb Price and later remove Liam Ford, so a side that had been 41 without loss became 47 for 4 with almost no real counterpunch. That was the decisive passage of the first innings. Tyler Stone then occupied for a very long time without ever finding any release, Ryan Brooks offered a small hand before being run out, and the middle overs became almost painfully dry because Crestview did not need magic balls; they only needed to keep the field tight and make Valley Hawks take risks they did not want to take. Jason Vale and Arjun Wells were especially important in that squeeze, giving away almost nothing, while Ethan Grant’s control and Dylan Hart’s later wickets made sure the lower order never turned time spent at the crease into proper runs. Dylan Reed’s unbeaten 15 at the end at least lifted the total from complete wreckage into the low nineties, and Noah Flint helped add the only meaningful late stand, but the card still closed at 92 all out in 42.2 overs, which told the real story: the first stand was useful, the rest was repeatedly interrupted, and only 80 runs came from the bat. Nolan Pierce’s 3 for 22 deserved the player-of-the-match weight because his wickets landed when Valley Hawks still had the chance to build; Dylan Hart’s 3 for 19 finished the innings off, Ethan Grant added two, and the collective discipline around them turned a possible low-scoring contest into something close to a foregone conclusion. Chasing 93, Crestview could have played carefully, taken the target in small pieces, and still won with plenty left. Arun Cole and Logan Reed chose something far more brutal. From the first over, they made clear that Valley Hawks would not be allowed even a symbolic defense. Arun was savage on anything short or overpitched, striking 58 not out from 29 balls with seven fours and three sixes, and the key to that innings was not only the pace but the refusal to let a small target create false caution. Logan Reed was the ideal partner, unbeaten on 31 from 21, sharp through the off side, quick to punish width, and active enough that Valley Hawks could not simply wait for Arun to make a mistake. The bowling gave them help as well, especially through wides, a no-ball, and a couple of leg-byes, but this was not a chase built mainly on gifts. It was a chase smashed open by batting intent. Luke Harris went early, Noah Flint lost control, Dylan Reed could not apply the pressure he had tried to create with the bat, and Connor Lane’s short spell only delayed the inevitable by a few balls. Crestview reached 96 without loss in 8.1 overs, and the ten-wicket result felt exactly right because they had won both halves in completely different ways: first by suffocating Valley Hawks after one stable opening stand, then by treating the chase as a chance to finish the match before any nerves could exist. Mason Vale’s 26 and Dylan Reed’s late resistance were the only real batting positives for Valley Hawks. For Crestview, Nolan Pierce broke the innings, Dylan Hart and Ethan Grant closed it, and Arun Cole plus Logan Reed made the second innings disappear. It was not just a win; it was a full-control performance disguised for the first ten overs as a potentially competitive match.
