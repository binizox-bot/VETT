Oliver was the one batter in the top order who looked capable of giving the card a little movement, finding four boundaries in a patient 24, while Ben’s 8 from 34 was more about resistance than pressure, and together they reached 28 before Adrian Voss broke the stand by having Ben caught by Dylan Vale. That wicket changed the tone sharply. Voss then took the return catch to remove Oliver soon after, bowled Isaac Lane before he could settle, and had Jonah Mills caught as well, so Sandridge slid from a modest but workable 28 for no loss to 36 for 4, and that was the phase that defined the match. Dylan Vale removed Carter, Ryan Cross picked off Aaron, Ethan Wells trapped Gavin, then bowled Aiden, and by the time Parker Dean was caught off Ryan Cross, Sandridge were all out for 96 in 42.2 overs. The innings had lasted a long time without ever becoming strong. Chasing 97, Northbridge did not make the reply completely clean, and that is why the result has a little texture rather than feeling like a simple walkover. Ethan Wells and Noah Finn began carefully, with Ethan making 13 before Ben Carter held his own return catch, and when Liam Hayes was bowled by Carter Wells after a useful 16, Northbridge were 51 for 2 and still needed to make sure a small target did not become awkward. Carter Wells kept asking those questions. He removed Mason Reed lbw, then later bowled Owen Parker, and his spell of 3 for 14 was easily Sandridge’s best resistance in the second innings. But the difference was Noah Finn. He did not panic after Ethan fell, did not chase the game after Liam’s wicket, and did not let the chase turn into a nervous sequence of new batters trying to finish it in one shot. His unbeaten 39 from 65 was a low-target anchor’s innings in the proper sense: careful when the bowlers were straight, willing to pick up boundaries when width appeared, and steady enough to keep the dressing room calm even as wickets fell around him. Liam Hayes gave the chase its best early support, Mason Reed and Owen Parker could not stay long enough to finish the job, but Caleb Grant arrived with the score already close and gave Noah the final bit of company required. Their unbroken stand carried Northbridge from 86 for 4 to 98 for 4 in 21.2 overs, sealing a six-wicket win. Sandridge’s bowling effort had one strong thread through Carter Wells and some useful control from Gavin Price, but the target was simply too small after the first innings collapse. Northbridge had won the game by making Sandridge work for every run, then by ensuring that one batter, Noah Finn, stayed in charge of the chase once it became slightly untidy. Adrian Voss was correctly named player of the match because his spell created the entire match condition: without that early four-wicket squeeze, Sandridge might have reached something awkward; with it, Northbridge were chasing a score they could absorb even through a few interruptions.
