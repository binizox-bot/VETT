At Willowbank Lakeside Ground in Eastport, Dunecrest Women chose to bat first and almost immediately found themselves in the sort of innings that never quite collapses all at once but keeps tightening until every run feels negotiated, because Cora Mills struck with the first ball and bowled Aria Frost before the batting side had even had a chance to test the pace of the surface, and that tiny scoreline wound at the start ended up shaping almost everything that followed. Nora Bell responded with the only real top-order counterpunch Dunecrest produced, three early boundaries in a brisk fifteen, sharp hands through the off side and no interest in letting Harborlight settle into a perfect new-ball squeeze, while Emma Price at the other end took the longer, more cautious route into the innings, reading length, waiting on anything too straight, and quickly becoming the batter around whom Dunecrest would have to build. The second-wicket stand moved the score to twenty-four, enough to calm the first-ball damage a little, but Isla Rowan removed Nora before that early burst could become a powerplay base, and from there Dunecrest were never allowed the luxury of two settled batters for long. Lucy Grant stayed for a long time without finding fluency, making only three from sixteen, and that partnership with Emma added runs on paper but never really changed the pressure, because Harborlight were happy to watch the overs disappear while the score crept rather than climbed. Mia Carter eventually ended Lucy’s struggle, and then Maya Cross came in, found one clean boundary, and was gone almost immediately to Grace Vale, caught and bowled, which meant the innings had moved from possible rebuild to another reset at forty-nine for four. Emma Price kept doing the one job Dunecrest absolutely needed her to do. She did not dominate, and she was never in the sort of position where domination made sense. She simply held the innings together, collecting five boundaries across her unbeaten forty-six, refusing to give Harborlight the central wicket too early, and trying to make small stands look larger through survival and careful placement. Eva Stone gave her a little time, Leah Ward gave almost none, and when Dunecrest slipped to sixty-eight for six, the innings was already leaning heavily on Emma’s ability to keep the final overs alive. Ivy Ross then produced the one later partnership that genuinely mattered, not a violent stand but a necessary one, sixteen from fifteen in a partnership worth twenty-five, and for a short passage Dunecrest at least looked capable of dragging the total into something that might make Harborlight bat with care. Grace Vale’s fielding ended that phase through a run-out, and Mia Carter then bowled Clara Shaw before the last over had fully opened, leaving Dunecrest at ninety-four for eight with only Emma and the tail trying to squeeze value from the finish. Paige Hunter’s unbeaten twelve from five was therefore more important than its size suggests, because with Emma nudging from the other end and three extras arriving late, the final stand added eighteen and lifted Dunecrest to one hundred and twelve for eight. It was still under par, but it was no longer completely empty. Harborlight’s bowling had been disciplined in layers: Cora Mills struck at the start and later removed Eva Stone, Isla Rowan cut out Nora Bell’s counterpunch, Grace Vale delivered the middle-overs squeeze and two wickets, Mia Carter removed the slow stand and the late tailender, while Ruby Hale and Zoe Collins gave the quieter overs that made Dunecrest feel they were always batting inside a narrow lane. Chasing one hundred and thirteen, Harborlight Women then played the reply with the calm cruelty of a side that understood a low target can become awkward only if you donate the first mistake. Lina Vale and Nia Parker gave Dunecrest nothing. Nia was the slightly sharper starter, using the width early and making sure the chase did not begin in a defensive crouch, while Lina settled into the controlling role, taking singles without panic, waiting for the bad ball, and refusing to let the required rate create any false urgency. That division of labor was the whole chase. Nia’s unbeaten forty-six from thirty-six kept the scoring bright enough that Dunecrest could not build pressure through dots, and her one six was especially useful because it broke the illusion that Harborlight were only going to nudge the chase home. Lina’s unbeaten sixty-one from fifty-two was the innings that settled the result, seven boundaries, no wasted risk, and a tempo that kept shrinking the target without ever opening the door to a wicket. Dunecrest tried a full spread of bowlers and had a few respectable fragments. Brooke Lane was tidy enough at the start, Tessa Cole completed her spell without the chase exploding, Faith Monroe briefly slowed the scoring, and Ivy Ross’s equivalent in the middle control came through the sort of lines that made Harborlight work for singles. But without a wicket, containment never became pressure. Paige Dawson and Nora Price leaked just enough to keep the pursuit comfortable, Claire Vaughn’s over did not produce the breakthrough Dunecrest needed, and Sadie Grant’s short closing spell arrived with the target already nearly gone. The extras also helped Harborlight, eight in all, quietly shaving away what little tension Dunecrest might have hoped to build. Still, this was not a chase won by gifts. It was won by structure. Lina and Nia added one hundred and fifteen without being separated, reached the target in the fifteenth over, and made the ten-wicket margin feel completely fair. Dunecrest had Emma Price’s lone anchoring hand, Nora Bell’s brief punch, Ivy Ross’s late support and Paige Hunter’s final spark, but they never created the second stable innings needed to defend a small total. Harborlight had the better bowling plan, the better fielding discipline, and then the one complete partnership of the match. Lina Vale was the correct player of the match because her unbeaten chase did not simply score most of the runs; it removed the only route Dunecrest had back into the contest by keeping the reply calm from first ball to last.
