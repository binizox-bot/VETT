At Brookside Training Oval in Eastmere, Tidehaven won the toss and chose to bat, but the decision was almost immediately swallowed by a first-innings collapse so severe that the match had already taken its permanent shape before the opening spell had finished. Noah Vance did not merely strike early; he stripped the innings of any chance to become normal. Harvey Lane was bowled before Tidehaven had a run on the board, Miles Sharp followed without scoring, and once Rohan Blake, the only top-order player to show even a small amount of timing, was caught behind for eight, the batting side had already been dragged into survival mode. Nolan Reed, the captain, tried to stand still and blunt the new ball, yet he could only crawl to a single before Noah hit the stumps again, and at twelve for four Tidehaven were no longer building an innings; they were just trying to avoid complete embarrassment. The most damaging part of that burst was the sequence rather than the raw figures alone. Noah removed the first four wickets, gave almost nothing through his six overs, and made every incoming batter start from panic rather than rhythm. Caleb Frost and Owen Marsh then produced the only stand that looked even faintly like resistance, and even that was not a real counterattack. It was a twelve-run pause, built mostly from defensive survival, the odd loose ball, and just enough help from extras to keep the card moving. Owen’s seven from twenty-eight became the second-best score of the innings, which says everything about how little control Tidehaven had, and when Arjun Wells bowled him, the innings went back to breaking apart. Dylan Pierce came and went without scoring. Caleb Frost, who had occupied twenty-three balls for only two, was trapped by Arjun. Theo Grant scratched out one before Elias Grant found the edge to Ben Cole. Arun Cole and Kian Stone pushed the score from thirty to thirty-five, but even that late movement leaned heavily on wides rather than clean batting, and Arjun returned to bowl Arun and then Vikram Holt, closing Tidehaven at thirty-six all out in the twenty-first over. The numbers are harsh, but the shape behind them is harsher. Tidehaven’s batters made only twenty-two runs between them. Extras contributed fourteen. No batter reached double figures. The largest partnership was only twelve. The fall of wickets moved from nothing for one to one for two, then eleven for three, twelve for four, and although there was a brief pause around Caleb and Owen, there was never a phase where Ridgefield had to rethink the field or move away from attack. Noah Vance gave Ridgefield the first and most important cut, four wickets for nine, three maidens, and the entire top order removed. Arjun Wells then made sure the innings did not limp into something awkward, taking five for fifteen and wiping out the lower middle and tail. Elias Grant’s small spell added the last piece of pressure, and Ben Cole’s overs gave no meaningful release. Chasing thirty-seven, Ridgefield did not need a complicated plan, and they played like a side that understood the match was already in their hands. Arlo Finch opened with immediate clarity, driving when Tidehaven overpitched and picking off three boundaries before the fielding side had settled into any defensive idea. Ben Cole gave the chase a brief flash as well, hitting seven from five, including one clean blow over the rope, before Owen Marsh at least gave Tidehaven a small moment by having him caught by Rohan Blake. That wicket made the score eighteen for one, but it never created tension because the target was already half gone and Arlo was still moving freely. Noah Vance came in after doing the damage with the ball and played the simplest possible finishing role, leaving what he could, taking the runs available, and letting the chase finish without any false drama. The second partnership added nineteen, helped by a messy batch of wides and no-balls, and Ridgefield crossed the line at thirty-seven for one in just three and a half overs. Tidehaven’s bowling reflected the same lack of control that had hurt their batting. Owen Marsh took the only wicket but still conceded nineteen from two overs. Arun Cole’s over leaked thirteen, mostly through poor control. Caleb Frost did not even complete a full over before the result was gone. So the match reads as a complete one-sided compression: Tidehaven’s innings was broken by Noah, sealed by Arjun, and never supported by the bat; Ridgefield’s chase then became a short confirmation rather than a separate contest. Arlo Finch finished unbeaten on nineteen, Noah stayed there at the end, and the nine-wicket margin accurately reflects how little resistance the target created. Noah Vance was the right Player of the Match because the game’s decisive act was his opening spell, not the chase. He removed the top order before Tidehaven had any structure, set up the thirty-six all out, and then calmly stood at the crease when Ridgefield completed the formalities.
