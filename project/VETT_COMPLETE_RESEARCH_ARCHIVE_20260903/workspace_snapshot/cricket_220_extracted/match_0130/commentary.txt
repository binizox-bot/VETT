At Riverside Turf - 2 in Eastport, Upland XI won the toss and chose to field, and for a short while that decision looked like it might give them exactly the kind of grip they wanted, because Marina XI’s innings never had the luxury of one clean, uninterrupted rise. Arman Cole survived the first little burst but was not the one setting the tone. Matteo Reed did that, and his brief stay gave Marina the early pulse they needed after being asked to bat. He struck twenty-six from fourteen, two of those cleanly over the rope, and for the first few overs Upland had to defend against a side that was not prepared to creep into the match. The opening stand reached thirty-nine before Aidan Frost had Matteo caught by Isaac Rowe, and that wicket changed the mood because it removed the batter who had given the powerplay its bite. Nolan Brooks then tightened the game even further by bowling Arman and later removing Leon Parker, so the first half of the innings moved from brisk beginning to controlled rebuild. That is where Noah Vale became important. His forty-six from forty balls was not a glamorous innings, and it was not meant to be. Marina needed someone to hold the innings together after the top order had been slowed, and Noah did that with the kind of measured captain’s hand that makes a total possible even when the fielding side keeps finding interruptions. Caleb Hays gave the stand its sharper edge. He was more willing to hit up and over, more willing to punish the slightly fuller ball, and his thirty-six from twenty-seven made sure the fourth-wicket partnership became more than simple repair. Their stand of seventy-two was the spine of the innings, carrying Marina from sixty-four for three to one hundred and thirty-six for four, and for a few overs it felt as though the batting side might still push well beyond a par score. Then the innings came apart at the back end. Noah was run out by Evan Stone. Caleb followed almost immediately to Aidan Frost. Ryan Ford gave one short swing and disappeared. Lucas Turner was run out. Owen Pierce and Connor Blake were both bowled by Cameron Hale in the final squeeze. Darren Vale and Tyler Grant squeezed out three at the end, but Marina had clearly left a few runs behind. From one hundred and thirty-six for three, they closed at one hundred and fifty-eight for nine, which felt competitive without ever feeling completely safe. Upland’s bowling effort had a split character. Nolan Brooks was the cleanest source of control, two for fourteen from four overs, and he did the heavy work of stopping Marina’s early innings from growing too freely. Aidan Frost was expensive but removed both Matteo and Caleb, two batters who had actually threatened to push the total higher. Cameron Hale’s final-over wickets gave the innings its ragged close. Jorden Cole struck once, while Blake Turner and Jacob Reed kept enough pressure in the middle to make Marina’s finishing job harder than it might have been. The chase, though, was where the match was really decided. Upland XI needed a calm start to make one hundred and fifty-nine feel manageable, and they received almost the opposite. Lucas Turner struck with the first ball, Aaron Clarke caught behind without scoring, and that immediately changed the emotional weight of the innings. Nolan Brooks and Isaac Rowe briefly countered, but Darren Vale then produced the spell that shaped the match. Isaac was bowled at fifteen, Nolan was bowled at twenty, and suddenly Upland were not chasing a target on their own terms. They were repairing damage. Connor Blake made it worse by trapping Evan Stone lbw, leaving Upland twenty-two for four, and at that point the pursuit looked much closer to collapse than contest. Jacob Reed and Logan Hays changed that. Their partnership of fifty-two was the only stretch of the chase in which Upland looked properly settled. Jacob’s twenty from thirty was slow, even heavy at times, but it mattered because somebody had to stop the wickets. Logan, by contrast, gave the innings real threat. His fifty-four from thirty-seven had three fours and four sixes, and he was the one batter who made Marina’s fielders start glancing at the scoreboard rather than simply attacking. Tyler Grant broke that stand by bowling Jacob, and that wicket mattered because it removed the stabilizer around Logan. Carter Ward then came in and struck sixteen from nine, including two sixes, and for a short phase Upland looked dangerous again. Tyler Grant answered again, having Carter caught by Leon Parker, and Marina had cut away another support act before it could become decisive. Still, Logan was there, and with Blake Turner he dragged the chase into the nineteenth over. Their stand of thirty-three was the last serious threat to Marina’s defence. Logan was already past fifty, Blake had begun to find rhythm, and the equation was no longer impossible. Owen Pierce then delivered the wicket Marina had been waiting for, Logan caught by Arman Cole at one hundred and thirty-two. Two balls later Cameron Hale was stumped by Caleb Hays off Owen, and the chase lost its final shape. Blake Turner stayed unbeaten, Aidan Frost added a few late runs, but Upland finished at one hundred and forty for eight, eighteen short. The match was close enough to remain alive because Logan Hays played a strong lone hand and because Jacob, Carter, and Blake each gave him some support, but Upland never escaped the damage of the first five overs. Marina’s defence was built in layers: Lucas Turner’s first-ball strike, Darren Vale’s double blow, Connor Blake’s fourth wicket, Tyler Grant’s two middle-over interruptions, and Owen Pierce’s late removal of Logan. That is why Darren Vale as Player of the Match fits the reconstructed scorecard. His batting was only a tiny late detail, but his new-ball spell turned a chase of one hundred and fifty-nine from a normal pursuit into a long recovery operation. Marina were imperfect with the bat and nearly wasted their best platform at the death. Upland were brave in the chase and had the best individual batting hand of the second innings. The difference was that Marina kept striking at the exact points where Upland were about to turn repair into control.
