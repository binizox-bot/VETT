At Marlow Park Ground in Eastbay, Cedar Vale Women won the toss and chose to bat, but the innings they put together never really found the kind of clean ownership that makes a low-scoring game feel safe. Ana Bell and Mira Hart tried to give the first few overs a careful shape, and for a short while they did the basic survival work: bat close to pad, leave the wider ball alone when they could, take the stray extra when Coral Ridge Women offered one, and avoid making the opening spell look more threatening than it needed to be. Yet the tempo was thin from the start. Mira was bowled by Maya Stone before the first stand could grow beyond a small base, and that first wicket pushed Cedar Vale into the pattern that would follow them for most of the innings: a little resistance, then another break, then another restart. Ana stayed a while longer with Lila Cross, but even that second passage felt like maintenance rather than control. Nadia Vale eventually removed Ana, and once Lila was left to do the central work, the whole innings began to depend on whether she could hold enough of the middle overs together to make the total defendable. Tara Finch gave her the best support Cedar Vale found all afternoon. Tara’s contribution was not large, but it had timing, because she came in when the innings could easily have become a procession and helped Lila create the one partnership with real weight. Lila was the more important figure in that stand: not dominant in a dramatic sense, not hitting the ball into another gear, but patient enough to keep the innings from collapsing and sharp enough to pick off the balls that were slightly overpitched. Their partnership took Cedar Vale to the mid-fifties, which in this sort of match felt like a working platform. Then Leah Brooks removed Tara, and the stability vanished almost immediately. Nora West was run out after a short stay, Sofia Lane was bowled by Isla Morgan without scoring, and the middle order suddenly looked much thinner than it had five minutes earlier. Lila tried to drag the innings deeper, but Sara Moon had her caught, and that was the wicket that changed Cedar Vale from a side trying to reach something useful into a side simply trying not to finish too early. Pippa Reed and Clara Stone added only scraps. Clara found one boundary and Ruby Vale at least gave the final overs a little bit of movement, but Isla Morgan’s return made sure the tail could not turn those scraps into a late push. Pippa went, Clara went, Eva North was bowled first ball, and Ivy Moor’s small last-wicket hand only delayed the finish before Isla closed the innings. Cedar Vale were all out for eighty-two with three balls still unused, and the card had the feel of an innings patched together by Lila’s patience, one useful stand, and a few tail-end flickers rather than anything more secure. Coral Ridge’s bowling had a clear structure. Maya Stone gave them the first opening, Nadia Vale broke the second stand, Leah Brooks cut away the best support act, and Sara Moon removed the batter who mattered most. Isla Morgan then supplied the decisive finishing edge, taking four wickets and making the last quarter of the innings feel as if Cedar Vale were batting on borrowed time. Chasing eighty-three should have been straightforward, but these small-target games often become uncomfortable when wickets arrive in clusters, and Coral Ridge Women almost allowed exactly that. Emily Hart and Sarah Ward started cautiously, with Emily clearly the more fluent of the two. Sarah could not escape the pressure and was bowled by Ivy Moor, which gave Cedar Vale an early opening and a little belief that the total might still create tension. Maya Stone then joined Emily and produced the chase’s most important batting phase. Maya was not reckless, and Emily was not simply swinging to end it quickly; they built the second-wicket stand with enough control to move Coral Ridge out of the danger of a truly nervous start. Emily found boundaries whenever the line offered room, while Maya kept the innings connected by turning the strike over and punishing the loose ball rather than searching for too much. That stand took Coral Ridge into a position of command, and when Sofia Lane bowled Maya, the target was already far smaller than it had been at the start. Yet Cedar Vale refused to let the chase flatten completely. Eva North removed Nadia Vale with a return catch, and Sofia then struck again by bowling Emily, the one batter who had made the pursuit look easy. That wicket mattered because it shifted the mood. Coral Ridge were still ahead, but the chase had suddenly become untidy. Emma Frost fell almost immediately to Eva, and when Sara Moon also went to the same bowler, Coral Ridge had slipped to six down with enough runs still required to make the finish feel slightly tense. Cedar Vale’s bowlers had earned that wobble. Sofia’s two wickets both came at meaningful moments, Eva’s three-wicket spell dragged the game back from routine into nervous territory, and Ivy’s early breakthrough had prevented the chase from beginning too smoothly. The reason the match still stayed with Coral Ridge was Isla Morgan. She had already shaped the first innings with the ball; now she was asked to do something quieter with the bat. The task was not acceleration. It was composure. She stayed in, left the dangerous ball alone, took the single when it was available, and allowed Leah Brooks to do the same at the other end. Leah’s unbeaten hand was actually the more visible scoring contribution in that final stand, but Isla’s presence mattered because she gave the chase its calm after the wobble. Together they added the runs that mattered most: not a big partnership in a broad scoring sense, but a match-settling one because it stopped Cedar Vale’s burst from becoming a full reversal. Coral Ridge reached eighty-four for six in the nineteenth over, and the result was a four-wicket win that felt earned rather than easy. Cedar Vale had enough fight to make a modest target awkward, especially through Sofia and Eva with the ball, and Lila’s first-innings effort gave them something to defend in the first place. Yet Coral Ridge had the better control of the decisive moments. Isla Morgan broke the first innings open at the end, Emily Hart supplied the fluency that made the chase possible, and the final pair of Isla and Leah kept the finish clean when the match briefly threatened to become disorderly. That is why Isla’s Player of the Match award fits the structure of the game. She did not just take wickets after the contest was already over; she made sure Cedar Vale could not stretch their innings into something safer, then returned in the chase to remove the last real chance of panic. Cedar Vale fought in pieces. Coral Ridge answered at the moments that mattered.
