At Elmbridge Turf Oval in Southmere, Rivergate won the toss and chose to bat, but the innings almost immediately slipped into the kind of slow, uncomfortable pattern where survival lasted longer than scoring and every partnership seemed to be built out of resistance rather than control. Rohan Pike, the captain, was bowled by Miles Carter before the batting side had found even the smallest rhythm, and that first wicket set the tone for the morning because Westhaven were not offering the easy release ball that a nervous top order needs. Evan Lowe stayed for a while without ever loosening the pressure, Noah Finch came in and briefly looked capable of putting a few pieces together, and the score crawled to four before Ryan Vale removed Evan. The third-wicket stand was the first time Rivergate got anything resembling batting movement, Noah making five and Kai Mercer staying long enough to help the score reach ten, but Miles Carter broke that too, and from that point the innings became a sequence of tiny stalls and fresh damage. Noah fell at fourteen, Owen Vale followed at fifteen, and Caleb Frost went without the score moving, so Rivergate were six down before they had even reached twenty. Liam Stone tried to hold the middle together, but his four from twenty balls could only slow the fall rather than change it, and when Aaron Blake trapped him in front, the batting card had no real center left. Arjun Reed and Mason Holt added a few more, yet Westhaven kept the same squeeze on the innings. Ethan Shaw removed Arjun, then later Mason, whose six from seventeen became the top score of the innings. Dylan West was left not out on two, Hugo Lane was bowled by Finn Cole, and Rivergate were all out for thirty-eight in the twenty-second over. The total is small enough on its face, but the split underneath makes it even harsher: only twenty-three came from the bat, fifteen came in extras, and no batter reached double figures. Miles Carter was the obvious center of the first innings, seven overs, three maidens, five runs, five wickets, and the value of that spell was in its timing as much as its economy. He broke the start, returned to damage the top order, and cut off the early middle before Rivergate could use the overs they were consuming. Ryan Vale, Aaron Blake, Ethan Shaw, and Finn Cole all added wickets around him, so the collapse never depended on one end alone. Chasing thirty-nine, Westhaven should have been cruising, but Liam Stone gave Rivergate one serious burst of hope. Ethan Vale was caught by Noah Finch before a run had been scored, and Owen Hart was bowled before the opening over was complete. At zero for two, the target was still tiny, but the emotion of the chase had changed. A wicket or two more could have made a simple pursuit feel ridiculous. Leo Mason and Ryan Vale refused to let that happen. Leo played the decisive counterpunch, unbeaten on twenty-three from fifteen balls, driving cleanly, pulling anything short, and clearing the rope once to make sure Rivergate could not crowd the bat for long. Ryan Vale gave him stable support, eleven not out from eleven, and together they turned the chase from danger into a short finish. Their unbroken stand of thirty-nine carried Westhaven home in the sixth over. Rivergate’s bowlers had one excellent moment through Liam Stone, but after that they had neither enough runs nor enough control to keep the squeeze alive. Arjun Reed, Dylan West, and Mason Holt all conceded just enough for the small target to vanish. Westhaven won by eight wickets, and the result fits the deeper shape of the match: Rivergate had one brief shock with the ball, while Westhaven owned the first innings so completely that even a 0/2 start in the chase could not undo it. Miles Carter was the correct Player of the Match because his five-wicket spell created the result before the chase began, leaving Leo Mason and Ryan Vale only to clean up the final act.
