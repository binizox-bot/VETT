At Mariner's Oval in Southport, Pearlbridge Women chose to field first, and for the first few overs the decision did not look reckless because Cresthaven Women were moving forward without yet looking completely loose. Elena Brooks found a couple of clean scoring strokes and Ava Morgan gave the opening stand a practical shape, but Pearlbridge still had enough early control to believe the innings could be kept within reach. The first breakthrough came when Ruby Knox had Elena caught, and although Cresthaven had already reached a useful base, the dismissal still offered Pearlbridge the chance to drag the scoring back before the middle overs opened. That chance did not last. Tara Finch arrived and immediately gave Cresthaven a different attacking profile. She was not simply swinging through the line; she was choosing her areas carefully, punishing width, leaning into anything full, and making every loose delivery feel expensive. Ava stayed long enough to help the second stand grow, but Lena Ward removed her before that partnership could become the whole story. At that stage Pearlbridge had two wickets and a workable position on paper, yet the match was about to move decisively away from them because Tara and Mira Carter built the stand that defined the innings. Tara supplied the visible pressure, the boundaries, the tempo, the sense that Pearlbridge’s bowlers could not miss by much without paying. Mira was less noisy at first and then increasingly severe, building her innings with a calmer rhythm before accelerating once the field spread. Their partnership was worth eighty-five, and it did not feel like one sudden burst so much as a steady taking-over of the game. Pearlbridge kept trying to reset with different bowlers, but the problem was that their discipline cracked too often. Wides kept appearing at the wrong moments, a couple of no-balls kept the batting side fed, and even when one over began neatly, the finish too often gave Cresthaven the release they needed. Ruby Knox did eventually come back to remove Tara for sixty-four, and that wicket prevented the innings from becoming completely unbroken through the final quarter. But by then the damage had already been done. Mira was set, the total was already moving toward a heavy range, and the batting side had enough resources left to keep pressing. Sienna Park fell quickly to Farah Noor, which gave Pearlbridge a second late interruption, yet Rowan Vale came in and played the small finishing role correctly. She did not need a major innings with the bat. She needed to keep Mira on strike, run hard, take the loose ball when it came, and make sure the last overs did not stall after the two quick breaks. Mira finished unbeaten on sixty-six, a compact but forceful innings that gave Cresthaven their permanent center after Tara departed. Rowan added a brief unbeaten hand, and Cresthaven closed on one hundred and eighty-four for four, a total built through layers rather than one isolated hit. Elena and Ava gave the base, Tara gave the acceleration, Mira gave the long finish, and Rowan made sure the closing phase stayed clean. Pearlbridge’s bowling had moments of value, especially Ruby’s two wickets and the control Mira Khan briefly found, but the attack as a whole never held pressure long enough. The extras were not a side note. They repeatedly turned half-decent overs into profitable ones and made a strong batting performance look heavier. Chasing one hundred and eighty-five required an exceptional start, and Pearlbridge got almost the worst possible one. Rowan Vale struck with the first ball, bowling Aisha Reed before the reply had any rhythm, and that single moment changed the emotional direction of the chase. Riya Noor tried to survive with Nia Grant, but the innings was already in repair mode, and Pippa Lane removed Riya before Pearlbridge could build a true opening stand. Lena Ward then came in and became the only batter who looked capable of resisting the collapse for any length of time. She did not dominate in a chase sense because the target was too large and the wickets kept falling around her, but she did stay, and that mattered because without her the innings would have ended much earlier. Nia Grant gave a small amount of support before Ruby North had her caught by Clara Moss, and once that wicket fell Pearlbridge entered the decisive collapse. Rowan returned and cut straight through the middle. Ruby Knox was bowled, Maya Wells was pinned in front, and suddenly the chase had gone from difficult to nearly impossible. The score had barely moved from the mid-twenties, and half the side was gone. Farah Noor tried to stand with Lena for a few overs, but Ava Reed removed her before any real repair could form. Then Rowan struck again, first bowling Mina Stone, then trapping Sara Vale, and the match was effectively over with Pearlbridge eight down and still nowhere near respectability. What made Rowan’s spell so damaging was not just the number of wickets. It was the timing. She started the collapse, accelerated it through the middle, and removed the players who might have stood long enough beside Lena to make the innings less one-sided. Tessa Lane hung around briefly before Elena Brooks bowled her, leaving Lena with only the last pair. Olivia Shore then gave the innings its final small pocket of resistance, not enough to threaten anything, but enough to prevent the chase from ending in complete silence. Lena kept taking what was available, found boundaries when Cresthaven finally missed fuller, and dragged the total to sixty before Olivia was run out by Jade Flint. That final wicket ended the reply in the seventeenth over, and the margin of one hundred and twenty-four runs reflected the actual gap between the sides. Cresthaven did not merely set a large target; they defended it with intent. Rowan Vale’s five wickets gave the result its sharpest edge, but the supporting work mattered too. Pippa Lane stopped the second wicket stand from forming, Ruby North removed Nia at the moment Lena needed a partner, Ava Reed cut off Farah’s brief resistance, and Elena Brooks added the ninth wicket before the last pair could stretch the innings too far. Pearlbridge, by contrast, had one batter standing and almost nothing around her. Lena’s unbeaten thirty-nine was a captain’s effort in the narrowest and hardest sense: not control, not a chase-shaping hand, just defiance inside a chase that had already collapsed. That is why Rowan Vale’s Player of the Match award fits cleanly. Tara Finch and Mira Carter shaped the match with the bat, and either would have been central in an ordinary win, but Rowan’s spell made sure the defence became a rout rather than a contest. Cresthaven built the heavier innings, accepted the extras Pearlbridge offered, then used the new ball and middle overs to turn scoreboard pressure into wickets. Pearlbridge were beaten by the size of the target first, then by the speed with which their chase lost structure, and finally by a five-wicket burst that removed any possibility of late resistance becoming meaningful.
