Good afternoon from Willowfield Ground in Eastmoor, where Bayport XI won the toss, chose to field, and almost immediately made that decision look tactically sharp, because Alderford XI lost Rowan Vale before the innings had even developed a heartbeat, Jace Ward finding the edge and Dylan Reed completing the catch with the score still untouched, and from that first interruption the batting side were forced into one long argument against pressure rather than a clean attempt to build a platform. Mateo Shaw and Elias Drake tried to steady the innings in the only way available to them, but it never looked comfortable, Mateo finding one early boundary and then being trapped in front by Nolan West, Elias taking time without ever really loosening the field, and by the time Felix Stone removed the captain, Alderford were three down with barely a base under them. Liam Hart was the reason the innings did not collapse into something much smaller. His fifty-two from forty-two was not a free-flowing cruise, even with four fours and two sixes on the card; it was a rescue innings played inside recurring damage, the kind where every partner seems to arrive with a job and leave before the job is finished. Colin Frost was bowled by Evan Brooks before he could change the tempo, Owen Marsh was pinned by Hugo Clarke after a small but useful stand, and through that whole middle section Liam had to keep recalculating what a respectable score even meant. The partnership with Seth Lane was the one passage where Alderford most nearly found control. Seth was sharper than most of the lower middle order, strong enough to make twenty-two from eighteen, and with Liam he dragged the score from the low sixties to just under three figures. That was the first time Bayport had to think about a finish rather than a squeeze. Ivan Ford stopped it at exactly the right moment by removing Liam, and once the top scorer had gone at ninety-eight, Alderford again became a side trying to steal pieces from the final overs. Kian Moore briefly helped with a six, Seth struck hard enough to keep the board moving, Bryce Allen and Toby Kent scraped at the end, and Mason Vale was left not out without facing, but the innings still closed on only one hundred and twenty-four for nine. That total had value because of Liam’s hand, Seth’s support, and a few scattered extras, but it also carried the unmistakable feel of a side that had never been allowed one uninterrupted stretch. Bayport’s bowling was spread in the right way. Jace Ward gave them the first wound and later returned for Seth. Nolan West and Felix Stone clipped the early rebuild. Hugo Clarke cut off Owen Marsh. Ivan Ford removed the one batter who mattered most. Evan Brooks, in just two overs, took the two wickets that kept the lower order from turning the end into a proper late surge. Chasing one hundred and twenty-five, Bayport did not get a completely silent passage either, because Blake Turner came out briskly and then was bowled by Mason Vale before the second over was done, which gave Alderford the early noise they needed if they were going to make a modest score feel heavier. Arlo Kane, though, never allowed that noise to become panic. He began quietly enough, letting Blake provide the first flash, then settling beside Carter Miles into the partnership that gave the chase its real shape. Carter’s twenty-four from twenty-six was not spectacular, but it was exactly the right supporting innings for this target: enough boundaries to prevent stagnation, enough occupation to keep Arlo from having to overplay, and enough calm to move Bayport from a slightly exposed start into a proper chase. Owen Marsh broke that stand by having Carter caught by Rowan Vale, then removed Dylan Reed after a brief attacking hand, and for a few overs Alderford could reasonably believe the match was still available if one more wicket came quickly. Arlo simply kept refusing to provide it. Gavin Price’s fifteen helped him through the next phase, a partnership that carried Bayport beyond the hundred mark, and although Kian Moore trapped Gavin in front at one hundred and eight, the equation by then had been pulled into manageable territory. That is where Arlo’s innings deserves its weight. His unbeaten fifty-eight was not only the highest score of the chase; it was the innings that kept every Alderford breakthrough from becoming a collapse. He scored with restraint when the chase needed stability, struck boundaries when the field crept in, and then accelerated just enough near the end to make sure the target disappeared before late nerves could gather. Hudson Shore’s five not out was small on the scorecard but useful in context, because he stayed with Arlo through the final stand and allowed the set batter to finish without further damage. Bayport reached one hundred and twenty-five for four in the eighteenth over, winning by six wickets and qualifying with a chase that was controlled more than it was explosive. Alderford had moments to defend: Mason Vale removed Blake early, Owen Marsh’s double strike made the middle phase interesting, Kian Moore’s wicket of Gavin Price briefly delayed the finish, and the fielding side never completely drifted out of effort. But a target of one hundred and twenty-five needed clustered panic, and Arlo Kane gave Bayport the opposite. Liam Hart had saved Alderford from a poor total, yet Arlo converted a modest target into a safe chase. That is why the player-of-the-match award belongs to him. In a game built around pressure rather than spectacle, his unbeaten hand was the cleanest piece of control on either side.
