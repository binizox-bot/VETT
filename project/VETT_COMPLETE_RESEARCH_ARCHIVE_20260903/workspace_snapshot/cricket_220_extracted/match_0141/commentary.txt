Amelia Rowe started with the sharper intent, three boundaries in a brisk sixteen, and even though Iris Cole never settled before Erin Pike bowled her, Amelia’s early scoring made sure Rivergate were not immediately pinned down. Nadia Cross then removed Amelia as well, and at twenty-nine for two the innings had lost both openers before it had really reached a stable shape. That is where Clara Vale became the first major figure in the match. She did not play a reckless innings, and she did not need to. What she gave Rivergate was continuity, and in a low-to-mid scoring women’s that often matters more than one spectacular over. Lena Brooks struggled to score freely but stayed long enough to help Clara add thirty-one, and even though Sarah Vale eventually trapped Lena lbw and Olivia Marsh then removed Sophie Marsh for a duck, Clara did not let those wickets turn into a full collapse. The most important Rivergate partnership came after that, when Tara Bennett joined Clara and the two rebuilt from a difficult sixty for four into something far more useful. Clara’s forty-two from thirty-six was the best pure batting hand in the innings, five boundaries, good control through the off side, and enough patience to let the innings breathe. Tara’s twenty-four from twenty-six was quieter but extremely important, because she stopped Highland Crest from bowling only at Clara and forced them to keep defending both ends. Their stand of thirty-eight was the hinge of the innings. It did not explode, yet it moved Rivergate from recovery into a properly defendable direction. Nadia Cross broke it by removing Clara at ninety-eight, and Sarah Vale later dismissed Tara, which meant Rivergate still had to fight through the last overs without either of the two set batters. Ruby Stone and Zoe Carter made that finish better than it might have been. Rivergate’s defense was not built on one spectacular five-wicket spell. It was built on correct pressure at the correct times. Tara Bennett’s two for sixteen, including a maiden, was the central spell because she broke the opening stand and later tightened the finish. Amelia Rowe and Grace Ward both struck in the middle. Rivergate, by contrast, had smaller contributions spread across more useful moments.
