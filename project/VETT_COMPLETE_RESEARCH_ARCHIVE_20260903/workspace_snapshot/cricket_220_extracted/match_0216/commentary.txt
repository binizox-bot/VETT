At Alderfield Oval in Northmere, under a sky that looked calm enough before the first ball and then slowly turned into one of those long afternoons where the fielding side begins by believing it has a plan and ends up chasing shadows, Brookhaven CC won the toss and chose to bat first, and for a short while Stonebridge CC had enough discipline to make that call look only cautiously correct rather than obviously dominant. Henry Cole and Marcus Flynn were not spraying early, and the first ten overs had a proper contest inside them, Noah Clarke taking the busier opening role while Adrian Vale started with the sort of quiet measurement that can look almost harmless until the field suddenly realizes the batter has read the pace better than everyone else. Clarke’s 28 gave Brookhaven the first platform, and when he was eventually taken off Henry Cole with the score in the mid-sixties, Stonebridge could still tell themselves they had not let the game run away. That feeling did not last, because the second act of the innings belonged almost entirely to Adrian Vale. Ethan Brooks was the partner who gave the stand its balance, not always fluent, not trying to dominate, but steady enough to keep the bowlers from locking every plan around one man, while Vale kept opening out, first through controlled driving, then through harder strokes square of the wicket, then through those lifted hits that tell a captain the normal ring field has already lost its authority. The partnership moved past a hundred and carried Brookhaven from a good start into command, and even though there were a few gifts tucked into the total, the main pressure was coming from the bat. Vale’s retirement hurt on 118 changed the visual rhythm of the innings, but not its direction. He had already built the architecture of the match, and Stonebridge were left with the awkward feeling that they had not dismissed the one batter who had done most of the damage. Ethan Brooks and Lucas Reed then kept the innings moving, Reed arriving with a sharper tempo and briefly giving the middle overs a second pulse before Ethan was finally removed by Dylan Hart. Reed also had to leave hurt after a brisk 39, and at that point Stonebridge might have hoped that two forced departures and a real wicket would cool the innings. Ryan Stone, Caleb Frost, Owen Pike, Finn Carter and Leo Ward all came through in shorter bursts, some useful, some messy, but Hale stayed as the constant, collecting from the gaps, taking on the fuller length when it appeared, and making sure every little lower-order fragment pushed the score further beyond normal reach. Stonebridge did keep taking wickets late, Marcus Flynn and Dylan Hart sharing the most visible reward, Caleb Price getting one, Henry Cole taking the opening break, and a run-out briefly giving the fielders something to shout about, but they never had the one sustained squeeze that could turn Brookhaven’s innings into a controlled score. The wides and the small extras hurt them as well, because against a side already carrying a hundred-plus retired hand, a half-century from Ethan Brooks, a second retired burst from Lucas Reed, and Hale still there at the close, every donation became expensive. Vale gave the innings its size, Brooks and Reed made sure the middle did not stall, Hale gave it the finish and then helped control the defence, and the fielders stayed sharp enough to make the chase feel smaller with every passing over.
