At Kingswell Club Ground in Southport, Crownvale Women won the toss, chose to bat, and put together an innings that did not depend on one early explosion so much as on a clean sequence of repairs, rebuilds, and late acceleration. Mara Bell gave the innings its first little push, sharp through the infield, quick to seize width, and already making Tidehaven Women adjust before the opening spell had settled. Nora Bennett was more measured beside her, taking the first few overs as a chance to let the pitch come to her, but the score still moved at a useful clip because Tidehaven could not keep every over clean. Lucy Reed broke the opening stand by having Mara caught, and at that moment there was an opportunity for Tidehaven to slow the innings into something more controllable. Instead, Iris Turner arrived and became the central figure of the match. At first she did not look like a batter trying to own the whole innings. She simply gave it shape. Nora kept working her way through the middle of the powerplay, and Iris settled into a rhythm that was calm without being passive. Their stand moved Crownvale out of the early-wicket phase and into a proper platform, not because it was spectacular in one rush, but because it gave the batting side time, order, and control. Mia Foster eventually removed Nora, and that wicket landed at a useful point for Tidehaven, but the deeper issue for the fielding side was that Iris had already found her range. Maya Collins came in and played the correct supporting hand, busy from the start, strong enough through the off side to keep the field honest, and disciplined enough not to waste the platform by trying to overhit. The Iris-Maya stand was the one that truly shifted the innings from useful to dangerous. Iris took greater control through that passage, punishing the loose ball but still keeping the strike moving whenever Tidehaven managed a tighter line. Maya’s contribution was compact rather than dominant, but that was exactly why the partnership worked. She did not ask the innings to restart around her; she kept it moving around Iris. When Maya was run out by Zoe Marsh, Tidehaven had finally broken the second large stand, but the score had already reached a position where the final overs could be played with freedom. Ava Stone added a short, bright hand, enough to keep the momentum from dipping, before Lucy Reed struck again. Sienna Pike then gave Crownvale the kind of late cameo that can alter the feel of a total, striking cleanly in her few balls and making sure the final over still had punch. Iris was run out from the final delivery, which felt less like a failure and more like the end of an innings she had carried through every important stage. Her seventy-two was the spine of the scorecard: present for the first rebuild, the main stand, the late push, and the last ball. Crownvale closed on one hundred and fifty-eight for five, a total that looked strong because it had been constructed properly rather than thrown together. Tidehaven had bowling efforts with value. Grace Harper’s full spell kept things quieter than the rest, Mia Foster removed the captain, and Lucy Reed’s two wickets prevented the innings from becoming completely seamless. Still, they never forced a collapse, and once Iris had settled, the bowling side were mostly chasing the game. The pursuit began badly for Tidehaven and never truly recovered. Aisha Reed was bowled early by Ava Stone, Clara Stone was run out by Grace Vale after a hesitant call, and Zoe Carter then took two wickets in a short, damaging burst, removing Maya Lane and Lucy Reed with the score still barely moving. Four down inside the sixth over meant Tidehaven were not really chasing anymore; they were repairing while the target moved further away emotionally with every dot ball. Grace Harper tried to change that with Hannah Price, and for a few overs the innings at least had a recognizable shape. Grace found one boundary, took advantage of the extras Crownvale offered, and helped push the score out of complete wreckage. Yet Grace Vale ended that stand with a stumping completed by Mara Bell, and that dismissal returned Tidehaven to the same problem they had faced all innings: one pair would begin to form, and Crownvale would break it before it could become a threat. Tessa Ward gave the next passage more urgency. Her nine from nine was brisk enough to make the field move, and Hannah, though slow, stayed with her to form the best middle stretch of the chase. But even that partnership was not dangerous enough for the target. It reduced the damage; it did not change the match. Grace Vale removed Tessa with Nora Bennett taking the catch, and after that Hannah was left carrying a chase that had already lost too many pieces. Hannah’s twenty-two from forty balls was a resisting innings rather than a chasing innings. She held one end, absorbed pressure, and kept Tidehaven from folding immediately, but the required tempo had already gone beyond what one slow anchor could repair. Ruby North then arrived as the closer. She had Hannah caught by Ava Stone, then returned through the tail with enough accuracy to make the finish feel inevitable. Ruby Scott was bowled by Ella Frost, Mia Foster was pinned by Ruby, and Ella Brooks gave one last boundary before Ruby finished the innings through a catch behind. Tidehaven were all out for seventy-six in the nineteenth over, and the margin of eighty-two runs reflected the difference between an innings with structure and an innings made only of fragments. Crownvale had Iris at the center, Nora and Maya building around her, Sienna finishing sharply, and then a bowling unit that shared the defence intelligently. Ava Stone started the damage, Zoe Carter broke the top order, Grace Vale cut off the middle-order repairs, Ella Frost chipped in late, and Ruby North turned the last quarter into a cleanup. Tidehaven had moments: Lucy Reed’s two wickets, Grace Harper’s control with the ball, Hannah Price’s long stay, and Tessa Ward’s brief spark. None of those moments connected for long enough. That is why Iris Turner as Player of the Match fits the reconstructed match cleanly. She did not need wickets to own the day. Her innings was the difference between a merely workable Crownvale score and a match-winning one, because she gave the first innings its continuity, its tempo, and its final shape. Once Tidehaven lost early wickets in reply, the chase had no equivalent center, and Crownvale’s win became steadily more certain with every broken partnership.
