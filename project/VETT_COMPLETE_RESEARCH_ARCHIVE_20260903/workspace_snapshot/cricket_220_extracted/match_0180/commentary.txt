At North Meridian Academy Ground in Westport, Meridian chose to bat first and built this match into a one-sided result not through one loose burst alone, but through repeated layers of batting control that kept Valley Ridge permanently chasing the previous phase rather than shaping the next one. The opening stand between Kai Bennett and Noah Grant gave Meridian the first clear advantage, and it did so with enough pace to make the fielding side feel under pressure almost immediately. Kai was the sharper presence, driving hard when the length was full and picking up anything loose with the confidence of a player who wanted the new ball taken away from the bowlers early, while Noah’s role was quieter but still useful, keeping enough of the strike moving to stop Valley Ridge from building their plans around one batter. Their partnership reached 74, helped by a messy extras stream, and by the time Leo Marsh finally removed Kai for 44, Meridian had already taken the first stage of the innings. Valley Ridge did briefly answer when Finn Carter bowled Noah not long after, leaving the score at 87 for 2, but that was the opening they needed to turn into a sustained squeeze and never quite did. Evan Cole and Arjun Stone then produced the stand that gave the innings its spine. Evan’s 58 from 62 was not just a supporting hand; it was the innings that stopped the double interruption from becoming a real wobble, because he gave Arjun a stable partner, rotated well enough, and still found enough power through the middle overs to make the bowling side keep adjusting. Arjun, though, was already becoming the central figure of the day. He started that stand carefully enough, not rushing the game after two wickets, then expanded as the partnership moved beyond survival and into command. Their 112-run stand carried Meridian to 199 for 3, and once Evan fell, the innings was no longer about whether Meridian could reach a defendable score; it was about how far Arjun could push it. Leo Hayes added a useful 18, Milan Reed gave him another small phase, and then Tom Parker’s 25 from 15 gave the late overs the directness they needed. The value of Arjun’s innings was that he kept changing with the match. He absorbed after the early wickets, accumulated through the middle, then accelerated in the final stretch with Tom and Ishan Wells. His unbeaten 119 from 111, with ten fours and four sixes, was the complete innings of the match, and the last partnership with Ishan only completed what had been obvious for several overs: Meridian had moved from a strong score into a crushing one. Valley Ridge did not bowl without effort. Mason Price and Finn Carter both took important wickets, Leo Marsh found the first breakthrough, Jasper Hall removed Milan Reed, and there were spells from Riley Shaw, Owen Bell, and Max Owen that at least tried to slow the tempo. The problem was that no spell stayed in control long enough, and the extras column kept giving Meridian room to breathe. Twenty-eight extras in a fifty-over innings are heavy enough on their own; combined with Arjun’s hundred and the Evan-Arjun partnership, they made 327 for 6 feel fully earned and almost unreachable before the chase had even begun. Valley Ridge’s reply needed a calm start and one large top-order innings. They got neither at first. Leo Marsh was bowled early by Felix Ward, Max Owen followed after making only 9, and at 25 for 2 the chase had already been pushed into a difficult shape. Riley Shaw and Mason Price briefly repaired the damage in the only phase where Valley Ridge looked capable of creating a contest. Mason played with proper intent, using width well and attacking anything overpitched, while Riley held enough of an end to let the score move from 25 to 82. That stand of 57 was the one passage that prevented the chase from becoming a collapse immediately. Ethan Brooks broke it by removing Riley, and from that point the innings began to narrow around Mason Price alone. Owen Bell came and went for 8. Mason reached a strong 52 from 46, full of positive strokeplay and two clean sixes, but Aaron Pike bowled him at 110 for 5, and that was the wicket that removed the last real possibility of Valley Ridge stretching the chase into anything competitive. After Mason fell, the scoreboard became a sequence of interruptions. Jasper Hall was caught, Tyler Shaw was run out without scoring, Dev Lane was bowled, Finn Carter’s resistance ended at 123, and Caleb North’s final dismissal left Valley Ridge 129 all out in 31.5 overs. Ethan Brooks’ spell was the cleanest cutting edge, three wickets for only 18 runs, and it mattered because he struck both in the middle phase and at the end. Aaron Pike’s two wickets were also crucial because he removed Owen Bell and then Mason Price, the one batter who had threatened to pull the chase into a more respectable shape. Connor Dale kept the lower order pinned down, Felix Ward set the early damage, and the rest of the attack did not need to chase miracles because the scoreboard was already doing half the pressure work. The match therefore resolves into a very direct contrast. Meridian had batting phases that connected: Kai’s start, Evan’s stabilizing fifty, Arjun’s full-innings control, and Tom’s late impact. Valley Ridge had one counterpunch through Mason Price and one decent partnership with Riley Shaw, but nothing strong enough before or after it to survive the size of the target. Arjun Stone is the correct player of the match because his unbeaten hundred was not decorative accumulation in a big innings; it was the innings that made every later phase of the match easier for Meridian and almost impossible for Valley Ridge. Meridian 327 for 6, Valley Ridge 129 all out, and a 198-run win that came from batting depth, scoreboard pressure, and a bowling unit disciplined enough to turn an enormous target into a collapse rather than a contest.
