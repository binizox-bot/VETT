At Cedarbrook Turf Ground in Westmere, Meadowvale won the toss and chose to field, and for a very short time that decision still had the normal logic of a match behind it, because the new ball gives a fielding side a chance to shape the morning before the batting side settles. Northbridge gave them almost no such chance. Leo Hart attacked so violently in the opening stretch that the innings had changed shape before the first ten overs were even complete. His seventy-six from thirty balls was not just a fast start; it was a field-setting assault, full of clean drives, pulled sixes, and enough early damage to force Meadowvale away from their attacking plans. Noah Barrett, at the other end, was already doing the more durable work, and that contrast made the first stand so destructive. Leo broke the game open, Noah made sure it did not close again. Their opening partnership of one hundred and thirty-six carried only five extras, which means the runs were being taken with the bat rather than donated through general disorder, and by the time Leo finally fell to Carter Wells, Meadowvale were already defending a match that had barely begun. Mason Reed came and went in a short second-wicket stand, but that wicket did not change the direction of the innings because Ethan Cole then joined Noah and gave Northbridge the match’s true control phase. Their partnership of one hundred and forty-three was less explosive than the opening blast, yet it was probably the section that made the total genuinely enormous. Noah moved through the middle overs with the calm of a player who understood he no longer needed to chase the game; he only needed to keep owning it. Ethan’s eighty-four gave him the perfect support, strong enough to score in his own right and busy enough to stop Meadowvale from loading pressure onto one batter. Noah’s hundred arrived as the natural center of the innings rather than a separate milestone, and when he eventually fell for one hundred and twenty-eight, Northbridge were already close to three hundred. Ethan added a little more with Arlo Bennett before Rafi Stone finally bowled him, and then the innings entered its last acceleration phase. Dylan Price’s fifty-two from thirty-eight balls made the closing overs hurt because he did not let the rate settle after the two major wickets. Arlo played a short supporting hand, Connor Hayes stayed unbeaten, and Owen Pike was there at the finish as Northbridge reached four hundred and seven for six. Meadowvale did collect wickets, especially through Rafi Stone’s two and the scattered breakthroughs from Carter Wells, Milo Grant, Arjun Vale, and Hugo Price, but those dismissals read more like interruptions than control. Their bowlers conceded too many boundaries in the first phase and then never fully stopped the middle. The total was built in three clean layers: Leo Hart’s opening violence, Noah Barrett and Ethan Cole’s long command, and Dylan Price’s late push. Chasing four hundred and eight, Meadowvale needed something close to a miracle, and instead they got a slow beginning followed by a collapse so complete that the target stopped mattering. Aaron Blake and Evan Hart at least survived together for a while, adding twenty for the first wicket, but the scoring rate was never close to relevant and five of those runs were extras. Connor Hayes removed Aaron for twelve, Caleb Turner bowled Evan soon after, and Isaac Cole followed lbw to the same bowler. Logan Price tried to be the second small point of resistance, but his eleven from twenty-seven could not carry a chase of this scale. Tyler Brooks briefly helped drag the score to thirty-six, and then the innings fell apart entirely. Luke Parker removed Tyler, Nathan Shaw bowled Liam Porter, then Logan Price, then Jordan Hayes, then Cole Bennett, and Meadowvale were suddenly thirty-six for eight without a run added through that central crash. That sequence was the real second-innings statement. Meadowvale had not been chasing in any meaningful way before it, but after it they were barely surviving. Aiden Stone and Gavin Moore added seven late runs, only enough to push the score into the forties, before Nathan Shaw returned to bowl Gavin and then trap Carter Fox, finishing with six wickets for ten. Meadowvale were all out for forty-three in the twenty-fourth over. Their batting card explains the margin with brutal clarity: only thirty-six runs came from the bat, only Aaron Blake and Logan Price reached double figures, and the best stand was twenty at the start, long before the innings had been asked any real middle-order questions. Northbridge’s bowling was clinical after the huge batting display. Ryan Cross kept the chase quiet without reward, Connor Hayes made the first incision, Caleb Turner removed two of the top four, Luke Parker chipped in, and Nathan Shaw turned the lower middle order into a procession. Noah Barrett was the correct Player of the Match because his one hundred and twenty-eight was the innings that linked the early explosion to the middle-over domination and made the final total possible. Nathan Shaw’s spell gave the scoreline its final cruelty, Leo Hart and Ethan Cole made the total massive, and Dylan Price ensured the closing overs did not soften. The final result, Northbridge winning by three hundred and sixty-four runs, fits the cricket exactly: one side built in waves, the other never produced even one meaningful reply.
