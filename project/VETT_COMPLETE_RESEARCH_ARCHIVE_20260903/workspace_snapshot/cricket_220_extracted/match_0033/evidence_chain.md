| L1 | Direct extraction | Basic match facts can be read directly from the scorecard and commentary. | Harborline won by 191 runs; Ethan Clarke was Player of the Match; Harborline made 262/5 and Atoll Bay were all out for 71. |
| L1 | Key player identification | Major contributors remain identifiable without calculation. | Ethan Clarke scored 78 and took three wickets; Lucas Byrne made 72 and later finished the chase; Mason Vale made 48. |
| L2 | Cricket-rule arithmetic | Requires checking batting runs, extras, final totals, and victory margin. | Harborline batting runs add to 234 and extras add 28, giving 262; Atoll Bay batting runs add to 51 and extras add 20, giving 71; 262 minus 71 gives the 191-run margin. |
| L2 | Partnership and FOW consistency | Requires cumulative verification across partnerships and fall-of-wickets. | Harborline partnerships move through 139, 214, 246, 251, 255, and 262; Atoll Bay partnerships move through 3, 5, 37, 50, 55, 66, 66, 68, 70, and 71. |
| L2 | Bowling reconciliation | Requires understanding that byes and leg-byes are not charged to bowlers. | Atoll Bay bowlers concede 259 bowler-charged runs while Harborline total 262 because three runs are byes/leg-byes; Harborline bowlers concede 65 charged runs while Atoll Bay total 71 because six runs are byes/leg-byes. |
| L2 | Ball-count consistency | Requires checking legal balls against overs and batting balls. | Harborline batters face 210 legal balls across 35.0 overs; Atoll Bay batters face 154 legal balls across 25.4 overs. |
| L3 | Commentary-hidden inference | Key facts are embedded through innings rhythm instead of dense scoreboard repetition. | The text presents the opening stand as the defining batting platform, Ethan Clarke as the all-round hinge, and Atoll Bay’s chase as a collapse interrupted by one brief counterstand. |
| L3 | Narrative-to-table validation | Readers must infer table evidence from descriptive match flow. | “The opening pair took the game past the first danger zone,” “the chase briefly breathed through Isaac and Daniel,” and “Caleb’s spell made scoring feel almost impossible” all map to scorecard fields. |
