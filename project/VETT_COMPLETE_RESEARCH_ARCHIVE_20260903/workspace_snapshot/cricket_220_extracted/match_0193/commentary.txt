At Sandmere College Oval in Brookfield, Moorfield won the toss and chose to bat, yet the innings almost immediately developed into one of those awkward low-score cards where the total kept moving in uneven little pieces without the batting side ever really owning the scoreboard. Kai Reed gave Moorfield their only early spark, striking one clean boundary and looking briefly capable of giving the innings a proper top-order shape, but around him Eastport kept finding interruptions. Owen Lane scratched around for five before Finn Cole removed him, and then Noah Price was bowled by Julian Cross without a run added, which meant the first small platform had already been cut down before Moorfield could use it. Kai and Arlo Bennett produced the largest stand of the innings, but even that was only fourteen, and two of those were extras, so it was less a recovery than a short pause in the damage. Julian Cross ended Kai’s stay at twenty-four for three, Finn Cole removed Arlo soon after, and by the time Mason Pike was caught and bowled with the score at thirty-five, Moorfield had lost half the side without a single partnership reaching anything that could change the field. Ethan Vale and Samir Hale then tried to slow the collapse, and they did consume enough balls to drag the innings beyond the first hour, but their stand of twelve was almost entirely shaped by Eastport’s looseness rather than batting pressure. Eight of those runs came through extras, and that detail explains the innings better than any single dismissal: Moorfield were staying alive because Eastport kept sending down wides and the occasional no-ball, not because the batters had found control. Caleb Ross then became the decisive figure. He bowled Ethan, had Dylan Ward caught, trapped Samir, and later removed Carter Mills, taking four wickets for eleven and turning an already weak innings into a complete collapse. Finn Cole’s three wickets had opened the middle, Julian Cross had damaged the top, Adrian Wells closed the card by bowling Leo Grant, and Hugo Ellis was left not out on seven as Moorfield finished at sixty-six in the twenty-third over. The score looks modest at first glance and worse when broken apart: only forty-two came from the bat, twenty-four were extras, the top score was twelve, and the best partnership was fourteen. Eastport’s bowling was not perfectly clean because the wides were far too many, but the wicket pressure was constant enough that Moorfield never turned those gifts into a defendable score. Chasing sixty-seven, Eastport should have moved through the target quietly, and the early wicket of Arlo Finch did give Moorfield the tiny opening they needed. Dylan Ward had him caught with only two on the board, and for a moment the fielding side could imagine the low-target discomfort that often appears when batters think the job is already done. Julian Cross and Ben Carter steadied the reply with the best partnership of the chase, thirty-one together, Julian playing the sharper hand with sixteen and Ben providing the more patient support. That stand took Eastport to thirty-three and appeared to settle the match, but Moorfield did not let the pursuit become entirely smooth. Julian was run out by Kai Reed, Ben then fell to Ethan Vale, and Eastport suddenly had to restart at thirty-seven for three. Caleb Ross, after his bowling spell, helped add fourteen with Finn Cole, but Ethan Vale struck again to remove Caleb, then bowled Mason Hart next ball, leaving Eastport wobbling at fifty-one for five. The target was still small, but the chase had become awkward enough to matter. Finn Cole and Ryan Brooks nudged the score to fifty-four before Ethan took his fourth wicket, and at six down Eastport still needed calm from the lower order rather than a careless swing. Ryan provided it. Adrian Wells came in and played the short finishing hand, striking a boundary in his four not out, while Ryan stayed unbeaten on ten and allowed the final stand to carry Eastport past the line. The final score, sixty-seven for six, says Eastport won by four wickets, but it also says Moorfield made them work harder than a target of sixty-seven should normally require. Ethan Vale’s four-wicket chase spell was a serious losing-side effort, yet the first innings had left too little margin for it to become match-winning. Caleb Ross was the correct Player of the Match because his four for eleven was the spell that made the chase possible in the first place, breaking Moorfield’s middle and lower order before they could use Eastport’s extras to build anything safer. Eastport were untidy, then effective; Moorfield were briefly competitive with the ball, but their batting never supplied enough substance to turn the fightback into an upset.
