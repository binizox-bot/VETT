At Eastbank Cricket Ground in Larkmoor, Aurora XI chose to field first in a shortened eight-over contest, and the match immediately took on that sharp, compressed rhythm where one mistake feels like a full over lost and one clean hit can briefly change the mood of the whole innings, because Cedar Ridge XI did not begin cautiously at all, Evan Dale swinging hard enough to clear the rope almost before the field had settled, only to fall inside the first over when Rowan Vale found the first breakthrough and Arlo Finch held the catch, and that opening exchange set up the central pattern of the innings: Cedar Ridge kept finding bursts of scoring, Aurora kept cutting them off before those bursts could settle into a proper platform. Gareth Pike was never fluent, and when Rowan Vale struck again to remove him, the innings had already been pushed into the hands of Ronan Scott. Ronan was the only Cedar Ridge batter who looked capable of making the shortened format fully belong to him. He did not merely swing from the hip; he chose his moments, waited when the ball was not there, and then punished anything even fractionally in his arc, four boundaries and three sixes giving his forty-one the kind of weight that made Aurora’s fielders keep glancing at the scoreboard even when wickets were falling around him. Miles Brook came and went quickly, bowled by Theo Wynn, and that was an important little interruption because it prevented Ronan from having a stable partner at the exact moment Cedar Ridge were trying to move from recovery into acceleration. Leon Carter gave him a short phase of support, mostly by staying long enough for Ronan to take the louder hand, and their stand of thirty-one was the only section of the innings where Cedar Ridge genuinely looked as if they might push toward a total above ninety. Mason Lark broke that phase by removing Ronan at sixty-two, and the effect was immediate. Once the main scorer was gone, the lower order had to manufacture momentum from almost nothing. Idris Young fell to Arlo Finch, Leon was pinned soon after, Henry Vale tried to drag the final over along, and Tomas Reed’s last-ball run-out left Cedar Ridge at eighty-two for seven. Aurora’s bowling was not perfectly quiet, because in a match this short very few spells are, but it was timely. Rowan Vale removed both early openers. Arlo Finch took two middle-late wickets. Theo Wynn broke one small stand. Mason Lark took the only truly decisive batting wicket by removing Ronan. Chasing eighty-three, Aurora should still have known the target could become awkward if the first over went wrong, and it went worse than wrong. Dario Wells fell without scoring, Mark Dale striking before the chase had moved. In a short chase, that kind of collapse can make every remaining batter feel as if they have to finish the match at once. Lucas Bard refused to let panic take over. Milan Marsh, though, was the player who truly understood the chase. He came in with the innings damaged and did not treat the situation as a licence for reckless hitting. He rebuilt first, then attacked. With Lucas, he added thirty-two, the stand that brought Aurora back from scoreboard trouble into a chase that could be managed again. Caleb Orion did remove Lucas, and when Cade Harper also fell at fifty-one for five, Cedar Ridge had one last genuine opening. That was the moment Milan’s innings became match-winning rather than merely useful. He stayed calm, kept finding the right ball to attack, and then Nolan Shore arrived with a finishing cameo that changed the chase’s tempo completely. Nolan’s unbeaten eighteen from six balls gave the end of the innings its cleanest acceleration, while Milan remained the anchor and striker in balance, finishing unbeaten on thirty-seven from twenty. Their unbroken stand of thirty-three settled the match with two balls unused, Aurora reaching eighty-four for five in the eighth over. Cedar Ridge had bowled well enough to create real panic through Mark Dale’s early double strike and Caleb Orion’s middle-over wickets, but they could not remove Milan, and that was the difference. The match was not a smooth chase. It was a recovery from collapse, and Milan Marsh owned the recovery. Ronan Scott had given Cedar Ridge the innings that made the contest competitive, but Milan supplied the innings that actually solved the match.
