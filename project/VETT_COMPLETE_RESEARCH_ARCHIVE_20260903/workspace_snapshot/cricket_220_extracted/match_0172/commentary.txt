At Meridian Youth Cricket Ground in Westhaven, Bayridge chose to bat first and ended up creating one of those fifty-over scorecards that looks respectable only after you remember how uncomfortable the first half had been, because Ridge Falcons, led by Leo Grant, controlled the new ball so well that Bayridge spent most of the innings repairing damage before Aiden Cross and Sam Price finally dragged the total into defendable territory. The early spell had real bite. Noah Vale was the first to go, caught behind off Leo after making only 5, Eli Carter followed after a brief attempt to settle the innings, and when Lucas Reed was pinned lbw without scoring, Bayridge were 41 for 3 and already batting from underneath the match. Mason Drew and Oliver Shaw then did what age-group fifty-over cricket often demands: they occupied. It was not glamorous, it did not move quickly, but their 45-run stand at least stopped the innings from sliding into something much smaller. Mason’s 31 from 60 and Oliver’s 26 from 60 were both heavy, slow, necessary hands, and even when Mason was run out at 86 for 4, Bayridge had at least forced Ridge Falcons to keep working. Henry Brooks helped for a while with 18, but Jonah Miles trapped him lbw, and when Oliver later fell at 126 for 6, the innings still looked more like a survival job than a proper first-innings total. That changed through Aiden Cross. His 56 from 64 was easily Bayridge’s most important batting contribution, not just because it was the highest score, but because it came at the only point where the innings could still move from modest to useful. Sam Price gave him exactly the support he needed, 22 from 37, and their 62-run stand turned Bayridge from 126 for 6 into 188 for 7. The last wicket of the innings fell on the final ball, but by then Bayridge had 196 for 8, a number that felt much stronger than the earlier scoreline had promised. Ridge Falcons’ bowling still deserved the better reading of that first innings. Leo Grant’s 3 for 27 framed the whole card, Samir Lane’s ten overs brought control, Nathan Brook and Jonah Miles struck at useful points, and although Jordan Hale leaked runs, the attack as a whole had done enough to keep Bayridge from ever fully escaping. The chase, though, began as if Leo Grant wanted to remove all suspense before it could grow. Kai Brooks gave him a little company at the top, but Leo was the one who changed the tempo, striking 68 from only 33 balls with eight fours and four sixes, and the second-wicket stand with Ben Turner was the burst that made 197 feel much smaller than it had at the break. Ridge Falcons reached 87 for 2 when Leo finally fell to Aiden Cross, and that wicket mattered because it shifted the chase from violent control into something more delicate. Ben Turner followed soon after, Miles Shaw was run out, and suddenly 99 for 4 gave Bayridge a real way back into the match. Nathan Brook then became the stabilizing figure the chase required. He did not play loudly, but his unbeaten 44 from 63 was the innings that stopped Ridge Falcons from wasting Leo’s work. Jonah Miles and Owen Dent helped him in slower stands that absorbed pressure rather than attacking it, and even though Henry Brooks kept Bayridge alive with a strong four-wicket spell, he could never get Nathan out. When Owen fell at 150 for 6, Bayridge still had hope. Logan Frost answered that with the kind of small cameo that wins chases, 21 from 12, enough to turn tension back toward Ridge Falcons before Henry removed him at 177 for 7. From there, Nathan still had to finish the job, and Samir Lane made sure there was no final collapse, striking 13 not out from 7 while Nathan guided the last stretch. Ridge Falcons reached 199 for 7 in 36.4 overs, winning by three wickets, and the result fit the flow of the day. Bayridge had recovered well from early trouble and Aiden Cross gave them a genuine total, while Henry Brooks nearly made the chase awkward with the ball. But Ridge Falcons had the player who controlled both halves of the match. Leo Grant’s new-ball wickets damaged Bayridge’s top order, and his explosive 68 gave the chase a head start that Bayridge spent the rest of the afternoon trying to undo. Nathan Brook’s calm finish then converted that advantage into the win. This was not a clean chase from start to finish, but it was a better-managed one after the early assault, and Ridge Falcons were the side that owned the most valuable passages.
