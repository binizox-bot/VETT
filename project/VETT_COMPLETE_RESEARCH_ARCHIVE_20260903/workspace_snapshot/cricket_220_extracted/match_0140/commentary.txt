At Willowbank International Ground in Eastport, Northshore Women won the toss and chose to field first, and the match quickly settled into the kind of opening-day rhythm where the side batting first never completely collapsed, yet never quite found the one clean stretch that would let them dictate the match either. Bayport Women began with visible intent through Amelia Rowe, who struck a couple of early boundaries and gave the innings a little shape, but Nadia Cross removed her before that start could harden into a proper platform. Maya Vale came in and played briskly for a few balls, only for Erin Pike to bowl her just as Bayport looked ready to keep the powerplay moving. Iris Cole then became the third wicket, run out after a short, busy stay, and at that point Bayport were already in a familiar kind of trouble: enough runs to avoid panic, too many wickets lost to feel in control. Sofia Lane and Tara Bennett gave the innings its first meaningful repair. Sofia’s sixteen from seventeen was steady enough to stop the score from freezing, while Tara began more carefully and tried to stretch the innings into the middle overs. Their stand of twenty-five mattered because it shifted Bayport from early disorder into something at least usable, but Northshore kept hitting the innings before it could grow into comfort. Nadia returned to remove Sofia, and then the most important Bayport partnership came through Tara Bennett and Lena Brooks. It was not explosive, and that is precisely why it reflected the conditions of the match so well. Tara worked through the gaps for twenty-three, Lena gradually became the innings’ main stabilizer, and together they added thirty-three, the largest partnership of the Bayport card. That stand carried the score toward the final third of the innings, but even there Bayport were still searching for acceleration rather than building from command. Olivia Marsh removed Tara, and after Grace Ward gave Lena some brief support, Sarah Vale cut that stand off as well. Lena Brooks’ thirty from thirty-four ended up being the top score, and it was valuable because she held the lower middle together through a phase where another quick wicket would have left Bayport well short of even a competitive total. Still, once she was run out late, the innings lost its last real chance of a sharper finish. Ella Stone had already been run out, Ruby Hale and Paige Quinn added a small unbeaten push at the end, and Bayport reached one hundred and twenty-two for eight. The score had a little substance, mostly because of Lena, Tara, and those middle-over repair passages, yet it also carried the unmistakable feel of a side that had left fifteen or twenty runs behind. Northshore’s bowling was controlled in the right places. Nadia Cross struck twice in key phases, Erin Pike took the early tempo wicket, Olivia Marsh removed Tara before the final push, Sarah Vale added the wicket of Grace, and Leah Frost’s quiet four-over spell gave the innings very little loose space. Chasing one hundred and twenty-three, Northshore did not produce a perfect pursuit, but they had the one thing Bayport lacked: a batter who stayed from the start and kept the whole chase under control. Clara Woods was that player. The opening stand with Erin Pike was helped heavily by extras, and Bayport will look back at those early wides and leg-side misses with real frustration because they allowed Northshore to move well into the chase before a wicket had fallen. Erin herself made only eight, but she stayed long enough for Clara to read the pace of the pitch, and that was enough to make the target feel smaller than it should have. Tara Bennett then gave Bayport their strongest route back into the match. She removed Erin, bowled Nadia Cross not long after, and later returned to bowl Leah Frost, finishing with three for fourteen in a losing cause and almost single-handedly keeping Bayport emotionally alive. Sarah Vale also came and went for seven, caught off Paige Quinn, so at ninety-eight for four the chase still had a narrow opening. Clara, however, never let that opening become a real crack. Her unbeaten sixty-seven from forty-seven was the decisive innings of the match because it moved through every stage: the loose but useful opening, the wickets through the middle, the brief counter from Leah Frost, and then the final calm partnership with Mia Hart. She hit nine boundaries without needing to force the finish, and what made the innings so strong was its control rather than its violence. Whenever Bayport threatened to string dots together, Clara found the release. Whenever Tara’s spell pulled the field closer, Clara waited for the next softer option rather than giving away the mistake Bayport needed. Mia Hart’s unbeaten ten was also important in context, because once Leah fell Northshore still had work to do and needed a partner who would not turn a visible target into late panic. The final stand of twenty-five did exactly that, Clara carrying the scoring burden and Mia giving her enough calm support to finish the chase in the eighteenth over. Northshore reached one hundred and twenty-three for four and won by six wickets, and the result felt fair because Bayport had too many fragmented batting phases and Northshore had the one continuous hand that defined the chase. Tara Bennett’s bowling was excellent, Lena Brooks gave Bayport a workable first-innings spine, and Nadia Cross had earlier done the damage with the ball, but Clara Woods was the player who converted a modest target into a controlled win. Northshore did not dominate every over. They simply handled the important passages better: they kept Bayport below a truly threatening score, absorbed the one dangerous losing-side spell, and let Clara finish the job without allowing the match to become louder than it needed to be.
