Eastgate Sports Oval in Harbor City gave Brookfield XI and Stoneford XI one of those awkward mid-range contests where the target never looks huge on paper, yet every wicket and every quiet over keeps adding weight until the chase starts to feel much tighter than the score alone suggests. Brookfield won the toss, chose to field, and the first innings immediately justified the call in patches, because Stoneford never really found a clean top-order rhythm even though Aaron Holt opened with the sort of sharp little burst that can briefly disguise deeper problems. Holt’s 17 from 12 had real intent, two boundaries and a clean strike over the rope, but it was also too brief to become a platform, and when Lucas Shaw drew the chance to Nathan Frost, the innings was already starting to tilt toward Brookfield’s bowlers. Connor Vale tried to give the reply a more settled shape, Dylan Marsh gave him a small busy phase, and for a while Stoneford had just enough movement to avoid looking trapped, yet Ryan Cross kept cutting away the attempted repair. Marsh went first, then Connor followed after a patient but limited 15, and when Tyler Quinn was removed without scoring, Stoneford had slipped to 48 for 4 and the innings had lost almost all of its early confidence. Ethan Drake became the one batter who gave Stoneford a real spine. He did not rescue the innings with one reckless burst; he rebuilt it in pieces, first stopping the immediate slide, then giving Logan Pierce enough time to form the largest stand of the innings. Their 45-run partnership was the first passage where Brookfield had to work through overs rather than simply collect wickets, though even that recovery was not clean enough to fully transfer pressure back onto the fielding side. Logan made only 4, Ethan carried the main scoring, and the extras inside that stand showed how Stoneford were still relying on a few loose balls as much as full control from the bat. Nolan Hart broke that stand, and that mattered because Stoneford had finally begun to look as though they might push into a total with sharper teeth. Carter Hayes then stayed long enough to help Ethan add another useful block, and Gavin Reed added a short burst later, but Caleb Stone kept finding the break whenever Stoneford threatened to stretch the innings. Carter was bowled, Gavin was taken, and when Ethan finally went for 49, caught by Adrian Wells off Caleb Stone, the innings had lost the only batter who had made it feel organized. Julian Fox stayed there at the end, Cameron Ross swung hard enough to add a small tail-end lift, and Stoneford eventually reached 144 all out in 35.3 overs, a total that had some defendable quality because the surface was never completely easy, but one that also felt short once the card was read properly. Ethan Drake’s 49 was the clear center, Holt had supplied the early spark, Connor Vale had occupied a little time, and Cameron Ross had added a late flicker, yet the innings depended too heavily on isolated hands. Brookfield’s bowling had the more coherent structure: Cross removed two of the early pieces, Shaw made the first incision, Hart’s 3 for 18 pulled apart the middle and tail, Stone’s three wickets gave the innings its late shape, and Felix Rowe closed one of the remaining support acts. Chasing 145, Brookfield should have had enough, but Stoneford made sure it was not a casual walk. Logan Pierce was outstanding with the new ball, pinning Ethan Cole early, then coming back to remove Mason Blake, and with Gavin Reed trapping Noah Grant in between, Brookfield were 36 for 3 and the game had become genuinely alive. That was the dangerous moment. The target was still modest, but a modest target can become heavy when the top three are gone and the bowling side can crowd the bat. Nolan Hart changed that with the kind of innings that wins low chases because it does not confuse calm with passivity. He started by taking time, letting the ball come, and refusing to chase Stoneford’s fielding energy. Owen Price gave him exactly the support Brookfield needed: slow, disciplined, and stubborn, but still capable of finding the rope when Stoneford risked leaving the field too tight. Their fourth-wicket stand of 78 was the decisive section of the match, not because it was explosive, but because it took the chase from danger into control. Nolan made 37 in that stand, Price added 36, and by the time Carter Hayes finally removed Price at 114, the job had changed from rescue to completion. Stoneford still had one more push. Pierce bowled Caleb Stone and briefly made the ending look untidy, but Nolan was already too set to let the chase reopen. Ryan Cross came in and played the right finishing hand, short and clear, while Nolan moved through the last stage unbeaten, ending on 66 from 86 with four boundaries and five sixes. Brookfield reached 145 for 5 in 33.2 overs, and the five-wicket margin fits the day because they had to survive a real top-order wobble before taking command. Stoneford had enough bowling quality to create a contest, especially through Logan Pierce’s 3 for 27, but they did not have enough runs or enough sustained pressure once Nolan and Owen settled. Nolan Hart was the correct Player of the Match because his influence crossed both halves of the game: first he took three wickets to keep Stoneford’s innings from becoming safer, then he carried the chase from 36 for 3 all the way to the finish. Ethan Drake gave Stoneford dignity, Logan Pierce gave them hope, but Nolan Hart gave Brookfield the result.
