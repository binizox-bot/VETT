At Cedar Park Oval in Ashford, Stoneford chose to field first and for a few minutes that decision had real tactical weight, because Finn Mercer found the new-ball shape almost immediately, Blake Sutton gone before the innings had even settled and Mason Pike following not long after, leaving Northmoor in the kind of early trouble that can turn an ambitious batting choice into a long afternoon of repair work, but that early control disappeared once Aiden Doyle and Elias Vale began to read the surface, and the important thing about their partnership was not simply that it became huge, it was that it changed the whole emotional temperature of the match. Aiden did the first repair work with discipline, not flashy at the beginning, just tight enough outside off, firm enough when the ball was full, and aware enough to take the loose runs Stoneford were handing over through wides and no-balls. Elias Vale then turned the innings from recovery into a statement. His 94 from 62 was the central hand of the match, and it had the feel of a captain deciding that the two early wickets were not going to define the innings. He struck through the line when the seamers missed full, cut hard when width appeared, and kept finding a boundary just when Stoneford thought they had created a quiet over. The stand between Aiden and Elias reached 147, and by the time Carter Flynn finally removed Elias, Northmoor had gone from 26 for 2 to 173 for 3, which meant Stoneford’s early breakthrough had been overwritten almost completely. Aiden fell not long after for 58, but by then the innings had already been pushed into a position where the next phase could be played aggressively rather than cautiously. Dylan Kerr and Jasper Reed made sure that happened. Dylan’s 69 from 49 was not just a continuation of the scoring; it was the second wave that made the total genuinely severe. He came in with the field already stretched and immediately played like a wicketkeeper-batter who understood that the platform demanded pressure, not consolidation. Jasper Reed’s 35 from 23 gave him exactly the right kind of company, sharp, uncomplicated, and strong enough to prevent Stoneford from isolating Dylan. Their 93-run stand took Northmoor to 280, and that was the point where the match began to feel less like a contest and more like a question of how much damage Stoneford could limit. To their credit, Stoneford did not stop taking wickets. Carter Flynn removed Dylan and later Owen Clarke, Aiden Marsh cut out Jasper and Felix Dean, and Finn Mercer returned for Isaac Lowe, so the final scorecard carried a fair amount of late interruption. Yet those wickets came after Northmoor had already broken the innings open. Gavin Cross stayed there unbeaten on 21, Isaac Lowe struck a useful 15, and Nolan Price’s 10 from 6 made sure the last wicket still added value before Hudson Pike ended the innings on 342. Stoneford’s bowling figures showed effort without control. Finn Mercer took three and had the best early impact, Carter Flynn took three as well and kept fighting through the heavier scoring phases, Hudson Pike picked up two, and Aiden Marsh collected two late, but too many overs were surrounded by release balls. The extras were not just a detail; they were part of the pressure leak that allowed Northmoor to turn early risk into sustained dominance. Chasing 343, Stoneford needed a near-perfect beginning or at least one long top-order stand, and for a brief spell Brandon Holt made the reply look louder than it had any right to be. He attacked Nolan Price hard, threw the bat cleanly through the line, and his 34 from 24 gave Stoneford their only genuinely defiant passage of the innings. Aiden Marsh was slower at the other end, but while Brandon was striking cleanly, the opening stand had enough movement to stop the chase from feeling dead immediately. Gavin Cross changed that by bowling Aiden, and Nolan Price then removed Brandon with Felix Dean taking the catch. That second wicket was the real emotional break. Once Brandon went, Stoneford no longer had a batter forcing Northmoor to adjust. Carter Flynn was bowled by Jasper Reed, Elliot Kane followed to the same bowler, and Declan Shaw was taken out by Gavin Cross, leaving the chase split open before it had reached any kind of middle-overs structure. Finn Mercer tried to stay, but his 7 from 25 was a survival innings rather than a chase innings, and Gideon Park’s unbeaten 13 became more of a holding line than a rebuilding platform. Their stand added only 10, and that said everything about the state of Stoneford’s reply: time was passing, but the target was not being reduced in any meaningful way. Isaac Lowe then removed Hudson Pike and Ivan Cross, Jasper Reed took Jonah Wells, and Gavin Cross returned to close the innings by bowling Kieran Ford. Stoneford were all out for 96, and the size of the defeat was not artificial. It came from Northmoor owning every decisive phase after the first few overs. Their batting had recovered from early danger through Aiden and Elias, accelerated again through Dylan and Jasper, and then kept scoring through the lower order. Their bowling then spread the pressure cleanly: Gavin Cross with 3 for 8 gave the chase its strongest squeeze, Jasper Reed’s 3 for 13 cut through the middle, Isaac Lowe’s two wickets prevented any lower-order patch, and Nolan Price’s dismissal of Brandon Holt removed the only batter who had briefly made the reply look alive. Stoneford had two moments in the match, Finn Mercer’s new-ball burst and Brandon Holt’s counterattack, but neither lasted long enough to become a phase. Northmoor had repeated phases, and that is why the result became so one-sided. Elias Vale was the correct player of the match because his 94 was the innings that transformed 26 for 2 into command, and once that transformation happened, everything after it moved in Northmoor’s direction.
