Good morning from Brookmere Cricket Ground in North Valley, with Daniel Foster and Peter Lane already set in the middle and Emily Carter watching the formal pieces from the side, and Ridgefield CC’s choice to bat first began to feel uncomfortable almost as soon as the new ball started asking its questions, because this was not one of those bright mornings where the first ten overs simply glide by and the openers build without consequence. Ethan Marsh kept the first end tight enough to make every forward press feel like a decision, Jacob Stone found the fuller length that kept bringing bat and pad into the conversation, and Ridgefield’s opening movement came as much from Valleygate’s occasional looseness as from anything truly imposed by the batters. Noah Pierce was the first to go, pinned in front before he had ever looked settled, and Adrian Wells, after one decent stroke and a little survival work, followed soon after when Stone drew the catch that made the top order look fragile rather than merely cautious. From there the innings quickly narrowed. Caleb Ross came and went without scoring, Ryan Cole only briefly interrupted the pressure, and at 19 for 4 the whole batting plan had already collapsed into the much smaller job of staying alive. Lucas Bennett became the one constant, not fluent, not dominant, but stubborn enough to make the innings last longer than it felt capable of lasting, and when Mason Doyle joined him there was at least a short passage where Ridgefield looked like they might drag the match into something resembling a contest. Doyle’s 10 was modest, but in context it mattered because he gave Bennett the only partnership that had any real body to it, the pair taking the score past the worst of the early wreckage and forcing Valleygate to work through a slightly longer spell rather than simply collect wickets in clusters. That was exactly where Mason Hale changed the innings from damaged to nearly broken. He removed Doyle when that stand was beginning to look like a small repair, then went through Owen Scott soon after, and the feel of the match shifted again because Ridgefield had not merely lost another batter, they had lost the one stretch in which the score had been moving with any shape. Tyler Quinn gave Bennett the next bit of company and pushed the total into the sixties, but Samuel Cross bowled him before that stand could grow into a true recovery, and once Finn Carter was also removed the tail was almost out of road. Hale returned to take Liam Brooks and then Jack Reed, leaving Bennett stranded on 21 not out and Ridgefield all out for 74 in 24.5 overs. The total was not disastrous only because Bennett refused to disappear and because the extras column kept contributing in small, untidy ways, but the batting itself had produced very little control: one not-out hand, one brief captain’s stand, Tyler’s small resistance, and too many entries that ended before the innings could rebuild around them. Valleygate’s attack was balanced in the way low-score bowling performances need to be. Marsh started the squeeze, Stone did the major early damage with three wickets, Cross made sure the lower order could not stretch the total into a more awkward range, and Hale’s 4 for 17 gave the innings its decisive shape. Chasing 75, Valleygate still needed to avoid the one thing small targets can create, which is self-inflicted panic, and Oliver Grant plus Aaron Blake began with enough calm to make Ridgefield work for their first lift. Grant did fall to Mason Doyle, but the opening stand had already taken the target out of the most dangerous new-ball pocket. Aaron Blake then played the firmer early hand, and with Kevin Hall he moved Valleygate to 50 before Tyler Quinn removed him. There was a tiny flicker when Ethan Marsh was trapped shortly after, the score only just past the halfway line in emotional terms even if the target was small, but Hall never let Ridgefield turn that flicker into a final act. He stayed clear, picked the right balls to hit, and once Brian Lewis arrived the finish became sudden. Lewis’ unbeaten 19 from five balls changed the chase from controlled to emphatic, a couple of clean boundary blows turning what might have been a careful ten-over job into a quick close. Hall stayed not out on 28, Lewis finished beside him, and Valleygate reached 76 for 3 in 10.2 overs. Ridgefield had their moments with the ball through Doyle and Quinn, but defending 74 requires near-perfection, and they did not have enough runs or enough wickets in hand on the scoreboard to make Valleygate truly doubt the chase. The match therefore resolves cleanly: Ridgefield chose to bat, were broken open by Stone and then finished by Hale, Lucas Bennett alone holding the innings together from inside the collapse; Valleygate lost three wickets in reply, but Kevin Hall’s calm and Brian Lewis’ sharp finish made the chase feel properly under control. Mason Hale was the correct player of the match because his spell cut through the only Ridgefield passages that looked as though they might develop into recovery, and once that innings had been held to 74, the rest of the match was mostly about Valleygate completing a job their bowlers had already made small.
