Ridgegate won the toss at Redstone Cricket Ground in Mirrendale and chose to field, but Mariner Coast made that decision feel uncomfortable almost immediately because Aiden Ross and Nolan Pierce produced the kind of opening partnership that does more than start an innings quickly; it changes the entire emotional pressure of the match before the bowling side has found a stable plan. Aiden was the more rounded aggressor, strong through the off side, quick to pick up anything short, and composed enough to keep scoring even when Ridgegate briefly pulled the length back. Nolan was more sudden, more violent in bursts, and his four sixes gave the stand the heavy scoring shape that forced the field to keep spreading earlier than Ridgegate wanted. The first wicket did not fall until the thirteenth over, and by then the opening stand had already reached one hundred and thirty-nine. That was the first decisive phase of the match. Ridgegate had not bowled without effort; Warren Blake tried to hit the pitch hard, Zack Dean kept searching for a controlled line, and Xavier Holt had one or two overs where the scoring looked less automatic. But neither opener let pressure settle. When Zack finally removed Aiden for seventy-four, Ridgegate had the breakthrough but not yet the recovery. Nolan stayed a little longer with Evan Vale, and that second stand kept the innings moving into the back end rather than allowing Ridgegate to build a squeeze from the wicket. Warren Blake eventually removed Nolan for sixty-four, but Evan had already started to read the stage of the innings. His role was very different from the openers. He arrived with the platform made and treated the final overs as a hitting window, not as a period for careful repair. His sixty-four from twenty-eight balls carried seven sixes, and that tells the story of the late innings more accurately than any general phrase could. He was not nudging Mariner Coast toward a strong total; he was launching them into a severe one. Marcus Reid’s unbeaten fourteen was not huge, but it mattered because he gave Evan the support and strike flow needed for the death overs to keep moving. Quentin Hale did at least give Ridgegate a late cluster of wickets, removing Evan, Kieran Moss, and Carter Miles in the final over sequence, and Felix Grant was run out in between, but those dismissals arrived after the main damage had already been inflicted. Mariner Coast closed on two hundred and thirty-one for six, with a giant opening stand, a short continuation after the first wicket, and a brutal late push from Evan. Ridgegate’s bowling card showed moments of resistance, especially Zack’s control and Quentin’s wicket-taking finish, but it also showed how badly the innings had been stretched: Xavier and Sawyer were hit hard, Oscar Kent could not alter the flow, and the early inability to separate Aiden and Nolan left the entire chase facing a huge ask. Ridgegate began that chase with the correct attitude. Aaron Blake and Theo Cole did not try to tiptoe toward the target. Aaron, in particular, made the first few overs feel alive with two clean sixes, and Theo settled into the kind of supporting rhythm that gave the innings enough structure to match the intent. Hugo Clarke broke that opening stand by trapping Aaron in front, but Ridgegate kept coming. Oscar Kent played a short attacking hand, and although Mason Clarke removed him, the chase had already moved briskly enough to keep Mariner Coast from settling into defensive comfort. Then came the stand that genuinely threatened to pull the match toward Ridgegate. Theo Cole and Quentin Hale added seventy-five, and the contrast between them made the partnership dangerous. Theo’s thirty-five was the more stabilizing hand, full of controlled boundary options and enough strike rotation to keep the innings coherent. Quentin was the destroyer. His fifty-six from twenty-three balls, with seven sixes, made the target suddenly look far less secure than Mariner Coast would have expected after posting such a large total. For several overs, Ridgegate were not merely keeping up. They were making Mariner Coast search for answers. Ian Foster eventually cut through Theo, and that wicket mattered because it removed the anchor of the partnership. Hugo then delivered the biggest ball of the chase when he bowled Quentin. That was the turning point. Ridgegate had been threatening to make the huge target feel ordinary, and Hugo removed the one batter capable of doing that in a few more overs. The pressure shifted at once. Riley Brooks fell trying to keep the momentum alive, Evan Vale taking the wicket with Nolan Pierce completing the catch, and Hugo struck again when Parker Shaw was caught by Nolan before the captain could rebuild the innings around the lower order. Those wickets changed the match from a live chase into a chase that needed another rescue. Sawyer Grant tried to supply a short counter, but Evan removed him as well, and Ridgegate were suddenly seven down with the rate still demanding more than simple survival. Theo Bishop and Warren Blake did not fold. Their final stand of thirty-six gave the innings a more respectable finish and kept Mariner Coast honest until the very last ball. Theo stayed unbeaten on twenty-three, Warren struck two late sixes, and for a little while there was still enough motion in the scoreboard to prevent the ending from becoming flat. Marcus Reid closed it by removing Warren from the final ball, and Ridgegate finished on one hundred and ninety-seven for eight, thirty-four short. The margin was clean, but the match itself had carried real danger through the middle overs. Mariner Coast won because their batting had two decisive layers: the Aiden-Nolan stand that built the platform and Evan Vale’s late hitting that pushed the total beyond normal reach. They then defended it because Hugo Clarke struck at the exact points where Ridgegate’s chase needed continuity. His wickets were not decorative. Aaron’s wicket slowed the first burst, Quentin’s wicket removed the central threat, and Parker’s wicket prevented the reset. That is why Hugo as Player of the Match fits the reconstructed match even in a game full of huge batting numbers. Aiden, Nolan, and Evan built the mountain; Hugo made sure Ridgegate could not climb it. Ridgegate had more than fragments in reply, especially through Quentin and Theo, and they were dangerous enough for long enough to make the contest feel alive. But once the key partnership was split and Hugo cut through the chase’s middle, Mariner Coast had enough scoreboard pressure and enough late control to finish the job.
