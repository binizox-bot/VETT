At North Meridian Academy Ground in Westport, this match settled into a very clear pattern long before the winning run was struck, because Ridgefield did just enough in one long early partnership to suggest they might build a defendable fifty-over total, yet Westport kept cutting the innings back before any second layer could properly form, and once the chase began Owen Carter made the whole target look smaller with an unbeaten hundred that turned a potentially patient pursuit into a rapid nine-wicket win. Ridgefield were asked to bat first and their opening few overs were uncomfortable almost immediately. Jay Mason found one early boundary but never looked settled for long, and when Felix Ward bowled him at 10 for 1 the innings had the feel of a side that would need someone to bat through a long stretch just to make the score respectable. Owen Hart and Noah Vale gave them exactly that first repair. Their second-wicket stand of 72 was the only truly substantial partnership Ridgefield produced, and for a while it gave the innings genuine shape. Owen Hart was the more fluent of the pair, using the bad ball better and getting to 35 from 55 with four boundaries and a six, while Noah Vale played the more patient hand, absorbing balls, taking fewer risks, and giving Ridgefield the kind of occupation that matters in youth fifty-over cricket. By the time that stand carried the score to 82 for 2, Ridgefield had recovered from the early strike and could still imagine something around 180 if the middle order supported the set batter. Westport did not let that happen. Felix Ward removed Owen Hart at the end of the stand, Connor Dale soon bowled Eli Turner, and then Noah Vale himself was caught after reaching 38 from 78, which meant the innings had moved from one steady platform into another restart. That was the key difference between a workable total and a thin one. Ridgefield kept finding small pieces, but never the next deep partnership. Mason Reed and Caleb Flynn put on 25, and Caleb’s 26 from 32 was the one later innings that briefly gave the score some movement, yet Aaron Pike removed Mason before that stand could stretch and Ethan Brooks then began to cut through the lower half. Toby Grant went without scoring, Caleb Flynn fell at 134, Luca Hayes could not push beyond a couple, and Finn Cole was bowled with the score stuck. Ryan Moss stayed there to the end, but by then Ridgefield were not building so much as trying to survive enough balls to drag the score past 140. Adam Brooks’ final wicket left them 143 all out in 43.4 overs, and the card told a fairly harsh story: one meaningful stand, one slow top-order anchor, one brisk lower-middle hand, then repeated breaks. Westport’s bowling was not built around one freak spell alone. Felix Ward gave them the early and second-wicket breakthroughs. Connor Dale controlled the middle with two important wickets. Aaron Pike’s three wickets damaged the lower middle. Ethan Brooks finished with three of his own and made sure the innings did not limp to a more awkward target. Owen Carter’s own four overs were tidy, Jacob Stone held a containing role, and the whole attack gave Westport a target that looked manageable as long as the chase did not begin with panic. It did not. Kai Morgan and Owen Carter opened with clear intent, and although Kai’s 12 from 19 was only a supporting hand, he gave Owen enough company for the first part of the chase to become almost completely pressure-free. The opening partnership reached 64 in 8.4 overs, and that was already enough to take most of the bite out of Ridgefield’s defense. Owen was the reason. He did not bat as though 144 required careful crawling. He batted as though the only danger was allowing Ridgefield to believe. His first fifty came inside the opening stand, full of strong driving, clean work through the leg side, and a willingness to punish anything short or overpitched. Luca Hayes eventually had Kai Morgan caught by Ryan Moss, but the wicket came too late to change the emotional direction of the innings, because Westport were already almost halfway there and Owen Carter was fully settled. Ben Hart then played the perfect finishing partner’s role. His unbeaten 30 from 29 was calm, useful, and properly paced for the situation. He did not need to match Owen shot for shot. He needed to keep the board moving, avoid a cluster of wickets, and make sure Ridgefield could not isolate the set batter. That is exactly what happened. Their unbroken stand of 82 finished the match with increasing certainty, Owen moving all the way to 101 not out from 67 balls, with fourteen fours and a six, and by the time he crossed three figures the chase had stopped feeling like a contest and had become a statement. Ridgefield’s bowlers had no sustained answer. Toby Grant and Adam Brooks were expensive, Luca Hayes at least took the only wicket, Ryan Moss and Finn Cole tried to keep some control, and Mason Reed’s short spell arrived when the game had already been largely decided. The contrast between the two innings was severe. Ridgefield needed 43.4 overs to reach 143 and relied heavily on one partnership between Owen Hart and Noah Vale. Westport needed only 19.1 overs to go beyond the target and had the one innings of real authority in the match. Owen Carter’s player-of-the-match award is therefore straightforward. Westport’s bowlers set up the win with disciplined pressure and regular wickets, but Carter’s unbeaten hundred removed every remaining uncertainty and transformed a modest chase into a nine-wicket demolition. Ridgefield had a phase of resistance, not control. Westport had both: first with the ball, then emphatically with the bat.
