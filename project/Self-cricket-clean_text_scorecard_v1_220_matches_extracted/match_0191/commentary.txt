At Briarfield School Oval in East Ashton, Ashford won the toss, sent Stonebay in, and then produced the kind of low-score squeeze that does not need one spectacular over to look severe because every phase of the innings kept narrowing the batting side’s options. Noah Pike made the first incision, bowling Tom Reed before Stonebay had found any rhythm, and from that point the innings never had a normal starting point. Kai Mercer, the captain, tried to absorb pressure at one end, but almost everything around him was either motionless or unstable. Arun Hale came in and was soon run out by Evan Hart, and that fielding involvement mattered because Stonebay were not only being beaten by the ball; they were being forced into decisions so uncertain that even small pushes into the field became dangerous. Kai and Nolan Frost then added what looked briefly like a little resistance, but it was only seven runs, one of them an extra, and it ended with Kai himself run out for six. That six became the highest score of the innings, which says nearly everything about how completely Ashford had locked the game down. Nolan Frost was trapped by Leo Marsh, Ben Ellis tried to stand for a while without changing the pressure, and then the innings entered the spell that decided the match. Leo Marsh did not simply collect wickets at the end of a collapse; he created the middle of it. He removed Nolan, bowled Evan Rowe, had Milo Grant stumped by Mason Voss, and then bowled Rafi Stone, turning a side already struggling at seventeen for four into twenty for seven with no sign of a batter capable of changing the innings. Ryan Cole’s spell at the other end made that burst even harsher because he gave almost nothing away, five overs for only five runs, three maidens, and the wicket of Ben Ellis, the one lower-order batter who had at least survived into the twenties of the innings. Dylan Shore and Carter Wells added the largest stand of the innings, but even that was only eight, and five of those were extras, so it was less a recovery than a brief delay created by Ashford’s few loose balls. Owen Hale then closed the innings by removing Dylan and Hugo Lane, leaving Carter Wells not out on four and Stonebay all out for thirty-four in the twenty-fifth over. The scorecard is brutal when read closely. Stonebay made only twenty-one runs from the bat. Extras supplied thirteen. No batter reached double figures. The fall of wickets went one, six, thirteen, seventeen, eighteen, twenty, twenty, twenty-three, thirty-one, thirty-four, and no partnership ever reached a number that could make Ashford reconsider their attacking fields. Ashford’s bowling worked as a unit, but Leo Marsh was the obvious center of it: nine overs, five maidens, eight runs, four wickets, and the spell that changed Stonebay from merely under pressure into completely broken. Noah Pike started it, Ryan Cole strangled it, Arjun Wells denied release, and Owen Hale finished it. Chasing thirty-five, Ashford did not need to take risks, but Evan Hart and Mason Voss still played with enough clarity to finish the match before any small-target awkwardness could appear. Evan was the more assertive hand, twenty-one not out from fifteen, three boundaries and a six, and he made sure every loose ball disappeared quickly enough that Stonebay could not settle into defending the tiny total. Mason Voss played the quieter support role, nine not out from twelve, steady enough to keep the stand unbroken while Evan did the louder scoring. Stonebay’s bowlers could not create even a symbolic breakthrough. Dylan Shore’s two overs cost seventeen, Carter Wells’ over went for eight, and Hugo Lane’s short spell finished the game while also giving up the no-ball that helped the chase move faster. Ashford reached thirty-five without loss in the fifth over, and the ten-wicket margin reflects the real balance of the match. Stonebay never built an innings, never found a batter to take ownership, and never made the chase feel like anything more than the final formality after Ashford’s fielding and bowling had already decided the contest. Leo Marsh was the correct Player of the Match because his spell sat at the center of the collapse, but the win also depended on the broader pressure around him: Noah’s new-ball strike, Evan Hart’s run-out involvement and chase finish, Ryan Cole’s suffocating economy, Mason Voss’ stumping and unbeaten support, and Owen Hale’s closing wickets. It was a full-team shutdown expressed through one extremely low total and one clean, wicketless chase.
