| L1 | Direct extraction | Basic match facts can be identified directly from the table and commentary. | Seabrook won by 252 runs; Ethan Clarke was Player of the Match; Seabrook made 304/5 and Highland Isle were all out for 52. |
| L1 | Key player identification | Main performers are visible without complex calculation. | Ethan Clarke scored 127; Noah Brooks made 74; Owen Price took three wickets; Connor Blake and Adam Wells took two each. |
| L2 | Cricket-rule arithmetic | Requires checking batting runs, extras, innings totals, and result margin. | Seabrook batting runs add to 269 and extras add 35, giving 304; Highland Isle batting runs add to 36 and extras add 16, giving 52; 304 minus 52 gives the 252-run margin. |
| L2 | Partnership and FOW consistency | Requires cumulative verification across partnership and fall-of-wickets tables. | Seabrook partnerships move through 24, 225, 248, 281, 296, and 304; Highland Isle partnerships move through 7, 17, 21, 29, 32, 37, 41, 47, 49, and 52. |
| L2 | Bowling reconciliation | Requires understanding that byes and leg-byes are not charged to bowlers. | Highland Isle bowlers concede 299 bowler-charged runs while Seabrook total 304 because five runs are byes/leg-byes; Seabrook bowlers concede 47 charged runs while Highland Isle total 52 because five runs are byes/leg-byes. |
| L3 | Commentary-hidden inference | Key facts are embedded through match rhythm rather than repeated as dense scoreboard strings. | The text describes a massive second-wicket stand, a late acceleration, a suffocated chase, and a lower-order collapse without mechanically listing every wicket score. |
| L3 | Narrative-to-table validation | Readers must connect descriptive phrases back to table evidence. | “A two-hundred-run central stand,” “the chase moving more through extras than authority,” and “Owen Price cutting through the lower order” all point to specific table fields. |
