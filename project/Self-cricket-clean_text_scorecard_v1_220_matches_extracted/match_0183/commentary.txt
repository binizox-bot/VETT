At Meadowbank School Oval in Westhaven, Northvale chose to field first, and for a few overs that decision had enough surface logic to avoid looking reckless, because Leo Grant found the first breakthrough before Seabrook had fully stretched their legs, Finn Archer flashing early, striking cleanly enough to give the innings a bright edge, and then falling with the score still in the thirties. But that was almost the last moment of the day when Northvale could honestly say the match was developing on their terms, because once Miles Carter and Oliver Kent came together, the entire tempo changed from a loose opening exchange into a long, controlled occupation that slowly squeezed the fielding side out of the contest. Miles played the quieter hand, and that mattered. He was not there to dominate every over; he was there to make sure Seabrook did not waste the platform after losing Finn, and he did that with a patient half-century built around soft hands, checked drives, and enough strike rotation to stop the bowlers from locking onto Oliver at one end. Oliver, meanwhile, played the innings that decided the match long before the formal result arrived. His hundred was not a wild swing through the line; it was a layered innings, first settling beside Miles, then gradually taking more control as the middle overs opened, and finally making Northvale pay whenever they missed their length or fed him width. The second-wicket stand reached one hundred and fifty-four, and that is the key hinge of the whole scorecard, because after Finn’s early dismissal Northvale needed one more wicket quickly to justify the toss. Instead, they spent more than twenty-five overs chasing the same two batters around the field, giving away wides, losing control of small moments, and watching a manageable beginning turn into a platform that already looked heavy. Samir Hale eventually bowled Miles for a well-made fifty-four, and he then removed Dylan Shaw after a brief partnership, but those wickets came after the damage had already been framed. Oliver stayed long enough to make a proper hundred and then just a little more, and when Rowan Ellis finally had him caught behind for one hundred and eight, Northvale had a short opening with Seabrook four down and the innings needing a finish. That opening did not become a recovery. Isaac Lane and Theo Marsh kept the board moving, not spectacularly, but usefully, and Theo’s tempo changed the final stretch because he was willing to attack the gaps rather than simply preserve wickets. Aaron Pike then gave the innings its sharpest late kick, and that was the phase that made the total feel truly severe rather than merely strong. He came in with the field pushed back, read the death overs quickly, and turned short scoring windows into clean damage, striking thirty-four from only seventeen balls while Ben Lowell stayed calm at the other end. Seabrook closed on three hundred and six for seven, a total built from three distinct layers: Finn’s short early spark, the enormous Miles-Oliver rebuild, and Aaron’s late acceleration. Northvale’s bowling card showed effort, yet not enough control. Samir Hale was the tidiest of their wicket-takers, Leo Grant had two important moments, Rowan Ellis removed Oliver and Theo, and Caleb Ross found a middle-order wicket, but the attack as a whole kept leaking pressure through wides, no-balls, and expensive pockets. The extras were not a side note. They were part of the story, because twenty-eight additional runs gave Seabrook breathing room during the long stand and helped keep the final total above the psychological line that turns a difficult chase into a suffocating one. Northvale’s reply needed a top-order partnership, preferably an urgent one, and it got neither. Leo Grant was bowled early by Ben Lowell, Hugo Price was trapped in front by Oliver Kent without scoring, and Caleb Ross, the captain, was caught soon after from Ben’s bowling, leaving the chase damaged almost before it had begun. Samir Hale and Rowan Ellis did attempt a repair, and for a while it looked like Northvale might at least stretch the game into a more respectable shape, but the problem was obvious: the runs were not coming from the bat quickly enough. Rowan occupied the crease, Samir resisted, extras kept the board from freezing entirely, yet there was no real counterattack, no batter forcing Seabrook to change plans, no partnership large enough to put even mild pressure back on the fielding side. Finn Archer removed Samir, Max Field stumped out Rowan through Theo Marsh’s quick work, and by then Northvale were already playing only for survival. Nolan Pierce and Ishan Cole put together the one later stand that had any weight, and Ishan eventually became the top scorer with eighteen, but that fact almost says more about the collapse than about the recovery. Nobody reached twenty. Nobody made Seabrook pull the field apart. Every time a batter appeared to settle, another over passed without enough scoring, and the required chase became less a target than a distant number sitting above the innings. Dylan Shaw then broke the middle and lower order open, bowling Nolan, removing Tariq Sen without addition, and later taking Ishan as the last meaningful Northvale batter. Aaron Pike cleaned up the tail with sharp, simple bowling, and the final score of one hundred and one all out felt entirely in line with the match’s rhythm. Seabrook were not perfect with the ball, because they also gave away a sizeable extras column, but Northvale’s own batting was so thin that the extra runs only softened the visual margin rather than changing the contest. The batters themselves produced just sixty-nine, and against a target above three hundred that is not a chase; it is a slow unraveling. The difference between the sides was therefore clear in every phase. Seabrook lost an opener and built a match-owning second-wicket stand. Northvale lost early wickets and never built anything close to a reply. Seabrook had Oliver Kent’s hundred, Miles Carter’s stabilizing fifty, Aaron Pike’s late burst, and then enough bowling discipline from Dylan Shaw, Ben Lowell, Finn Archer, Max Field, and Aaron himself to close the innings without stress. Northvale had scattered resistance, a few tidy bowling spells, and far too many releases with the ball and bat. Oliver Kent was the correct Player of the Match because his one hundred and eight formed the center of the innings, his early wicket kept Northvale from settling, and his presence defined both halves of a one-sided game. Seabrook won by two hundred and five runs, and the margin feels earned because the match was not decided by one sudden collapse alone; it was built through sustained batting control first, then confirmed by a chase that never developed a coherent shape.
