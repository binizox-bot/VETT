Brookfield did not begin with fluency. Brookfield were two down with the innings still narrow, the new ball had not been wasted, and the fielding side could still imagine forcing a total built on scraps rather than shape. That was where Carter Blake and Dorian Shaw changed the match. Their partnership did not explode immediately, and that is why it mattered. It began as repair, then became accumulation, then slowly turned into control. So the result belonged to Brookfield because they were stronger in the two decisive disciplines of this match: they built the one major partnership of the day with Carter Blake and Dorian Shaw, then defended by never allowing Northmoor to build one of equal weight. Brookfield’s innings had its ugly parts, and the tail was held back sharply by Logan Cross, but the total had enough substance through Carter, Dorian, Elias and the late scraps from Isaac and Jude. Northmoor’s chase had effort everywhere and certainty almost nowhere. That is why Finn Grant was the right player of the match. His batting barely mattered, but his bowling controlled the second innings from its first serious moment to its last meaningful one, and by the time the final wicket fell Brookfield had turned a defendable total into a clear, deserved win.
