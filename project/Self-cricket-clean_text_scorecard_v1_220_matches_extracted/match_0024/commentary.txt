At Northlake Park Ground in Lakeside, with the final carrying that slightly sharper noise around every misfield and every dot ball, Crest Women A chose to bat first and built the kind of title-winning innings that never felt completely safe while it was happening, yet kept finding just enough structure whenever Delta Women A threatened to drag it back. Ariana Cole and Mia Turner gave Crest the opening they needed without turning the first six overs into anything reckless, Ariana taking the calmer captain’s route, waiting for the ball in her areas and getting two clean boundaries away, while Mia was the more visible tempo-setter, using the straight hit early and lifting one over the infield so that Delta could not settle into a pure squeeze. That opening stand moved a little past thirty and, importantly, most of it came from the bat rather than gifts, so Crest had earned their first base before Lauren Reed finally got through Ariana. Mia stayed just long enough with Sienna Brooks to keep that base alive, but Emma Foster’s breakthrough removed the keeper before the innings had really opened up, and that left the middle overs balanced in an interesting way: Crest had a platform, yet Delta still had enough control to believe one more wicket could make the whole thing awkward. Sienna was the player who made that harder for them. She batted with the cleanest rhythm of Crest’s top half, not wildly, not with panic, but with the confidence of someone prepared to punish anything slightly short or overpitched, and the stand with Tara Hayes gave Crest their next useful block. Tara’s own scoring was modest, and yes, a few byes helped that passage, but her stay mattered because it allowed Sienna to keep the innings pointed forward. Nora Brooks removed Tara and later Sienna, and the second of those wickets was a real turning point because Sienna’s thirty-three had been the innings’ most fluent hand. Olivia Reed had already arrived by then, though, and this is where the final began to tilt toward Crest in a quieter way. Olivia did not try to solve the innings with one swing. She worked into it, took the safe option when the ball deserved respect, and then began to find the rope often enough to keep Delta from crowding the new batters. The dangerous phase for Crest came immediately after Sienna fell. Ella Ward was run out, Chloe Foster went without scoring, and Grace Bennett could not stay long either, so a score that had looked as if it might rush past the mid-one-thirties suddenly sat with seven wickets down and the finish still not fully shaped. That was the pressure point of the innings. Olivia’s value was not just her not-out score; it was the fact that she stayed calm while the innings around her frayed. Nina Parker then gave the late support Crest badly needed, a short cameo with one boundary and enough clean contact to make the last two overs feel profitable rather than merely survived. Even after Nina was caught near the close, Ruby Collins held her end for the last ball and Olivia remained there, unbeaten on thirty-two from twenty-four, taking Crest to one hundred and thirty-four for eight. It was not a total built from domination. It was layered: an opening stand that gave the innings footing, Sienna’s middle-over fluency, a wobble that nearly damaged the finish, and Olivia’s late hand with Nina’s cameo making the score feel properly defendable. Delta’s bowlers had not failed. Lauren Reed was excellent for control and removed both Ariana and Chloe. Nora Brooks took out Tara and, more importantly, Sienna. Emma Foster’s spell stayed tidy and brought Mia. Sophie Clarke got Grace at the moment Delta were trying to keep the back end pinned. Lucy Bennett was expensive earlier but came back to remove Nina. The problem for Delta was that every time they seemed close to finishing a phase, Crest found one more piece of batting. Chasing one hundred and thirty-five in a final, Delta Women A needed a clean start and then one partnership with enough length to make Crest defend under scoreboard pressure. They got little flashes, not continuity. Hannah Blake gave them the first flash, striking briskly enough to make the opening overs feel alive, but Ruby Collins removed Ava Cross before the stand could settle and then took Hannah herself with a caught-and-bowled dismissal. That mattered because Delta lost both the anchor possibility and the early scoring pulse inside the first four overs. Megan Price then became the chase’s main organizing batter. She was not explosive, but she was the one who looked most capable of making a plan hold for more than a few deliveries, finding two boundaries and trying to give the innings a shape through the middle. Sarah Hayes briefly joined her with one boundary, but Chloe Foster’s introduction changed the chase sharply. Chloe bowled the Delta captain, then removed Zoe Turner, and suddenly the pursuit had shifted from structured chase to damage management. Paige Ward offered a short burst, including the one clean hit over the rope that briefly lifted Delta’s mood, but Olivia Reed produced the wicket that most clearly justified her match award, getting Megan caught by Chloe Foster just when Megan was the one batter who could still make the chase feel organised. Ruby Collins then removed Paige, and Crest had taken away almost every route Delta had to a stable pursuit. Lauren Reed fought hard from there, finishing unbeaten on eighteen, and her effort deserves credit because she kept the lower order from folding immediately. Emma Foster helped her through the best late stand, a partnership inflated a little by extras but still valuable because it delayed Crest’s celebration and made them keep bowling properly. Chloe Foster, though, kept returning with the kind of wickets that stop resistance becoming danger. She trapped Emma, later removed Lucy Bennett through a catch by Leah Morgan, and finished with four wickets for fourteen, the sharpest defensive spell of the final. Olivia Reed also struck again by having Nora Brooks stumped by Mia Turner, and Nina Parker closed the match by drawing the final catch from Sophie Clarke. Delta were all out for one hundred and six with two balls unused, which left Crest winners by twenty-eight runs and gave the final its proper shape: close enough in phases to feel contested, clear enough at the end to feel earned. Delta had Hannah’s early push, Megan’s middle-order attempt, Paige’s small spark, Lauren’s unbeaten lower-order resistance, and a useful bowling effort from Lauren and Nora earlier in the day. What they never had was a partnership that survived long enough to reshape the match. Crest, by contrast, kept finding the right intervention at the right moment. Olivia Reed held the first innings together after the late slide and then removed Megan, the key Delta batter, during the chase. Ruby Collins cracked the top half open. Chloe Foster cut through the middle and lower order. Nina Parker finished the match. That is why Crest Women A were the winners: not because they overwhelmed Delta from start to finish, but because in every pressure pocket of the final, they found one more player who could make the next useful play before Delta could turn a fragment into control.
