| L1 | Direct extraction | Basic match facts can be read directly from the table and commentary. | Highridge won by 102 runs; Aron Cole was Player of the Match; Highridge made 181/8 and Bayfront were all out for 79. |
| L1 | Key player identification | Main contributors remain visible without multi-step calculation. | Lucas Byrne made 42, Evan Park made 37, Ryan Foster and Dylan Price repaired the late innings, and Aron Cole took three wickets after a late cameo. |
| L2 | Cricket-rule arithmetic | Requires checking batting runs, extras, innings totals, and victory margin. | Highridge batting runs add to 168 and extras add 13, giving 181; Bayfront batting runs add to 64 and extras add 15, giving 79; 181 minus 79 gives the 102-run margin. |
| L2 | Partnership and FOW consistency | Requires cumulative verification across partnerships and fall-of-wickets tables. | Highridge partnerships move through 60, 69, 84, 98, 128, 134, 169, 178, and 181; Bayfront partnerships move through 13, 45, 50, 51, 59, 62, 70, 70, 77, and 79. |
| L2 | Bowling reconciliation | Requires understanding that byes and leg-byes are not charged to bowlers. | Bayfront bowlers concede 178 bowler-charged runs while Highridge total 181 because three runs are byes/leg-byes; Highridge bowlers concede 77 charged runs while Bayfront total 79 because two runs are leg-byes. |
| L2 | Ball-count consistency | Requires checking legal balls against innings length. | Highridge batters face 210 legal balls across 35.0 overs; Bayfront batters face 185 legal balls across 30.5 overs. |
| L3 | Commentary-hidden inference | Key facts are embedded through pressure, rhythm, and turning points instead of dense scoreboard repetition. | The text presents Highridge’s opening base, Bayfront’s middle-overs squeeze, the late Ryan-Dylan repair, and Aron Cole’s closing spell as inferable match pivots. |
| L3 | Narrative-to-table validation | Readers must connect descriptive phrases to table evidence. | “The opening pair got Highridge out of the new-ball danger,” “Bayfront’s chase lost both tempo and structure after the run-outs,” and “Aron’s spell closed the door” map to partnerships, FOW, and bowling figures. |
