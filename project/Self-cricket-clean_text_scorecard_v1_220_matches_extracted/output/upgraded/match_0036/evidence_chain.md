| L1 | Direct extraction | Basic match facts are visible directly from the table and commentary. | Eastport won by 7 wickets; Noah Reed was Player of the Match; Westshore made 128 all out and Eastport reached 130/3. |

| L2 | Partnership and FOW consistency | Requires cumulative verification across partnerships and fall-of-wickets. | Westshore partnerships move through 3, 16, 21, 39, 82, 89, 102, 114, 126, and 128; Eastport partnerships move through 66, 91, 104, and 130. |
| L2 | Bowling reconciliation | Requires understanding that byes and leg-byes are not charged to bowlers. | Eastport bowlers concede 122 charged runs while Westshore total 128 because six runs are byes/leg-byes; Westshore bowlers concede 128 charged runs while Eastport total 130 because two runs are leg-byes. |
| L2 | Ball-count consistency | Requires checking legal balls against innings length. | Westshore batters face 206 legal balls across 34.2 overs; Eastport batters face 154 legal balls across 25.4 overs. |
| L3 | Commentary-hidden inference | Key facts are embedded through pressure and match rhythm rather than dense scoreboard listing. | The text presents Westshore’s early damage, Callum-and-Finn’s repair, Noah Reed’s middle-overs intervention, and Eastport’s controlled chase as inferable pivots. |
| L3 | Narrative-to-table validation | Readers must connect descriptive phrases to table evidence. | “The only real repair came through Callum and Finn,” “Noah cut out the batters Westshore needed,” and “the chase was broken open by Liam before Noah closed it” map to partnerships, wickets, and batting figures. |
