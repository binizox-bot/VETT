Ridgeview Cricket Ground, Hillview. Desert Lions against Gulf XI on a day when the weather would ultimately have the final say, but not before Mason Hart had produced a spell of bowling that deserved to be the centrepiece of any match report, result or no result. Gulf XI won the toss and chose to field, a decision shaped by the early moisture in the surface that promised assistance to the seamers, and for the first hour that decision looked inspired because the Desert Lions batting card unravelled in ways that suggested the surface was doing far more than anyone had anticipated.

Desert Lions were bundled out for 136 from 42.3 overs, a total that owed almost everything to Ethan Ward. His 37 from 51 deliveries — 6 fours, the lone innings of substance in a card that otherwise offered only fragments. Owen Hayes made 14 at the top, but fell at 33 for 1 to Noah Grant, the early breakthrough that set the tone for everything that followed. Liam Parker went for a duck at 34 for 2, Grant again the bowler, and the innings was already in trouble. Ethan Ward and Caleb Brooks then produced the one partnership of note — 35 together, Ward contributing 20 and Brooks adding 7 — that took the score from 34 for 2 to 69 for 3. But Caleb Shaw removed Ward at that score, and with the anchor gone, the innings began to list. Brooks followed for 13 at 78 for 4, Daniel Cross the bowler. Connor Blake made a fighting 18 and Ryan Cole chipped in with 7, their stand of 20 offering brief resistance at 100 for 5 before Mason Hart removed Cole. Hart was the destroyer: he had Dylan Shaw caught without scoring at 100 for 6, then returned to dismiss Connor Blake at 102 for 7. Zane Foster was trapped for a duck at 114 for 9, the tail offering only token resistance, though Evan Turner's unbeaten 18 at least pushed the total into three figures before the last wicket fell at 136 for 10.

Mason Hart finished with 5 for 20 — a devastating spell in conditions that rewarded his accuracy and movement. Noah Grant supported with 2 for 50, the two of them accounting for seven of the ten wickets between them. The extras column for Desert Lions read 18 — 10 wides, a single no-ball, 3 byes and 4 leg-byes — with the batting contributing 118 and the bowling charge at 129.

Gulf XI began their reply knowing that 137 was a modest target, and Ben Carter came out swinging. His 27 from just 20 deliveries — 3 fours and a six — was the kind of aggressive opening that makes a small target look even smaller, and when the rain arrived with Gulf XI at 45 without loss from only 8.4 overs, the match was abandoned as a No Result. The innings extras were a mere 3 — all wides — with the batting at 42 and the bowling charge at 45, barely a footnote in a contest that would ultimately be remembered not for its result but for Mason Hart's five-wicket haul on a surface that had briefly made batting look like a trial by seam movement.
