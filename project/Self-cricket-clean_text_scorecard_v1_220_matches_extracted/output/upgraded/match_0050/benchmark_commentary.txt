At Riverside Academy Ground, Lakeford, Harborline won the toss and chose to bat. The logic was sound enough — the surface looked like it would offer early assistance to the seamers, and putting runs on the board first felt like the right call. By the time the innings had reached 84 for 6, that decision was looking less like strategy and more like a gamble that was about to fail spectacularly.

Fraser Knight made the first incision. Owen Hart gone for 5, the board reading 12 for 1, and you could feel the first stirrings of anxiety in the Harborline dressing room. Henry Cole then removed Liam Brooks for 18, the score slipping to 39 for 2. Isaac Dunn cut out Ethan Cole for a scratchy 2, and at 45 for 3 the innings was in genuine trouble. Leo Nash trapped Logan Hayes for 12, the board now showing 69 for 4. Rhys Turner came and went for 7 — another Nash victim, the score slipping to 77 for 5 in the process — and when Jack Porter removed Noah Reed for 29 at 84 for 6, Harborline were staring at a total that would embarrass them.

Noah Reed had been the one batter in the top order who looked capable of reading the conditions. His 29 from 43 balls was built on patience rather than power, and his partnerships — 27 with Brooks, 22 with Hayes — were the only passages where the bowlers had to work rather than simply collect wickets. But at 84 for 6, with Reed gone, the innings needed something extraordinary.

Callum Shaw provided it. What followed was not a recovery in the conventional sense. It was a rebuttal. Shaw found Mason Pike, and together they constructed 87 for the seventh wicket — Shaw making 41, Pike contributing 39 — from 84 for 6 to 171 for 7. That stand would prove to be the largest of the innings. Pike had faced 47 balls for his 41, a supporting hand played with understated competence. When Porter returned to remove Pike at 171 for 7, the partnership had already changed the game.

Shaw was not finished. Dylan Cross walked in and together they added 46 — Shaw blasting 31, Cross providing a careful 11 from 24 deliveries — as the score climbed from 171 for 7 to 217 for 8. And then Harvey Blake, in a cameo as valuable as it was unexpected, joined Shaw for 53 for the ninth wicket — Shaw contributing 29, Blake making 22 — pushing Harborline past 250, the score surging from 217 for 8 to 270 for 9. Shaw was the last man out, caught on the boundary attempting one more big shot, his innings ending at 103 from 93 deliveries with 11 fours and 3 sixes. The full ten wickets had fallen, the innings closing at 276 from 48.2 overs.

The extras column rewards attention. The innings total of 276 had been assembled from 252 runs off the bat combined with 24 in sundries. The bowlers' own accounts were charged with 19 wides and a couple of no-balls. A single bye and a couple of leg-byes, meanwhile, had arrived through routes that sit outside any individual bowler's analysis, leaving the attack collectively answerable for 273 of the 276 conceded. Fraser Knight with 3 for 52 and Leo Nash with 2 for 45 were the main wicket-takers, and the rest of the attack shared the remaining five wickets among them.

Chasing 277 to win, Reef Bay sent out Aaron Holt and Ben Carter. Harvey Blake removed Carter for 5 at 13 for 1, and the chase was under immediate pressure. Mason Pike then accounted for Holt — gone for 18 at 33 for 2, the partnership between Holt and Price having added 20 — and the early losses meant the required rate was already beginning to feel heavy. Callum Price and Daniel Fox then produced the one partnership of substance for Reef Bay, adding 39 together from 33 for 2 to 72 for 3. Price made 16, Fox contributed 18, and for a brief passage the chase had a pulse.

But Callum Shaw, fresh from his century, produced the delivery of the afternoon to remove Price for 21 at 72 for 3. Ethan Cole then took out Elliot Marsh for 2, the score slipping to 78 for 4. Harvey Blake took over — Daniel Fox, whose 25 from 59 deliveries with 2 fours and a six had been the innings of lone resistance, fell at 89 for 5. Fraser Knight followed for 7 at 91 for 6, George Miles for 1 at 97 for 7, Henry Cole for 8 at 103 for 8, Jack Porter for 3 at 106 for 9. Blake finished with 5 for 31 from his spell, and Reef Bay were all out for 113 — all ten wickets gone — from 36.5 overs. Shaw supported with 2 for 16.

For the chase, the extras told a similar story of occasional indiscipline. The total of 113 had been built from 97 runs off the bat plus 16 in sundries — 10 of those wides and a single no-ball coming directly from the bowlers, while 3 byes and a couple of leg-byes arrived through those same routes that sit outside individual analysis. The bowling charge stood at 108.

Harborline Won by 163 Runs. Callum Shaw was named Player of the Match — 103 from 93 with the bat, 2 for 16 with the ball, a performance that had first rescued an innings from the depths of 84 for 6 and then, for good measure, taken two wickets in a chase that had briefly flickered before it was extinguished.
