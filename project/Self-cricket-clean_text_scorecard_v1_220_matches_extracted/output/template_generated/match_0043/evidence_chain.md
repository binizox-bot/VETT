| L1 | Direct extraction | Basic facts remain readable directly. | Eastshore won by 170 runs; Dylan Archer was Player of the Match; Eastshore made 243/9 and Granite XI made 73 all out. |
| L1 | Key player identification | Main contributors can be extracted from tables and text. | Dylan Archer made 64, Evan Marsh made 44, Gavin Price was 25 not out and took two wickets, Hudson Lane took three wickets. |
| L2 | Cricket-rule arithmetic | Requires checking runs, extras, totals, and margin. | Eastshore batting runs add to 236 and extras add 7, giving 243; Granite XI batting runs add to 64 and extras add 9, giving 73; 243 minus 73 gives a 170-run margin. |
| L2 | Partnership/FOW consistency | Requires cumulative checking from partnership table to fall-of-wickets. | Eastshore partnerships progress through 52, 68, 84, 187, 190, 199, 212, 238, and 243; Granite XI partnerships progress through 4, 16, 28, 30, 33, 54, 57, 57, 67, and 73. |
| L2 | Bowling reconciliation | Requires understanding that byes and leg-byes are not charged to bowlers. | Granite XI bowlers concede 240 charged runs while Eastshore total 243 because three runs are byes/leg-byes; Eastshore bowlers concede 72 charged runs while Granite XI total 73 because one run is a leg-bye. |
| L2 | Overs and legal balls | Requires validating innings length against batting balls and bowling overs. | Eastshore batters face 300 legal balls across 50.0 overs; Granite XI batters face 146 legal balls across 24.2 overs. |
| L3 | Commentary-hidden inference | Key facts are embedded as match flow rather than mechanical score strings. | “Three down before the innings had weight,” “the long Dylan-and-Evan repair,” and “Granite never built a second proper phase” map to scorecard evidence without dense numeric listing. |
| L3 | Narrative-to-table validation | Requires connecting descriptive pressure points to specific table fields. | Dylan Archer’s central innings, Hudson Lane’s lower-order burst, and Gavin Price’s first-and-last wicket pattern are all recoverable through batting, bowling, partnership, and FOW tables. |
