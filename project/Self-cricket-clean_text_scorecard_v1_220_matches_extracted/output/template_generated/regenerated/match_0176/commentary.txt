Let's remain at the Westhaven Youth Cricket Ground in Meridian, a venue that was seeing more cricket than most this season, for yet another contest between Ridgefield and Coastline, and this one, fair to say, followed the now familiar pattern of Coastline dominance, the chasing side getting home by 8 wickets with a staggering thirty-two and a half overs still in hand, a margin that borders on the embarrassing for Ridgefield because when a side chases down your total losing only two wickets and using barely a third of their allotted overs, you know that you have been comprehensively, utterly, and totally outplayed in every department of the game. The thing about Ridgefield winning the toss and electing to bat for a third time in this series of matches was that you had to question the logic, you had to wonder whether the captain was watching the same pitch as everyone else or whether he was simply being stubborn, because the evidence was mounting that the Westhaven surface, with its green tinge and its propensity to offer movement to the seamers, was a bowler-friendly deck where batting first was an invitation to disaster, and that invitation was accepted with grim inevitability as Ridgefield were skittled out for a mere 84 in 34.2 overs, a total so low that it barely qualified as a target, more of a formality that Coastline had to complete before they could go home. Extras contributed 9 to the Ridgefield total with one bye, one leg bye, no no balls, and seven wides, the Coastline bowlers once again demonstrating the kind of control and accuracy that had become their hallmark, not giving away cheap runs, not letting the batters off the hook, and the result was a scorecard that made for the grimmest of reading from a Ridgefield perspective, batters coming and going with alarming regularity, nobody able to get past thirty, nobody able to build the kind of partnership that turns a struggling innings into something remotely competitive. Owen Pike was the one Ridgefield batter who showed any kind of sustained resistance, his 30 from 73 balls an innings of patience and grit if not of flowing strokeplay, the kind of knock where the batter has to work incredibly hard just to survive, never mind score, and while his determination was commendable, the reality was that his 30 from 73 deliveries was never going to be enough to build a defendable total around, not when wickets were falling like autumn leaves at the other end. Coastline, chasing 85 to win from 50 overs, could have approached the task in any number of ways, they could have been cavalier and tried to knock the runs off in five overs, they could have been ultra-cautious and taken forty overs over it, but they chose the sensible middle path, batting with controlled aggression and knocking off the runs for the loss of just 2 wickets in a mere 17.4 overs, winning by 8 wickets, extras contributing just 4 to their total with no byes, no leg byes, no no balls, and four wides, the Ridgefield bowlers at least keeping things relatively tight in the extras column but lacking the penetration to take the wickets that might have given Coastline even a moment's pause. Tristan Hayes anchored the Coastline chase with a composed 38 from 47 deliveries, four boundaries struck along the way, the kind of innings where the batter never looks in trouble, never looks like getting out, just works the ball around, picks off the bad deliveries, and guides his side home with the minimum of fuss, and Ronan Cross was named Player of the Match for his earlier contribution with the ball, his three wickets the spearhead of a bowling performance that had set up the Coastline victory long before the batters had even started thinking about their chase. The Westhaven Youth Cricket Ground had, by this stage of the season, become something of a fortress for Coastline and something of a house of horrors for Ridgefield, the pitch continuing to offer assistance to the bowlers but not so much that a total of 84 all out in 34.2 overs could be excused, because the simple truth was that Coastline's bowlers exploited the conditions better, executed their skills more precisely, and asked more difficult questions of the batters than their Ridgefield counterparts, and that is why Coastline kept winning and Ridgefield kept losing, not because of luck or because of the toss, but because one side was simply better at the fundamentals of cricket, better at hitting the right lengths, better at building pressure, better at seizing the crucial moments and turning them to their advantage, and the 8-wicket margin was a fair reflection of that superiority, a result that left Ridgefield with a great deal of soul-searching to do and Coastline with the quiet satisfaction of a job thoroughly, professionally, and completely well done.
