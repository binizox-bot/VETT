Now then, let's head over to the Pinewater Oval in Eastmere for what was, without any shadow of doubt, the most extraordinarily one-sided and breathtakingly brief contest in the entire history of this competition, Seabrook absolutely demolishing Dunecrest by the crushing margin of 10 wickets in a match that lasted barely nineteen overs of actual playing time, the kind of result that makes you wonder whether Dunecrest had accidentally wandered onto the wrong field, or whether they had sent a team of complete novices who had been taught the rules of cricket approximately five minutes before the start of play, because their batting performance was not just bad, it was historically, monumentally, almost incomprehensibly bad, a total of 24 all out in 17.4 overs that was not so much a cricket score as it was a player's shirt number. The thing about Dunecrest winning the toss and electing to bat on a Pinewater surface that, to be completely fair to the Dunecrest batters, was doing a bit, a pitch that had a healthy covering of grass and was offering the kind of seam movement that makes batters wake up in a cold sweat the night before a match, was a decision that required the kind of courage that borders on recklessness, and that recklessness was punished in the most brutal, most unforgiving, and most comprehensive fashion imaginable, because the Seabrook bowlers, led by the utterly devastating Marcus Dale, produced a spell of bowling so incisive, so accurate, so relentlessly hostile, that the Dunecrest batters were reduced to the status of mere extras in a one-man show, the innings lasting a mere 17.4 overs and producing a total of 24 that was so low, so minuscule, so utterly beyond the pale of normal cricketing performance, that it almost defied belief. Extras contributed 9 to the Dunecrest total with no byes, one leg bye, no no balls, and eight wides, and when you consider that the batters contributed just 15 runs from the bat and extras added 9, you realise that almost forty percent of the Dunecrest total came from sundries, which is a statistic so damning, so humiliating, so utterly beyond any acceptable standard of cricketing competence, that it almost defies commentary, because if you take away the eight wides, the Dunecrest total would have been 16, and that is not a cricket score, that is the number of players in a rugby union team, and the fact that a cricket team could be bowled out for 24, with almost half those runs coming from extras, tells you everything you need to know about the gulf in quality between these two sides on this particular day. Marcus Dale was the undisputed star of the Seabrook bowling effort, taking a magnificent 5 wickets in a spell of such devastating accuracy and such unplayable movement that he was deservedly named Player of the Match, his figures the centrepiece of a bowling performance that was as clinical, as ruthless, and as utterly dominant as any we have seen in this competition or indeed in any competition at any level of cricket, the ball doing everything he wanted it to do, seaming both ways off the pitch, swinging late through the air, moving enough to find the edge but not enough to miss it, the complete fast-bowling masterclass that left the Dunecrest batters looking like they had never faced a cricket ball in their lives. Seabrook, set the absurdly small target of 25 to win, knocked off the runs without losing a wicket in just 1.5 overs, which is eleven deliveries, which is barely enough time to take guard and have a look at the bowler, and extras contributed 3 to the Seabrook total with no byes, no leg byes, no no balls, and three wides, the Dunecrest bowlers unable even to land the ball on the cut strip during their brief, painful, and utterly humiliating spell with the ball, and the entire Seabrook chase lasted eleven deliveries, which is about the time it takes to walk from the pavilion to the middle and back again, the kind of statistic that you simply cannot make up and would not believe if you had not seen it with your own eyes. The Pinewater Oval at Eastmere had witnessed something that bordered on the surreal, a cricket match that lasted barely nineteen overs in total, a Dunecrest total of 24 all out, a Seabrook chase of 25 for no loss in 1.5 overs, the kind of contest that those who were present would dine out on for years, the day Dunecrest were bowled out for 24 and Seabrook knocked off the runs in eleven deliveries, a match so brief, so one-sided, and so utterly extraordinary that it would forever hold a special place in the annals of cricketing folklore, the day that Marcus Dale took five wickets and Seabrook won by 10 wickets in less time than it takes to boil an egg, a result that would be remembered, recounted, and relived for as long as cricket is played at the Pinewater Oval in Eastmere.
