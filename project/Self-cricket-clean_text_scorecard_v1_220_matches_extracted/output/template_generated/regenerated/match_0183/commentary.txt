Let's make our way to the Meadowbank School Oval in Westhaven for what turned out to be, fair to say, another complete and utter demolition job, Seabrook absolutely crushing Northvale by the staggering margin of 205 runs in a contest that was about as one-sided as any you could ever wish to see, the kind of performance where one side plays at the absolute peak of their powers and the other side, well, the other side just desperately wants the ground to open up and swallow them whole, because when you are on the receiving end of a 205-run defeat in a fifty-over match, there is nowhere to hide and nothing positive to take from the experience except the determination never to let it happen again. The thing about Northvale winning the toss and electing to field was a decision that, on a Meadowbank School Oval surface that was a batting paradise, flat and true and absolutely full of runs, looked strange at the time and looked utterly inexplicable by the end of the Seabrook innings, because the Seabrook batters feasted on the conditions, filled their boots, helped themselves to a banquet of runs, and posted a monstrous 306 for 7 from their full 50 overs, a total that was essentially match-winning on its own regardless of what happened in the second half, the kind of score that tells the opposition that the contest is effectively over and the only thing left to determine is the margin of victory. Extras contributed a significant 28 to the Seabrook total with one bye, two leg byes, three no balls, and an extraordinary twenty-two wides, that wides count of twenty-two being, well, more than four full overs of free deliveries, the kind of indiscipline that makes bowling coaches tear their hair out and captains wonder whether their bowlers have forgotten the basic mechanics of delivering a cricket ball, and twenty-two wides when the batting side is already in rampant form is the equivalent of pouring petrol on a bonfire, it just makes everything worse, makes the target bigger, makes the bowlers more frustrated, makes the fielders more demoralised, and makes the scoreboard tick over even when the batters are not hitting the ball. The Seabrook innings was built around a magnificent century from Oliver Kent, who was deservedly named Player of the Match, his 108 from 114 deliveries an innings of genuine class and authority, ten boundaries and two sixes laced through a knock that was the absolute centrepiece of the Seabrook batting effort, the kind of innings where the batter seems to be playing a different game to everyone else, seems to have so much time, seems to know exactly what is coming and exactly where to put it, and his century, constructed with such assurance and such effortless accumulation, was the difference between a good Seabrook total and the commanding one that batted Northvale completely and utterly out of the contest before they had even had the chance to strap on their pads. Northvale, set an impossible 307 to win, produced a batting performance that can only be described as abject, crumbling to a pathetic 101 all out in 38.2 overs, a chase that was over long before it had properly begun, the Northvale batters overwhelmed by the scale of the task, unable to cope with the scoreboard pressure, unable to cope with the bowling, unable to cope with anything that Seabrook threw at them, and extras contributed an eye-watering 32 to the Northvale total with three byes, four leg byes, two no balls, and an extraordinary twenty-three wides, the Seabrook bowlers perhaps losing their discipline a touch given the comfort of the situation, but twenty-three wides is still a number that would have been discussed at some length in the post-match debrief despite the enormous margin of victory, because those are free runs that could have been avoided with better execution. The Meadowbank School Oval was a picture, the pitch true and even, the outfield fast, the conditions perfect for cricket, and yet the contest was a complete mismatch, Seabrook demonstrating a gulf in quality that was almost embarrassing, the kind of performance that makes you wonder whether the two sides should even be in the same competition, because when a team posts 306 and then bowls the opposition out for 101, winning by 205 runs, you are not just talking about a victory, you are talking about a statement, a declaration of intent, a warning to every other side in the league that Seabrook are the team to beat and that anyone who comes up against them needs to be at their very, very best just to be competitive, because anything less than that and you will be blown away, dismissed, obliterated, just as Northvale were on this unforgettable and, for them, deeply forgettable day at the Meadowbank School Oval in Westhaven.
