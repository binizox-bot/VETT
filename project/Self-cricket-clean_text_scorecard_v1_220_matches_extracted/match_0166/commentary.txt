At Cedarbank Cricket Oval in Westmoor, Bayview Women chose to field first and for almost the whole Highland Vale innings they had the game moving in the direction they wanted, not through one huge dramatic collapse at the start, but through a long squeeze that kept the batting side from ever turning occupation into real control. Clara Moss went early, caught by Tara Quinn off Ella Ray, and after that Grace Allen and Nora Vale had to do the slow repair work, but even their partnership felt more like resistance than command because the scoring was being carried as much by extras as by clean striking. Nora spent a long time at the crease for 8, Grace Allen made the more meaningful hand with 24 from 39, and together they dragged Highland Vale from the early loss to 38 for 2, yet the innings never really opened up because Zara Pike and Ruby Shaw kept the middle overs tight and Bayview’s fielders kept making Highland Vale restart. Ava Mercer’s spell became the defining bowling work of the innings. She first removed Nora Vale to end the only meaningful top-order repair, then later returned to cut through the back half, bowling Paige Ford, having Lena Grant stumped by Mia Hart, and taking a caught-and-bowled from Eva Dale. Grace Allen’s run-out at 55 for 3 was another key break because it removed the only batter who had looked capable of carrying the innings deeper, and once Ivy Stone and Paige Ford were both gone, Highland Vale had slipped into a position where the final overs became about scraping rather than launching. Maya Wells was run out before she could settle, Paige went at 66, and the only reason the score moved as far as it did was Hannah Rowe’s late punch. Her unbeaten 20 from 10 balls, with four boundaries, gave Highland Vale the one genuinely bright burst of the innings, and the small late stands with Lena Grant, Eva Dale and Skye Ward lifted the total to 91 for 9 rather than something much smaller. Still, that score felt badly limited because 15 of the runs were extras, and Bayview’s bowling figures told the larger story clearly: Ava Mercer with 4 for 14, Ella Ray striking early, Sophie Lane breaking another middle passage, and the quiet overs from Zara Pike and Ruby Shaw making sure Highland Vale never had a clean release phase. Bayview’s chase then began so sharply that the eventual no result felt especially frustrating. Tara Quinn looked comfortable almost immediately, moving to 18 not out from 14 with three boundaries, while Mia Hart gave the other end a brisker edge with 10 not out from 6 and one clean hit over the rope. Grace Allen’s first two overs disappeared for 24, and even though Eva Dale slowed things down a touch, Bayview had already reached 32 without loss in just 3.2 overs, which meant the target was shrinking quickly and Highland Vale had not found even one wicket to make the chase feel tense. Then the match stopped. The official line remains No Result, and that matters because the scorecard cannot be treated as a completed chase, but the cricket that was played had a clear shape: Highland Vale had battled to 91 for 9 through one slow anchor, a heavy extras column, and Hannah Rowe’s late cameo, while Bayview had started the reply with far more clarity and tempo than Highland Vale had shown in their full innings. It was one of those incomplete games where the result column stays neutral, yet the live momentum had already started to lean strongly toward the chasing side before the interruption cut it off.
